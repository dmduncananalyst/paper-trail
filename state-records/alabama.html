Alabama Bookkeeping

Sales, use, and rental activity can create different sets of records, especially when a business operates across local tax jurisdictions. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Alabama.

Keeping records that support the books

Alabama tax law in Title 40 of the Code of Alabama imposes recordkeeping duties tied to the tax being reported. For sales, use, and rental tax, a multistate retailer, seller, vendor, lessor, or person doing business in Alabama must keep complete and adequate records needed for the Alabama Department of Revenue to determine the tax owed. The rule text lists records including gross receipts from sales or rental payments in Alabama, whether or not the seller regards the receipts as taxable. Source: Alabama Department of Revenue, Sales and Use Tax Rulebook, Rule 810-27-1-.7.01 summary in search results, at https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/ultraviewer/viewer/basic_viewer/index.html?form=2022/08/Sales_Use_Rulebook.pdf

Taxpayers must keep copies of filed returns as part of their records, and keep property records for as long as needed to figure basis in property. That wording appears in Alabama individual income tax return instructions published by the Department of Revenue. Source: https://revenue.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/25f40bk.pdf

How state taxes affect the ledger

Alabama has a state sales tax, a use tax, a rental tax on tangible personal property in the rule cited above, an individual income tax, a corporate income tax, and employer withholding of Alabama income tax. Each tax has its own return, so bookkeeping must separate gross receipts, taxable and nontaxable sales, purchases subject to use tax, and wages subject to withholding. Sources: Alabama Department of Revenue rulebook above, and the Alabama income tax booklet above, which discusses individual income tax, withholding returns, sales tax returns, and Form W-2 filing.

Payroll records and employer reporting

All Alabama employers must report each newly hired or recalled employee to the Alabama Department of Labor within seven days from the date of hire or reemployment. Employers reporting electronically may transmit twice monthly, with transmissions not less than 12 days and not more than 16 days apart. Employers with five or more employees must report through the Internet system. Required data includes the employee name, address, and Social Security number, the first day of work, whether the person was newly hired or recalled, and the employer Federal Employer Identification Number, name, and address. Failure to report may be fined up to 25 dollars for each violation, as stated on the agency page. Source: Alabama Department of Labor, Alabama New Hire, at https://adol.alabama.gov/employers/alabama-new-hire/

When an industry needs additional records

Businesses in regulated Alabama industries have extra record duties under their licensing regimes, including motor vehicle dealers and licensing offices for vehicle documents, financial institutions, and businesses subject to Alabama alcohol, tobacco, and motor fuel taxes. The vehicle document retention memo found in search results was addressed to licensing officials, not to general businesses, so it is not generalized here. Source: Alabama Department of Revenue memo at http://revenue.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/Memo_2016-23_Record_Retention_Requirements-1.pdf

Employers in construction and other industries that employ workers covered by Alabama unemployment and workers compensation laws must keep the payroll and injury related records those programs require, in addition to tax records.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A small Alabama retailer makes a sale, collects sales tax, and later buys equipment from an out of state seller who charged no tax. Its books need a sales journal showing gross receipts for the first transaction and a purchase record showing the equipment cost for the second, so the sales return and any use tax return can both be prepared from the same records.

A cafe hires a cook on a Monday. The owner reports the new hire to the Department of Labor within seven days, sets up a payroll record with the start date and Social Security number, and files the quarterly unemployment wage report and withholding return from that same payroll register, so the three reports agree.

Rental transactions and the split between state and local sales taxes

A business that rents equipment as well as selling it cannot assume the same receipt description belongs in both revenue categories. The monthly ledger should identify rental receipts separately, maintain the underlying agreements, and keep sales activity linked to the jurisdiction where it occurred.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Alabama is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for Alabama businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
