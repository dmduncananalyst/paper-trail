Florida Bookkeeping

County surtaxes, reemployment tax, and industry taxes create different records even though Florida has no individual income tax. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Florida.

Keeping records that support the books

Florida law requires persons subject to Florida taxes under chapter 212, Florida Statutes, to keep and preserve records prescribed by the Florida Department of Revenue, and those records must be open to examination by the Department or its authorized agents at reasonable hours. That duty is described in Department audit materials citing the state tax section12.12, subsection 6, Florida Statutes. Source: Florida Department of Revenue order materials at https://floridarevenue.com/TaxLaw/Documents/Mitchell%27s%20Grocery%20%26%20Seafood;%20DOR%2004-3-FOF_Redacted.pdf

Keep sales records by county, because Florida discretionary sales surtax varies by county and is reported by county on the sales tax return.

How state taxes affect the ledger

Florida has a sales tax and a use tax, with county discretionary sales surtax added in many counties, a corporate income tax, a communications services tax, a documentary stamp tax on specified documents and loans, and a reemployment tax on employers, which is Florida name for unemployment tax. Source for the range of taxes filed through Department eServices: Florida Department of Revenue eServices list at https://floridarevenue.com/rules/Pages/12D-51.001.aspx , which lists communications services tax, documentary stamp tax, gross receipts tax on utility services, insurance premium tax, reemployment tax, and sales and use tax among Department filing services.

Florida has a gross receipts tax on utility services only, not a general gross receipts tax on all businesses. It is listed as a Department tax in the eServices source above.

Payroll records and employer reporting

Reemployment tax employers must register with the Department, file quarterly reports, Form RT-6, pay reemployment tax, and correct reports with Form RT-8A where needed. The Department can require reports and corrected reports going back five years, as stated in the Employer Guide cited above. That five year lookback is a practical reason to keep reemployment wage records at least five years.

There is no Florida wage withholding return, for the reason stated in the state tax section. Employers still file federal withholding and must keep federal payroll records.

When an industry needs additional records

Florida tourist and hospitality businesses have extra tax records. Transient rentals are subject to state transient rental tax collected as part of sales tax, plus county tourist development tax and related local taxes in many counties, administered in some counties by the county rather than the Department. Lodging operators keep occupancy, rent, and exemption records by property and by county.

Florida businesses selling alcohol or tobacco as wholesalers or distributors have Department reporting listed in the eServices source in the state tax section, for alcoholic beverages and tobacco products wholesaler and distributor reporting.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Florida vacation rental operator rents the same property to guests for stays of a few nights. It records rent by county and by stay, separates exempt longer stays where an exemption applies, and keeps lease and exemption records, because state tax and county tourist taxes are computed from those records and may be filed with different administrators.

A Florida retailer with no taxable sales in a month still files a sales tax return for that period, because Florida law requires a return even when no sales and use tax is due, as stated in Department filing guidance summarized in search results for Form GT-800069 materials in part 7. It also keeps the confirmation number from its electronic filing in its records, as that guidance instructs.

County surtaxes and transient rental records

A short stay property business should distinguish rent, cleaning charges, refunds, occupancy dates, and the county in which the property is located. Those details affect how a qualified preparer evaluates applicable sales and tourist taxes, while the bookkeeper preserves the records behind each reported total.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Florida is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for Florida businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
