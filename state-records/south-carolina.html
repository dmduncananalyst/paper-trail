South Carolina Bookkeeping

South Carolina tax records need to support both tax filings and, where relevant, licensed business activities. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in South Carolina.

Keeping records that support the books

A person who applies for or holds a licence administered by the South Carolina Department of Revenue, who is liable for any tax, fee, or surcharge administered by the Department, or who is required to file any return or statement with the Department must keep books, papers, memoranda, and records sufficient to establish the right to obtain or hold the licence, any amount required to be shown on any return or statement, or any tax, fee, or surcharge due. For this purpose, a return includes information returns and reports. This is South Carolina Regulation 117 200.1, summarised here in plain words. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/south-carolina/R-117-200.1

Those books and records must be kept available at all times for inspection by agents or auditors of the Department. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/south-carolina/R-117-200.1

How state taxes affect the ledger

South Carolina has a state sales and use tax, a personal income tax, a corporate income tax and corporate licence fee, and employer withholding. The state sales tax rate is 6 percent, and counties may add local sales and use taxes, including capital project, transportation, education, and local option taxes, so records must show the county of the sale or delivery.

South Carolina also imposes an admissions tax, a liquor by the drink tax for establishments that sell liquor by the drink, and other category taxes listed in the Department tax portal, including alcohol beverage licensing, bingo tax, and motor fuel taxes for the businesses in those categories. Source: http://dor.sc.gov/mydorway/

Businesses with sales in several counties keep a local tax worksheet for each filing period that lists gross sales and tax by county, because the state return requires local tax amounts to be reported by county.

Payroll records and employer reporting

Employers withhold South Carolina income tax and file withholding returns and annual wage statements with the Department of Revenue, and they file quarterly unemployment tax and wage reports with the South Carolina Department of Employment and Workforce.

Income, purchases, and documentation

Regulation 117 200.1 lists the kinds of supporting material covered when records are reproduced, including documents of original entry, purchase orders, invoices, checks, vouchers, and payroll records, which shows that purchase and payment documents are part of the South Carolina tax record. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/south-carolina/R-117-200.1

How long to retain records

Books and records under Regulation 117 200.1 must be retained for at least 4 years after the return was filed or due to be filed, whichever is later. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/south-carolina/R-117-200.1

Microfilm reproductions of supporting records, such as documents of original entry, purchase orders, invoices, checks, vouchers, and payroll records, may be kept in place of the actual documents only with prior written approval of the Department, and only where the taxpayer keeps the copies as long as their contents may become material in the administration of a law by the Department, provides facilities for preservation and ready inspection including a projector for viewing, and is ready to make transcripts of the information when required. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/south-carolina/R-117-200.1

When an industry needs additional records

Hospitality businesses in South Carolina have layered duties. Restaurants and bars that sell liquor by the drink collect the liquor by the drink tax, lodging providers collect state and local accommodations taxes, and places of amusement collect the admissions tax. Each of these requires revenue records split by category. The Department lists admissions tax, liquor by the drink tax, and alcohol beverage licensing among the tax types in its portal. Source: http://dor.sc.gov/mydorway/

Manufacturing and industrial businesses report business personal property and, for manufacturers, manufacturing property tax matters, which require fixed asset and depreciation records beyond ordinary income tax records. The Department lists business personal property tax and manufacturing property tax among its tax types. Source: http://dor.sc.gov/mydorway/

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Charleston retailer files a sales and use tax return for a period and keeps the books, local tax worksheet by county, purchase orders, invoices, and checks for that period together. The retailer sets the destruction date at least 4 years after the return was filed or due, whichever is later, and keeps the file if the Department opens an audit before that date.

A Columbia restaurant that sells liquor by the drink keeps drink sales, food sales, and other sales in separate register totals. At month end the bookkeeper reconciles the liquor by the drink tax amount and the sales tax amount to two different totals in the same sales journal, and an owner who starts a new server reports that hire within 20 days of the first day of work.

Accommodations, admissions, and beverage taxes

South Carolina hospitality businesses may have several tax categories that cannot be explained by a single gross revenue number. Lodging stays, admission receipts, and liquor sales require distinct classification and supporting source documents where those taxes apply.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in South Carolina is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for South Carolina businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
