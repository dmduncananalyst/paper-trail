Georgia Bookkeeping

Sales by county, quarterly wage records, and corporate reporting can require several views of the same transactions. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Georgia.

Keeping records that support the books

Georgia employers must keep wage records and file quarterly tax and wage reports for unemployment insurance with the Georgia Department of Labor, called GDOL, and must keep records that support those reports. Source: GDOL employer newsletter at https://dol.georgia.gov/document/unemployment-benefits/employer-newsletter-dol-5137/download , which describes quarterly tax and wage filing, the Employer Portal, and new hire reporting.

How state taxes affect the ledger

Georgia has a state sales tax and a use tax, with local sales taxes for counties and other purposes added in most counties, a state individual income tax, a corporate income tax, and employer withholding of Georgia income tax. DOR administers these taxes, and sales tax is filed through the Georgia Tax Center.

Georgia has a net worth tax on corporations in addition to corporate income tax, so corporations keep net worth information. Georgia also has a title ad valorem tax on motor vehicles instead of annual ad valorem tax and sales tax on most vehicle purchases, which affects dealer bookkeeping. Dealer penalties and title tax rules were summarized in DOR legislative materials found in search results, at https://dor.georgia.gov/document/legislative-summary/2013-legislation-summary/download

Sales records need county level detail, because the combined sales tax rate and the local tax distribution depend on the county where the sale is delivered or sourced under Georgia rules.

Payroll records and employer reporting

Georgia employers must report all new hires and rehires to the Georgia Department of Human Services, through the Georgia new hire reporting site, within 10 days after employees are hired, rehired, or return to work. GDOL states that it is critical that employers report both new hires and quarterly wages using the same Federal Employer Identification Number, to help prevent unemployment fraud. Source: GDOL employer newsletter at https://dol.georgia.gov/document/unemployment-benefits/employer-newsletter-dol-5137/download

The Georgia new hire rule rests on Georgia Statute 19-11-9.2 and on the federal Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act of 1996. The Georgia new hire brochure in search results states that employers must report paid individuals regardless of whether the individuals are considered employees under the federal tax code, which is broader than an employees only rule. Source: https://ganh-uat.stellarware.com/files/GANewhireBrochure.pdf (program brochure hosted by the program contractor)

When an industry needs additional records

Georgia nonprofits that solicit charitable contributions may need registration and financial records with the Georgia Secretary of State, Securities and Charitable Organizations Division, depending on exemptions and size.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Georgia shop hires a clerk on the first of the month. Because Georgia deadline is 10 days, not 20, the owner reports the hire through the Georgia new hire site by the tenth day, using the same Federal Employer Identification Number it uses for quarterly wage reports, exactly the matching point GDOL makes in the payroll section.

A Georgia seller delivers goods into three counties in one month. Its sales journal records the delivery county for each invoice and separates gross sales, exempt sales supported by exemption certificates, and taxable sales, so the Georgia Tax Center sales tax return and the local tax distribution can be prepared from the journal without rebuilding the month.

County sales sourcing and consistent employer identifiers

Georgia sales reports are more useful when the delivery county is preserved with each transaction. Employers also gain an audit trail by using consistent identifying details across hiring reports and quarterly wage submissions, rather than piecing those reports together after the fact.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Georgia is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for Georgia businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
