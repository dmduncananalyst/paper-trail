Kentucky Bookkeeping

Kentucky sales tax records and limited liability entity tax considerations require separating gross receipts and purchases. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Kentucky.

Keeping records that support the books

Kentucky Revised Statutes the recordkeeping section39.720 requires every seller, every retailer, and every person storing, using, or otherwise consuming tangible personal property, digital property, or taxable services in Kentucky to keep such records, receipts, invoices, and other pertinent papers in such form as the Department of Revenue may require. Source: Kentucky Legislature, https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/Law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=49181

For an exempt sale, the retailer must maintain a record of the appropriate exemption certificate, other required documentation, or applicable data elements in order to take a deduction from gross receipts on the sales and use tax return. The burden of proof is on the seller to substantiate why a customer was not charged tax. Source: Kentucky Department of Revenue, Sales Tax Facts, https://revenue.ky.gov/DOR%20Training%20Materials/Sales%20Tax%20Facts%202011%20-%20Jun.pdf

How state taxes affect the ledger

Kentucky has a sales tax and a use tax at a state rate of 6 percent. Kentucky does not impose general local sales taxes statewide, although it does impose specific local taxes such as transient room taxes, restaurant taxes in some cities, and other local levies authorized for specific purposes. Bookkeeping for lodging and restaurants must therefore check for a local tax in that city or county in addition to the state 6 percent.

Kentucky also has individual and corporate income taxes, a limited liability entity tax, employer withholding, and excise and provider taxes including a healthcare provider tax for certain providers, coal severance tax, and motor fuel taxes. There is no statewide gross receipts tax, although the limited liability entity tax is computed with reference to gross receipts or gross profits, which makes gross receipts records important for entities subject to that tax.

Payroll records and employer reporting

New hire reporting. Kentucky employers report new hires and rehires within 20 days of hire through the Kentucky New Hire Reporting Center. The 20 day period is stated in the reporting center instructions summarized in secondary reporting of the form, and is consistent with the federal framework.

Unemployment insurance. Employers register with the Kentucky Office of Unemployment Insurance, file quarterly wage and contribution reports, and pay contributions. Employers also register for withholding with the Department of Revenue. Payroll records must support wages by employee by quarter, withholding, and amounts paid.

When an industry needs additional records

Healthcare provider tax. Hospitals, nursing facilities, and certain other providers subject to the provider tax must keep census, revenue, and service records that support the provider tax return, limited to taxed providers.

Admissions and hospitality. Charges for general admission are taxable in Kentucky, and transient room tax records must be kept by lodging operators for the local taxing district as well as for state sales tax on the room charge where applicable.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Kentucky retailer sells equipment to a manufacturer that claims an exemption. It keeps the exemption certificate and the invoice for at least four years from when the record was made, and does not deduct the sale from gross receipts on its return without that file.

A Louisville restaurant keeps daily sales reports that separate dine in sales, any local restaurant tax where applicable, and state sales tax, so its monthly sales tax return and its gross receipts records for entity tax agree.

Exemption substantiation and entity tax records

A manufacturer exemption can affect whether a seller charges sales tax, but the invoice and exemption documentation must still be linked. Separately, businesses subject to Kentucky limited liability entity tax need income and gross receipts detail beyond a sales tax report.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Kentucky is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for Kentucky businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
