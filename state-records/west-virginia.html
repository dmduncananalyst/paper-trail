West Virginia Bookkeeping

West Virginia sales tax rules require records of taxable charges and tax collected, with separate treatment for certain industries. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in West Virginia.

Keeping records that support the books

Each taxpayer under the consumers sales and service tax must keep complete and accurate records of taxable sales and charges, together with a record of tax collected, and must keep all invoices, bills of lading, and other pertinent documents in the form the Tax Commissioner may require by regulation. Source, West Virginia Code the recordkeeping section1.15.23, official code site: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/11-15-23/

For taxes under the Tax Procedure and Administration Act, taxpayers must keep records, receipts, invoices, and other pertinent papers in the form the Tax Commissioner requires, as in West Virginia Code the recordkeeping section1.12B.14 for the mineral severance related article summarized in search results, which states a general record duty for taxpayers liable under that article. Source: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/11-12B-14/

How state taxes affect the ledger

West Virginia imposes a personal income tax, a corporation net income tax, and a consumers sales and service tax and a corresponding use tax. The consumers sales tax rate in the code materials is stated as six percent in the direct pay permit and related sections summarized in search results, with local and municipal taxes possible in some jurisdictions. No general gross receipts business and occupation tax for ordinary businesses is stated here, because the historic municipal business and occupation taxes are local, not a current statewide tax for all businesses, and should not be confused with the state taxes above.

West Virginia also imposes severance taxes on coal, oil, gas, timber, and other natural resources, and a business registration tax tied to the business registration certificate. Those taxes apply only to businesses in the relevant activities or to businesses required to register.

Sellers need a record of taxable sales and of tax collected as a separate amount, per the recordkeeping section, and purchasers need use tax records for taxable purchases and services where sales tax was not collected.

Payroll records and employer reporting

Employers must report the hiring, rehiring, or return to work of an employee or independent contractor to the Bureau for Child Support Enforcement within 14 days of the date of hiring, rehiring, or return to work. That is shorter than the 20 day rule in most states in this batch. If reports are transmitted magnetically or electronically in two monthly submissions, they must be not less than twelve days nor more than sixteen days apart. The report includes the person name, address, and Social Security number, start date, employer name and address, any different payroll office address, and the employer federal tax identification number. Source, West Virginia Code section 48.18.125, official code site: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/48-18-125/

The same code section provides a civil penalty of no more than 25 dollars per failure to report, with a higher penalty described in the code where the failure results from a conspiracy between employer and employee or contractor not to report or to report falsely, as summarized from the code text in search results.

Income, purchases, and documentation

Purchase invoices are kept with a use tax review note for services and tangible property brought into West Virginia, and contractor and severance records in Part 6 are kept by job or by well, mine, or property, as applicable.

How long to retain records

Consumers sales and service tax records and documents must be preserved for not less than three years, unless the Tax Commissioner consents in writing to destruction within that period or orders that they be kept longer. Source: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/11-15-23/

For the article covered by the recordkeeping section1.12B.14, records must be kept for not less than three years after the annual return is filed, unless the Tax Commissioner authorizes earlier destruction in writing, and an extension of time for assessment automatically extends the record period for years covered by the extension agreement. Source: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/11-12B-14/

When an industry needs additional records

Energy and mining. Coal, oil, gas, and other severance taxpayers must keep production, extraction, sales, and price records that support severance tax returns, in addition to income and sales tax records. Scope is limited to producers and processors in severance taxable activities. The record duty example in the recordkeeping section1.12B.14 in the retention section is from a severance related article.

Motor carriers. International Fuel Tax Agreement licensees keep mileage and fuel records by jurisdiction and file quarterly reports through the Tax Division program. Source: West Virginia Tax Division, International Fuel Tax Agreement, https://tax.wv.gov/business/motorfuel/internationalfueltaxagreement/pages/internationalfueltaxagreement.aspx

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Charleston retailer keeps a daily record of taxable sales and tax collected, plus invoices and bills of lading, for at least three years, and does not destroy them at three years for a year the Tax Commissioner has ordered kept longer or has under audit.

An employer hires a contractor and an employee in the same week and reports both to the Bureau for Child Support Enforcement within 14 days, because West Virginia new hire law in the payroll section covers independent contractors as well as employees, unlike a rule limited only to employees.

Consumer sales tax and resource industry records

A retail tax record explains money collected from customers, while a severance tax record may rely on production quantities and values. West Virginia businesses in both worlds need distinct support for those figures.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in West Virginia is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for West Virginia businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
