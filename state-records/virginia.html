Virginia Bookkeeping

Virginia expects taxpayers to retain records substantiating returns, with sales records detailed by locality where applicable. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Virginia.

Keeping records that support the books

Virginia Code the retention section8.1.102 requires every taxpayer to retain suitable records and documents substantiating all information contained on any return required under the tax subtitle, and any other pertinent records the Tax Commissioner may require by regulation, and the retention section8.1.103 requires those records to be available during regular business hours for inspection. Source, Department of Taxation ruling quoting those sections: https://www.tax.virginia.gov/laws-rules-decisions/rulings-tax-commissioner/22-108

For retail sales and use tax, Virginia Code the retention section8.1.633 and regulation 23 Virginia Administrative Code 10.210.470 require every registered dealer to keep adequate and complete records necessary to determine and substantiate tax liability, including sales, leases, and purchases, books of account, and exemption and resale certificates. Source: https://www.tax.virginia.gov/laws-rules-decisions/rulings-tax-commissioner/06-20 and https://www.tax.virginia.gov/laws-rules-decisions/rulings-tax-commissioner/22-132

How state taxes affect the ledger

Virginia imposes an individual income tax, a corporate income tax, a retail sales tax and a use tax, and employer withholding tax on Virginia wages. Registered dealers collect and remit sales tax and file returns, including Form ST.9 for retail sales and use tax, with schedules that allocate sales to localities and regions where additional taxes apply. Source for the ST.9 allocation structure, Department form material in search results from tax.virginia.gov, is summarized here without a direct form link, to avoid inventing a URL.

A dealer with locations in more than one locality, or with no fixed location, needs sales records by locality, because Virginia sales tax includes a state component and a local component and, in specified regions, additional regional taxes described on the ST.9 schedules.

Payroll records and employer reporting

Employers withhold Virginia income tax from wages, register for a withholding account with Virginia Tax, and file withholding returns and annual reconciliations. Covered employers also register with the Virginia Employment Commission, file quarterly wage reports, and pay unemployment tax.

How long to retain records

Records and documents under Virginia Code the retention section8.1.102 must be preserved for three years from the required date for filing the return to which they pertain. Source: https://www.tax.virginia.gov/laws-rules-decisions/rulings-tax-commissioner/22-108

Sales and use tax dealers must keep and maintain records for three years, including exemption and resale certificates, under Virginia Code the retention section8.1.633 and the regulation cited in the recordkeeping section. Source: https://www.tax.virginia.gov/laws-rules-decisions/rulings-tax-commissioner/06-20

When an industry needs additional records

Construction and contractors. Virginia sales tax treatment for contractors differs from retailers because contractors are generally treated as users of materials in real property construction in many fact patterns, so contracts, materials invoices, and records separating materials and labor are important audit records under the dealer and purchaser record rules in the recordkeeping section. The specific treatment depends on contract facts and current Department guidance.

Government contractors and nonprofits that claim exemptions need certificate and contract records that link an exempt purchase to the exempt entity and the contract, because an unsupported exempt sale can be assessed to the dealer, as illustrated by the exempt sales discussion in the ruling in the recordkeeping section from 2006.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Virginia dealer with stores in two localities keeps sales and exemption certificates by location for three years from the filing date for the return, and files the ST.9 schedules that allocate sales to each locality, rather than filing from a single combined sales total.

A Richmond employer reports a new hire within 20 days, withholds Virginia income tax from wages, and files a separate quarterly wage report with the Virginia Employment Commission, because the new hire report does not satisfy the wage report duty.

Local sales allocation and dealer record retention

Virginia dealers working across locations should preserve the allocation used for their tax return with the underlying invoices. A customer exempt from tax also creates a documentation question that cannot be answered from a bank deposit alone.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Virginia is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for Virginia businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
