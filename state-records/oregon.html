Oregon Bookkeeping

Oregon has no general sales tax, but corporate activity tax and combined payroll reporting affect business records. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Oregon.

Keeping records that support the books

Every employing unit must maintain a payroll record for individuals performing services for it. The Oregon unemployment rule, Oregon Administrative Rule 471 031 0005, requires the record to show total payroll by calendar quarter and, for each individual, name, Social Security account number, total remuneration for each pay period and for all pay periods in each calendar quarter shown separately for wages not excluded by employment law, wages excluded by that law, and the cash value of noncash remuneration, plus the date of each pay period, hire and rehire dates, termination date, and hours worked in each calendar quarter. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-471-031-0005

Oregon businesses keep a commercial activity file that accumulates receipts from Oregon activity during the year, because the corporate activity tax is measured on Oregon commercial activity rather than on sales tax collections.

How state taxes affect the ledger

Oregon has no statewide sales tax and no statewide use tax on ordinary purchases. The Department of Revenue corporate activity tax page describes the corporate activity tax as a tax that is not a transactional tax such as a retail sales tax and not an income tax, which confirms that Oregon businesses do not collect a state sales tax on retail sales. Source: https://www.oregon.gov/dor/programs/businesses/Pages/Corporate-Activity-Tax.aspx

Oregon has a corporate activity tax, called the CAT. It is an annual tax, applicable to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2020, imposed for the privilege of doing business in Oregon and measured on a business commercial activity, meaning the total amount a business realises from transactions and activity in the normal course of business in Oregon. The CAT is applied to Oregon taxable commercial activity above 1 million dollars. The tax is computed as 250 dollars plus 0.57 percent of Oregon commercial activity above the 1 million dollar threshold, under Oregon Revised Statutes 317A.125. Only taxpayers with more than 1 million dollars of taxable Oregon commercial activity have a payment obligation, and the return is due on the 15th day of the fourth month after the end of the tax year. Source: https://www.oregon.gov/dor/programs/businesses/Pages/Corporate-Activity-Tax.aspx

Oregon also has a personal income tax, a corporate income or excise tax, and employer withholding, plus a statewide transit tax withholding and local payroll based taxes in some areas, including the Portland area transit and arts taxes where they apply. Bookkeeping must keep Oregon receipts, income, and payroll amounts separate from amounts sourced to other states.

Payroll records and employer reporting

Employing units must keep the payroll records described in the recordkeeping section of this state section for a period of 3 calendar years and furnish the information to the Employment Department on demand. The employing unit must also maintain, for each calendar quarter, the date each pay period ends, total wages by pay periods ending in the quarter, and the total number of employees in each pay period. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-471-031-0005

Oregon employers report payroll taxes on a combined quarterly report that covers withholding, unemployment insurance, and other payroll programs, and they report through the Employment Department system called Frances Online for unemployment and paid leave matters, while tax payments and many tax returns are handled through Department of Revenue Revenue Online.

How long to retain records

Payroll records under Oregon Administrative Rule 471 031 0005 must be kept for a period of 3 calendar years. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-471-031-0005

Motor carrier records for the Oregon weight mile tax must be stored at the carrier principal office or place of business, made available for inspection on request, and retained for a period of 3 years unless the Department authorises otherwise. Distance records must show origin and destination, Oregon entry and exit points, Oregon miles, routes, dates, and daily beginning and ending odometer readings for each vehicle. Source: https://www.oregon.gov/odot/mct/pages/recordkeeping.aspx

When an industry needs additional records

Motor carriers subject to the Oregon weight mile tax have the detailed distance and vehicle record duties described in the retention section of this state section. This applies to carriers operating covered vehicles on Oregon public highways, not to businesses generally. Source: https://www.oregon.gov/odot/mct/pages/recordkeeping.aspx

Cannabis businesses licensed by the Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission must track inventory and sales in the state cannabis tracking system and keep licensed activity records, in addition to income tax and CAT records. This applies only to licensed cannabis businesses. Oregon also has a cannabis tax administered by the Department of Revenue for licensed retailers.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Portland wholesaler has no sales tax to collect on Oregon sales, but its Oregon receipts pass 1 million dollars during the year. The bookkeeper keeps a monthly Oregon receipts total from the sales ledger, files the CAT return by the due date in the fourth month after year end, and keeps the receipts worksheet with the return.

A Salem employer prepares its combined quarterly payroll report. The payroll file for the quarter shows each employee name, Social Security number, wages by pay period, hours worked, withholding, unemployment wages, and paid leave contributions, and the file is kept for at least 3 calendar years.

Commercial Activity Tax and combined payroll reporting

An Oregon business can have no statewide retail sales tax yet still need detailed state sourced revenue information for Corporate Activity Tax. Its payroll file may also need to explain withholding, unemployment, and paid leave figures from the same underlying pay periods.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Oregon is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for Oregon businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
