New Mexico Bookkeeping

New Mexico gross receipts tax places receipts by location, deductions, and compensating tax at the center of recordkeeping. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in New Mexico.

Keeping records that support the books

New Mexico does not impose one general bookkeeping code on all private businesses. The enforceable duties arise tax by tax, mainly through the Tax Administration Act and the combined reporting system for gross receipts, compensating, and withholding taxes. A registered taxpayer must file a return for each reporting period even when no tax is due, a rule stated in the gross receipts regulations. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-mexico/3-2-2-15-NMAC

Records must be sufficient to show gross receipts by location, deductions claimed, and tax due, because the return reports receipts location by location. The Taxation and Revenue Department guidance to filers also explains assessment lookback periods, which is why the underlying receipts records matter in practice. Source: https://realfile.tax.newmexico.gov/CRS-1%20Filers%20Kit%20For%20Reporting%20Gross%20Receipts%20Withholding%20and%20Compensating%20Taxes%20July%20through%20December%202019.pdf

How state taxes affect the ledger

New Mexico has a gross receipts tax, not a conventional retail sales tax. It is imposed on the seller for the privilege of doing business, and it applies broadly to receipts from selling property and performing services in the state, subject to statutory deductions and exemptions. The statewide rate was reduced to 5 percent effective July 1, 2022, and local option rates add to that rate by location. Source: https://www.tax.newmexico.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Tax-laws-take-effect.pdf

New Mexico also has a compensating tax on the use of property and certain services acquired outside the state, a personal income tax, a corporate income tax, and employer withholding of income tax. Bookkeeping must therefore separate gross receipts, deductible receipts, taxable receipts, purchases subject to compensating tax, and payroll withholding.

Because local rates differ, point of sale and invoicing systems record the delivery or performance location for each transaction, not only the business address.

Payroll records and employer reporting

Employers withhold state income tax and report withholding through the Taxation and Revenue Department. Unemployment insurance tax and quarterly wage reporting go to the New Mexico Department of Workforce Solutions, which assigns the employer unemployment account.

How long to retain records

Department guidance to combined reporting filers states the normal assessment period is 3 years back from the end of the year the tax was due. The period extends to 6 years where a tax was underreported by more than 25 percent, to 7 years for nonfiled reports, and to 10 years where there is evidence of fraud. The same guidance advises filers that it is in their interest to maintain combined reporting records for 10 years. Source: https://realfile.tax.newmexico.gov/CRS-1%20Filers%20Kit%20For%20Reporting%20Gross%20Receipts%20Withholding%20and%20Compensating%20Taxes%20July%20through%20December%202019.pdf

When an industry needs additional records

Cannabis businesses are licensed and tracked through the Cannabis Control Division, and licensed activity creates inventory and sales tracking duties in addition to gross receipts tax records. This applies only to licensed cannabis establishments.

Construction contractors have gross receipts reporting tied to construction projects and locations, and the location based return structure means project records must show where work was performed.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Santa Fe services firm files gross receipts returns by location. It keeps, for each month, a receipts listing by customer location, the invoices that support two large deductions, and the filed return. When the Department asks about a deduction three years later, the firm can trace the return line back to the invoice and contract.

A shop hires a technician on March 3. The office reports the hire to the New Mexico New Hire Directory within 20 days, opens the unemployment wage record for the quarter, and files the hire paperwork with the first payroll record for that employee.

Gross receipts reporting for services and property

For a New Mexico service company, an invoice should preserve the service and location detail used when evaluating gross receipts tax. Bookkeeping should keep any deduction support with the receipt, rather than subtracting ordinary expenses from the gross receipts ledger.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in New Mexico is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for New Mexico businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
