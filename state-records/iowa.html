Iowa Bookkeeping

Iowa specifies electronic sales record expectations and detailed unemployment payroll records. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Iowa.

Keeping records that support the books

Iowa Code section 423.41 requires every retailer required or authorized to collect sales tax, and every person using tangible personal property, services, or the product of services in Iowa, to keep records, receipts, invoices, and other pertinent papers as the Director of Revenue requires, in the form the Director requires, for as long as the Director has authority to examine and determine tax due. Books and records must be made available within the state for examination on reasonable notice. If records are electronic, the taxpayer must comply with reasonable requests to provide them in a standard record format. Source: Iowa Code section 423.41, https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2011/423.41.pdf

How state taxes affect the ledger

Iowa has a state sales tax at 6 percent and a complementary use tax at 6 percent, plus local option sales tax of up to 1 percent in many jurisdictions, for a combined rate that is commonly 7 percent where the local option applies. Bookkeeping must record the sourcing location for each sale, because local option tax depends on where the sale is sourced.

Iowa also has individual and corporate income taxes, employer withholding, a statewide hotel and motel tax framework with local administration in some cases, motor fuel taxes, and excise taxes on cigarettes, tobacco, and vapor products. Recent Iowa law requires electronic filing for cigarette and tobacco filings under chapter 453A, effective July 1, 2025, according to Department guidance. Source: https://revenue.iowa.gov/taxes/tax-guidance/sales-use-excise-tax/cigarette-tobacco-tax/changes-cigarette-tobacco-tax-forms

Payroll records and employer reporting

Unemployment insurance records. Iowa Administrative Code rule 871-22.1 requires each employing unit to maintain, for five years after the calendar year in which the remuneration was paid or due, records evidencing remuneration reportable to Iowa Workforce Development. The records must show for each employee: name, Social Security number, dates of hire, rehire, return after layoff, and separation with reason, scheduled hours where applicable, total wages paid and date of payment with separate detail for money wages, cash value of other remuneration, allowances or reimbursements for travel and business expenses and amounts actually incurred, the state and Iowa county where services were performed, and hours and wages for covered versus excluded work where a pay period includes both. Source: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/rule/871.22.1.pdf

New hire reporting. Iowa employers report new hires within 20 days under the federal and state new hire framework administered for child support enforcement through the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services, Employer Child Support reporting. The 20 day period is the general period described in federal guidance for state directories.

When an industry needs additional records

Construction. Iowa has detailed sales and use tax rules for construction activities, readopted as Chapter 219 by the Department. Contractors must keep records that separate building materials, equipment, and taxable services, and that show whether a project is for a taxable or exempt owner, because tax on materials and on certain construction services depends on those facts. Source: Department rulemaking notice summary, https://revenue.iowa.gov/news/2024-07-30/administrative-rules-adopted-filed

Hotel and motel. Operators subject to hotel and motel tax must register, file, and keep guest and rate records, and on sale or discontinuation of the business file a final return within the following month and keep records as noted in the retention section.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

An Iowa retailer sells online and at a store in a local option jurisdiction. Its sales system records the delivery address, product description, price, tax status, and tax amount for each order, and can export those records in a standard format for the Department, so local option tax can be verified.

A small employer keeps a payroll file for each worker showing hire and separation dates, county of work, wages by quarter, and travel reimbursements with amounts accounted for, and holds the file and its quarterly reports for five years.

Electronic record readability and payroll location detail

Retaining a spreadsheet is only useful if it still explains the transaction years later. Iowa electronic record expectations make field definitions, vendor identity, dates, and tax status especially relevant. Employers also have reason to preserve county and work location data with wage records.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Iowa is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for Iowa businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
