Texas Bookkeeping

Texas sales and use tax, franchise tax calculations, and unemployment reporting rely on distinct supporting records. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Texas.

Keeping records that support the books

Texas Tax Code the recordkeeping section11.0041 requires a taxpayer who is required to keep records under the Tax Code to keep those records open to inspection and to produce contemporaneous records and supporting documentation that substantiate a claim about tax, penalty, or interest. Examples in the statute include invoices, vouchers, checks, shipping records, and contracts. Source, statute text reproduced by a secondary code publisher: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._tax_code_section_111.0041 and https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2022/tax-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-111/subchapter-a/section-111-0041/

In a Comptroller audit, the taxpayer must furnish records requested by the auditor that are necessary for a complete audit. The Comptroller audit notice lists sales invoices, purchase invoices, capital asset invoices, resale and exemption certificates, accounting books, general ledgers, subsidiary journals, charts of accounts, financial statements, federal income tax information, bank statements, workpapers, computer records, and documentation for refunds and credits. Source: Texas Comptroller, https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-146.pdf

How state taxes affect the ledger

Texas imposes a state sales tax and a use tax, plus local sales and use taxes, and a franchise tax on covered taxable entities including corporations, limited liability companies, limited partnerships, and professional associations, regardless of whether tax is due in a given year, with an annual report and a public information report described in Tax Code Chapter 171. Source for franchise tax report duties: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.171.htm

Franchise tax is not an income tax. It is generally based on margin as defined in Chapter 171, so revenue, cost of goods sold, and compensation records need to be kept in a form that supports the margin calculation chosen, in addition to sales tax records.

Payroll records and employer reporting

Texas has no state wage income tax withholding, because it has no personal income tax, as in the state tax section.

A liable unemployment employer must register with the Texas Workforce Commission within ten days of paying wages and becoming liable, file wage reports and pay unemployment taxes electronically unless it has an approved hardship waiver, and report new and rehired workers within 20 days of the hire date to the Employer New Hire Reporting Operations Center administered by the Office of the Attorney General. Sources: https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-tax/responsibilities-liable-employer and https://www.twc.texas.gov/employer-resources/new-hire-reporting

Income, purchases, and documentation

Purchase invoices are marked to show whether Texas sales tax was paid, whether a resale or exemption certificate was given, or whether use tax was accrued, because that distinction is a routine Comptroller audit point.

How long to retain records

The Comptroller audit notice states the statute of limitations is four years for taxes due to the state and that an audit normally covers the most recent four year period, with exceptions where a false or fraudulent return was filed with intent to evade, where a return would increase tax by 25 percent or more on correction as described in the notice, or where no return was filed. Source: https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-146.pdf

When an industry needs additional records

Energy. Oil and gas producers and other severance taxpayers must keep production, sales, and price records that support severance tax reports, in addition to franchise and sales tax records. The scope is limited to businesses in severance taxable production.

Construction and real estate improvement contractors must distinguish taxable services, materials incorporated into real property, and resale purchases, because Texas sales tax treatment differs by contract type. Their invoices and contracts are among the records listed in the Comptroller audit notice in the recordkeeping section.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Texas limited liability company keeps its sales invoices, purchase invoices, resale certificates, general ledger, bank statements, and franchise tax margin workpapers for at least four years, and keeps them longer for a year that is under Comptroller hearing, as required in the retention section.

An employer hires a worker on the first of the month, registers or confirms its Workforce Commission account, reports the new hire within 20 calendar days, and files its quarterly wage report electronically rather than by mail, unless it holds a hardship waiver.

Franchise tax calculations and seller documentation

A Texas company can keep a perfectly reconciled bank account and still lack the schedules needed to explain franchise tax margin calculations. Revenue, qualifying cost records, customer invoices, and exemption documentation should be connected but not lumped together.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Texas is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for Texas businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
