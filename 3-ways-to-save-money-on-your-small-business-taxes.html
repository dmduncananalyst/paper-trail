Three Ways Better Bookkeeping Can Support Accurate Business Taxes

Published 11/13/2026

Good tax preparation begins before the return

Businesses often think about taxes when the filing deadline approaches. By then, missing receipts and misclassified payments can make the work slower and less reliable. Clean bookkeeping does not create deductions that do not exist, but it can help a qualified preparer identify legitimate items and avoid costly reporting mistakes.

1. Capture business expenses with their evidence Maintain a consistent method for collecting invoices, receipts, merchant statements, and payment confirmations. Label transactions so it is clear who was paid, what the purchase was for, and which business activity it supported. For certain deductions, additional substantiation rules apply. A transaction description alone may not be enough.

Separate personal spending from business expenses and track shared expenses carefully. A tax preparer should determine what portion, if any, qualifies under the applicable rules.

2. Keep revenue and deposits correctly matched Deposits can represent customer payments, loans, owner contributions, transfers, or refunds. Not every deposit is revenue, and the timing of income recognition depends on the business's accounting method and applicable rules. Reconcile bank deposits with invoices, merchant statements, and other records so the tax preparer can understand what money came in and why.

Do not erase or ignore payments that appear inconsistent. Flag unresolved items and document their eventual resolution.

3. Review books before year end A year end review gives the business time to identify missing documents, review outstanding invoices and bills, confirm asset purchases, and correct duplicate transactions. Compare account balances with actual statements and verify payroll or contractor information when applicable. Discuss unusual items with the accountant before filing rather than guessing at tax treatment.
