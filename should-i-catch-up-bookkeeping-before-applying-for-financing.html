Get Your Books Ready Before Applying for Financing

Usually, yes, current and supported books make it easier to assemble financial information and explain business performance when applying for financing. Ask the lender which periods and reports it needs before preparing the package. Lenders set their own approval criteria, so completing bookkeeping does not guarantee funding.

Ask for the lender’s document list early

The request may include financial statements, tax returns, debt details and projections. Find out the required periods, reporting basis and acceptable report format before assembling the package. The SBA’s business planning guidance includes historical income statements, balance sheets and cash flow statements when supporting a funding request for an established business.

Correct inconsistencies before submission

Review bank and card reconciliations, loan balances, owner activity and outstanding customer or supplier amounts. A loan deposited into checking should not inflate sales. A missing card account can understate expenses and liabilities. These errors can make an otherwise polished report difficult to explain when the lender asks for supporting information.

Keep historical records separate from projections

A forecast describes expected future performance, while the completed books describe recorded activity. Label assumptions and unusual events clearly. For example, a planned new contract should not be inserted into historical sales merely to strengthen an application.
