Keep the Records From a Closed Bank Account

Closing a bank account does not remove its history from your bookkeeping. Record all final activity, reconcile the closing statement and document where any remaining money went. Keep the account’s records accessible for the applicable retention period, even after the account is no longer used.

Follow the final balance to its destination

The last withdrawal may transfer funds to another business account, repay an obligation or represent an owner transaction. Each situation needs its own treatment. A final service charge or interest credit can leave a small balance behind. Compare the books with the bank’s closing records before treating the account as fully settled.

Resolve payments still in transit

Check for outstanding checks, delayed deposits, merchant payouts and automatic charges connected to the old account. Some entries may require cancellation and replacement, while others need documentation showing where they ultimately cleared. Do not simply delete old outstanding transactions, because they may represent real obligations or customer payments.

Retain history while stopping new activity

Download statements and relevant transaction detail before online access ends. Once the closing reconciliation is documented, the account can generally be made inactive in the bookkeeping system without deleting its historical entries. Review recurring payment instructions and bank feed connections so transactions do not continue to be assigned to the closed account. IRS recordkeeping guidance emphasizes preserving support for reported income and expenses, which remains necessary after an account closes.

  • IRS, How long should I keep records?
