How to Prepare Your Books for Tax Season Without Guessing at Balances

Published 11/13/2026

Begin with the accounts that can be verified

Before a tax preparer can rely on reports, the underlying bank, credit card, and loan balances should be reviewed. Download the full year's statements and reconcile each account through the reporting date. Identify accounts that were closed, payment platforms that hold balances, and transfers that may be counted twice. A report can look polished while its balances remain wrong; reconciliation is the basic test.

Clear transaction problems

Review any uncategorized spending, unidentified deposits, duplicate invoices, unapplied customer payments, outstanding checks, and vendor credits. Separate loan proceeds from earned revenue, and debt principal payments from interest or other charges. Verify the purpose and documentation of large or unusual expenses.

Do not force adjustments just to make an account match. If an item cannot be substantiated, document the question and leave a clear exception for review.

Check reports and supporting schedules

Once transactions are reconciled, review the profit and loss statement, balance sheet, and general ledger. Look for negative balances where they do not make sense, income recorded in the wrong period, duplicated expenses, missing liabilities, or unexplained owner activity. Create or update supporting schedules for fixed assets, loans, prepaid items, and other material balances as applicable.

Where payroll is involved, compare payroll reports, employer tax payments, and relevant liability accounts. If inventory is material, coordinate with the person responsible for inventory counts and the accounting method.

Send a clean, documented package

Provide your tax professional with reconciled reports and access to the documents supporting unusual transactions. Clearly distinguish completed reconciliations from open questions. Keep a dated copy of the package and a record of any later corrections. The IRS emphasizes that records must support items reported on tax returns.
