Ohio Bookkeeping

Ohio gross receipts reporting can require a different view of activity from the profit and loss statement. Build that bridge alongside sales and payroll schedules.

Sales And Purchase Tax Evidence

Ohio sales tax covers tangible personal property and selected services; use tax addresses taxable purchases where sales tax was not collected. Keep the taxable sales detail, exemption support and transaction location with each reporting period.

Save the location evidence and tax calculation used in the sales system. For vendor purchases, retain invoices and receipt locations and flag untaxed supplies or equipment for use tax review. Ohio's use tax statute includes exceptions where the applicable sales tax was paid and requires support for claimed exemptions.

Sources: Ohio Sales Tax Levy, Ohio Purchase Use Tax

Commercial Activity Tax Receipts

Ohio's CAT statute imposes a gross receipts privilege tax on the business rather than directly on its customers. It distinguishes prohibited invoicing of the tax from recovery through prices and specified written lease arrangements. Determine applicability with the preparer using the current year rules, then keep a receipts schedule that can be reconciled to the ledger.

Do not assume net profit is the CAT reporting base. Separate sales proceeds, returns, financing deposits and other receipts, and provide transaction support for the preparer's inclusion and sourcing decisions. Avoid turning a monthly expense report into an unsupported gross receipts return.

Sources: Ohio Commercial Activity Tax Levy

Payroll And School District Evidence

Ohio employer guidance addresses both employer and school district withholding and electronic filing and payment. Retain completed IT 4 forms, employee residence and school district information, compensation dates, gross wages and tax withheld.

Reconcile each withholding liability to payroll registers, filed reconciliations and remittances. Route an employee move to the payroll specialist before changing the residence field alone; keep the effective date and the authorization supporting the revised setup.

Sources: Ohio Employer And School District Withholding 2026

Payables And Monthly Statements

As general accounting practice, connect supplier bills to delivery and approval records and record credits against the original purchase. Separate resale stock, operating supplies and fixed assets so purchase tax review can use meaningful categories.

At close, reconcile banks, cards, receivables and payables and issue a profit and loss statement and balance sheet. Present the CAT receipts bridge with sales tax and payroll liability rollforwards so the owner can see why tax reporting totals differ from revenue or cash deposits.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Ohio Distributor

Hypothetical example: a distributor records a customer sale, a bank loan and an owner contribution in one month. The bookkeeper labels the three receipts and supplies the sales documents to the CAT preparer. Adding all deposits or using net profit would leave the preparer without the required transaction analysis.

Sources: Ohio Commercial Activity Tax Levy

Unemployment Contributions And Quarterly Wages

Ohio law requires contributory employers to file quarterly contribution and wage reports showing total and taxable remuneration, individual wages and weeks paid. Its administrative rule calls for prescribed electronic transmission and continued reporting until written permission to discontinue. Keep the employer account and assigned rate notice with the payroll file, separately from Ohio income tax withholding.

Treat the report and contribution payment as separate evidence. Reconcile reported wages, taxable wages and the assigned contribution calculation to unemployment expense and the closing liability, then carry unpaid contributions visibly into cash planning. Include these reports with the CAT receipts bridge in the year end handoff. A cash shortage does not remove the quarterly report requirement.

Sources: Ohio Quarterly Contribution And Wage Reporting, Ohio Quarterly Electronic Unemployment Reports

Year End Records And Retention

Assemble the final receipts bridge, sales and use returns, payroll reconciliation, asset purchases, debt statements and open questions for the tax preparer. Include the current CAT applicability conclusion rather than copying a prior year threshold.

Ohio's CAT record rule generally requires four years from the return due date or filing date, whichever is later, with written authority for earlier destruction or longer retention. The 2026 employer filing instructions require accurate withholding records for at least four years from the due date. These distinct periods are not a universal authorization to destroy sales, asset or disputed records.

Sources: Ohio Employer And School District Withholding 2026, Ohio CAT Record Retention Rule
