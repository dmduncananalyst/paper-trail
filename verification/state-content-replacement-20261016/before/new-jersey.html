New Jersey Bookkeeping

New Jersey payroll uses distinct withholding and wage reporting forms, while sales records need enough detail to reproduce transaction history.

Sales, Exemptions And Purchase Use Tax

New Jersey sales documentation should distinguish total receipts, taxable receipts, tax due and purchases subject to use tax. Keep product descriptions, delivery evidence and exemption certificates linked to sales. Review goods and services using current classification guidance rather than relying on a broad customer or department label.

For vendor bills, preserve the invoice and destination, identify resale stock and company use separately, and send uncertain purchases to the preparer. Keep credits linked to the original bill so a later refund does not disappear into miscellaneous income or an unexplained tax adjustment.

Sources: New Jersey Sales Tax Publication S&U-4

Payroll Filing And Wage Evidence

New Jersey employers must register for payroll tax purposes to file NJ-927, NJ-W-3 and WR-30. Those forms support different reporting functions. Preserve the submitted versions and confirmations with the wage register so a successful payment is not mistaken for completion of every wage reporting requirement.

Use employee withholding instructions and actual work location information in payroll review. Reconcile gross pay, deductions, net pay and employer contributions separately. Maintain records supporting any change affecting an employee rather than replacing the earlier form without its effective date.

Sources: New Jersey Employer Payroll Filing Options, New Jersey Employer Payroll Forms

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

New Jersey entity reporting can include Corporation Business Tax or partnership reporting based on classification and activity. Partnerships can have a filing fee and nonresident partner payment obligations; eligible entities may also elect Business Alternative Income Tax. Preserve federal and state entity status, partner counts, ownership changes and resident and nonresident income allocation for preparer review.

Track partnership filing fee payments, nonresident partner tax and elected BAIT payments separately. The current partnership instructions distinguish where certain entity tax credits may be applied. At year end, provide member statements, election evidence and a payment and credit rollforward so the preparer can confirm that credits allocated to owners agree with those retained or used by the entity.

Sources: New Jersey Corporation Business Tax Overview, New Jersey Partnership Instructions

Income, Vendor Bills And Equipment

A retailer can receive online settlements, store cash, customer deposits and owner funding through the same bank account. Preserve the source of each receipt and reconcile processing fees independently. Compare customer refunds with their original invoices and the inventory returned.

For accounts payable, match approved purchases with goods received and supplier statements. Track equipment, improvements and financing separately from merchandise and routine costs. A debt payment needs an allocation between principal and other charges supported by the loan statement. These are general accounting controls, not a promise of a tax deduction.

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

New Jersey covered employers report wages on WR-30 and applicable unemployment, workforce, disability and family leave contributions through NJ-927. Preserve the actual employer and worker contribution rates and the accepted reports; using common forms does not mean every component is company expense.

Reconcile each contribution component separately, including employee amounts withheld and employer charges. Review the rate notice and payment history in Employer Access and compare wage records with the payroll register. The annual handoff should identify adjustments, deficiencies and program balances so a contribution credit is not confused with an income withholding credit or an owner tax payment.

Sources: New Jersey Employer Contribution Registration

Monthly Reports And System Changes

Close bank and card reconciliations, compare supplier balances with unpaid bills and investigate old customer credits. The monthly profit and loss statement should connect to a balance sheet containing supported inventory, deposits and liabilities. Explain the effect of stock counts and damaged merchandise adjustments.

When the sales system changes, preserve item mappings, transaction exports and the date each tax setting changed. Reconcile old and new system totals across the transition. Keep payroll and sales liability schedules with the monthly package so return adjustments remain visible to the owner.

Hypothetical Register Migration

Hypothetical example: a New Jersey gift shop replaces its registers and finds that the new system combines several product categories under one button. The bookkeeper exports old transaction detail, preserves the item mapping and reviews the new categories with the preparer. The scenario does not assume every item within a category shares one tax treatment.

Sources: New Jersey Sales Tax Publication S&U-4

Year End And Preservation

Prepare the tax handoff with annual sales and use reconciliations, inventory support, vendor totals, payroll form confirmations, asset records and open notices. Include the register conversion explanation and any corrections posted after the annual statements were first produced.

The New Jersey sales publication requires relevant books, certificates and other tax evidence to remain available for at least four years. It also sets conditions for electronic or copied records and original destruction, so a scan alone should not be treated as blanket permission to discard everything. Confirm the record medium and retention clock before destruction; employer, federal, asset and disputed period requirements need separate schedules.

Sources: New Jersey Sales Tax Publication S&U-4
