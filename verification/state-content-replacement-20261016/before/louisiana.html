Louisiana Bookkeeping

Louisiana books need transaction locations and separate state and parish tax support, including current digital product records.

State And Parish Sales Support

Louisiana state and parish taxes involve separate taxing jurisdictions. Maintain the location behind each sale and preserve state and local return acknowledgments. A centralized filing portal does not remove the need to explain which transactions belong to each jurisdiction.

The current dealer record statute includes tangible property, digital products and taxable services. Keep the product description, transaction date, delivery information and evidence supporting any deduction. Review purchased software and digital products with the preparer using current rules rather than relying on an old tax code in the accounting system.

Sources: Louisiana State And Parish Tax FAQ, Louisiana RS 47:309 Dealer Records

Payroll Accounts And Withholding

Louisiana requires withholding returns and payments electronically. Its current employer page requires a quarterly return for each assigned period, including zero periods, and an annual reconciliation. An active account does not close merely because the business stops paying wages.

As a payroll control, preserve worker certificates, wage registers, actual work locations and confirmations from LaTAP or approved software. Reconcile the withholding liability separately from net wages, federal taxes and unemployment obligations. Keep any final return and account closure evidence with the last payroll.

Sources: Louisiana Withholding

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

Louisiana corporation income tax remains distinct from the corporation franchise tax, which was repealed for franchise tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. A corporation should have the preparer evaluate its income tax filing position and any earlier franchise periods. Preserve federal taxable income support, Louisiana adjustments, activity locations, credits and estimated payments; do not remove old franchise balances solely because current periods changed.

For the monthly tax schedule, distinguish current income tax estimates, prior franchise liabilities and interest or penalties. At year end, give the preparer a reconciliation from the ledger to payments by tax type and period, along with ownership changes and historical filings. A franchise account credit or notice can relate to a period that survives repeal.

Sources: Louisiana Corporation Income And Franchise Tax

Vendor Bills And Operating Income

The dealer statute calls for invoices, shipping papers and other pertinent records supporting taxable purchases and use. Keep a vendor register identifying resale stock, operating supplies, equipment and digital subscriptions. Send invoices with uncertain tax treatment to a purchase review list with their use location.

For the general ledger, identify customer receipts separately from owner advances, borrowings and refunds. Connect supplier credits to the original invoice and customer credits to the original sale. Preserve contracts and financing documents for assets so cost and debt are not confused.

Sources: Louisiana RS 47:309 Dealer Records

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

Liable Louisiana employers file quarterly wage and tax reports through Louisiana Works. Its employer services provide wage amendments, payments, benefit charges and tax rate notices. Preserve the unemployment employer account number separately from the revenue withholding account, together with employee wage detail, the assigned rate and filed report confirmation.

As a practical reconciliation, compare quarterly report wages with payroll registers and explain any difference in taxable wages. Match contributions and benefit charge activity to the unemployment liability account. Include rate changes, amended quarters and outstanding notices in the year end payroll handoff so the preparer can distinguish reporting corrections from ordinary payroll expense.

Sources: Louisiana Employer Wage And Tax Reporting

Monthly Statements By Location

A business serving several parishes benefits from a location reconciliation that bridges sales system totals to tax reports and bank settlements. Investigate missing address information before the close. A processor deposit reduced by fees should still connect to the gross sale and tax detail.

Reconcile cash, cards, receivables, bills and debt before delivering financial statements. Keep state and local liability rollforwards beside the balance sheet. Explain payments applied to an earlier period and avoid clearing an unexplained agency balance through miscellaneous expense.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Festival Seller

Hypothetical example: a Louisiana maker sells at events in two parishes and also ships online orders. The bookkeeper saves each event location, sale detail and shipping address, separates deposits by event, and gives the preparer a jurisdiction summary. The scenario establishes neither an exemption nor a universal filing method for every seller.

Sources: Louisiana State And Parish Tax FAQ

Year End And Preservation

Prepare a handoff containing sales by location and product class, exemption evidence, payroll annual reconciliation, inventory counts, asset invoices and unresolved purchase questions. Keep historical tax mappings where a system changed its treatment of digital products.

Louisiana RS 47:309 requires dealer records to remain until the related taxes have prescribed. That is a legal condition requiring review of the relevant periods and any suspension or interruption, rather than an automatic number of years from the invoice. Federal employment tax records have their own minimum four year requirement. Obtain written retention decisions for state payroll, income tax and contested periods before destruction.

Sources: Louisiana RS 47:309 Dealer Records, IRS Employment Tax Records
