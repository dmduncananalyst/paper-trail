New Hampshire Bookkeeping

New Hampshire has no general sales tax, but business profits, enterprise value and hospitality activity need distinct accounting support.

General Sales And Hospitality Activity

New Hampshire has no general sales tax on goods purchased in the state. That statement does not eliminate the separate Meals and Rentals tax or another state tax where a transaction reaches another jurisdiction. Retain sales destinations and the exact activity description for professional review.

A lodging or food business should preserve room, meal, rental and refund detail separately from other receipts. Save vendor invoices and receiving evidence for all purchases. A New Hampshire supplier address should not be used to decide an outside jurisdiction obligation without the actual delivery and use facts.

Sources: New Hampshire Sales Tax FAQ, New Hampshire RSA 78-A:19

Business Profits And Enterprise Value

New Hampshire Business Profits Tax and Business Enterprise Tax measure different bases. The agency describes enterprise value using compensation, interest and dividends, while business profits concerns taxable business profits. Maintain separate schedules so a preparer can evaluate applicability and available credits.

As a bookkeeping practice, distinguish operating expense, debt interest, owner distributions and compensation in the ledger. A company showing a small accounting profit can still need enterprise tax review. Retain the tax professional calculation and reconciliation rather than posting an unexplained annual tax adjustment.

Sources: New Hampshire Business Taxes

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

New Hampshire business profits and enterprise taxes have independent applicability tests and bases; the absence of general sales tax does not resolve them. Enterprise value includes compensation, interest and dividends, while taxable business profits uses the business tax computation. Keep gross receipts, entity ownership, federal classification and state activity evidence for the preparer eligibility review.

Maintain schedules of owner compensation, interest and distributions with source documents, and retain business tax estimates and any enterprise tax credit used against profits tax. At year end, reconcile the two tax calculations and all payments rather than applying one flat percentage to accounting profit. A change in legal form or operating locations belongs in the handoff because it can affect return preparation and allocation.

Sources: New Hampshire Business Taxes

Employer And Vendor Records

New Hampshire business record rules include payroll, paid and unpaid vendor invoices, bank records and contracts. State labor guidance identifies unemployment coverage as a separate employer question. Maintain wage registers, approved hours, work locations and worker agreements so payroll specialists can assess the actual relationships.

Reconcile payroll deductions and employer charges to payments and reporting confirmations. For accounts payable, link supplier bills to services or goods received, approvals and credits. A contractor invoice should describe the engagement, but its label does not by itself establish independent contractor status.

Sources: New Hampshire Rev 300, New Hampshire Labor Worker Classification

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

Liable New Hampshire employers must file a quarterly tax and wage report and pay unemployment contributions, including a report when no wages were paid. NH Employment Security separately identifies gross, excess and taxable wages. Keep its employer account, assigned rate and accepted wage detail independent from business enterprise compensation schedules.

Compare reported worker wages with the payroll register, explain annual wage base differences and preserve corrections on the appropriate adjustment records. Reconcile contributions, payments and account credits before preparing year end statements. Include zero quarter confirmations and any closure or suspension communication so a temporary business shutdown does not leave an unexplained missing report.

Sources: New Hampshire Quarterly Tax And Wage Report

Monthly Financial Statements

For a hospitality business, compare booking system activity with deposits, refunds and cash settlements before preparing financial statements. Review advance bookings and amounts still owed to suppliers. Explain why current cash can include payments for services scheduled in a later period.

Maintain a monthly bridge from the ledger to the business profits and enterprise value schedules. Preserve separate information on compensation, financing costs and distributions so annual tax work can use the books directly. Review bank reconciliations, receivables, payables and debt with the owner.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Workshop And Guest Rooms

Hypothetical example: a New Hampshire woodworking business adds guest rooms above its studio. The bookkeeper creates distinct receipt categories for merchandise, room stays and customer deposits, preserves booking and refund records and asks the preparer to review hospitality tax registration. The general absence of sales tax is not treated as an exemption for room receipts.

Sources: New Hampshire RSA 78-A:19, New Hampshire Business Taxes

Year End And Retention

Give the preparer annual statements, compensation and interest schedules, distribution records, hospitality reports, assets, loans and outstanding notices. Preserve filing versions and any credit carryforward calculations so next year begins with supported balances.

Rev 308.01 requires business profits records for at least three years from filing or through completion of audits, administrative appeals and judicial proceedings. Meals and Rentals operator records have a separate three year rule under RSA 78-A:19. Employer unemployment, federal tax, property basis and unresolved claims can need other periods, so do not apply the business profits minimum to every file.

Sources: New Hampshire RSA 78-A:19, New Hampshire Rev 300
