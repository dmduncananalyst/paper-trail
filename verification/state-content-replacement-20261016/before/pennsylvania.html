Pennsylvania Bookkeeping

Pennsylvania record rules require usable transaction evidence, including why a purchase was treated as nontaxable. A payment alone cannot answer that question.

Sales And Purchase Records

Pennsylvania requires businesses to report use tax on taxable purchases when the seller has not collected the tax. Keep invoices and evidence of tax paid, or document the reason a purchase was considered nontaxable. Record the nature, use, price and relevant dates.

The recordkeeping regulation calls for daily and reporting period sales detail by outlet, including cash and credit sales. It also addresses separate books for distinct retail and other business activities. Organize these records before summarizing them in the general ledger.

Sources: Pennsylvania Code Section 34.2 Keeping Of Records, Pennsylvania Use Tax For Businesses

Employer Evidence

Pennsylvania withholding guidance considers both employee residence and where services occur, including reciprocal arrangements supported by the appropriate certificate. Keep work locations, employee declarations and the effective dates of changes with the payroll record.

As a general control, compare compensation and tax withheld in payroll registers with filed reports and payments. Give local payroll obligations their own review task; a state withholding reconciliation does not resolve every municipality or work location question.

Sources: Pennsylvania Income Subject To Withholding

Payables, Income And Expense

Match a supplier bill to the receiving record and its payment. A retailer that also installs products should retain the contract and job reference so inventory purchases, installation costs and business assets can be traced to the correct activity.

Separate credit sales from collections in the customer ledger. Identify owner transfers, borrowing and refunds before recording bank deposits as income. Keep explanations for disputed bills and customer credits.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Monthly Statements And Tax Reconciliation

General bookkeeping practice is to reconcile banks, cards, customer balances and unpaid bills before issuing a profit and loss statement and balance sheet. Compare outlet sales totals with ledger revenue and explain timing differences in card settlements.

Include a purchase use tax schedule with invoice references and the preparer's decisions. Tie reported tax and payments to ledger liabilities, leaving adjustments visible so the next month's opening balance has a clear explanation.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Two Activity Business

Hypothetical example: a Pennsylvania business sells cabinets at retail and performs construction work. The bookkeeper uses separate activity codes and attaches purchase purpose and job details, giving the preparer evidence to evaluate each transaction rather than applying the retail treatment to every job.

Sources: Pennsylvania Code Section 34.2 Keeping Of Records

UCMS Reporting And Entity Tax Handoff

Pennsylvania's UCMS process requires covered employers to file quarterly wage and tax reports and remit contributions. The wage reporting page distinguishes gross and taxable wages, employee wage detail and credit weeks, and provides correction reports. Retain those components and compare them with payroll records. Accrue employer contributions distinctly, explain wage basis differences and match payments to the covered quarter.

Pennsylvania recognizes federal S status unless the business elects otherwise for Pennsylvania; its guidance describes owner shares of income, loss and credit. Give the preparer the election file, ownership history, resident and nonresident owner detail and state adjustments to income and expenses. Preserve distributions and capital activity separately from deductible spending and keep state entity tax payments traceable to the approved filing.

Sources: Pennsylvania UC Wage Reporting, Pennsylvania Partnerships S Corporations And LLCs

Year End And Retention

Supply reconciled outlet sales, purchase review, payroll reports, asset invoices, loans and unresolved classifications to the tax preparer. Include filing acknowledgments and an account of every material year end adjustment.

Section 34.2 requires its sales and use tax records to remain available for at least three years from the end of the calendar year to which they relate. Preserve longer when another applicable rule or an open matter requires it; that period does not establish a disposal date for all payroll and asset records.

Sources: Pennsylvania Code Section 34.2 Keeping Of Records
