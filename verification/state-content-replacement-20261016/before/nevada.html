Nevada Bookkeeping

Nevada combines sales and use reporting with a payroll based Modified Business Tax and a separate gross revenue Commerce Tax review.

Sales And County Purchase Records

Nevada sales and use taxes cover taxable tangible property transactions. Consumer use tax complements sales tax when taxable property is used, stored or consumed in Nevada without the applicable sales tax paid. Keep the invoice description and use or delivery county for review.

Link sales, returns, resale support and purchase invoices with the reporting period. A vendor bill lacking tax should enter the purchase review register. Preserve equipment delivery evidence and separate items bought for resale from items retained for company use.

Sources: Nevada Sales And Use Tax

Modified Business Tax And Employer Wages

Nevada Modified Business Tax is based on payroll, with different employer categories and exceptions. The agency ties gross wages to the quarterly wages reported to the Employment Security Division and identifies eligible employer paid health benefits in the calculation. Preserve the wage comparison and benefit evidence.

Reconcile gross wages, employee deductions and employer costs before reviewing the MBT return. Keep unemployment reporting and MBT schedules separately identifiable even when they use the same underlying wage register. Do not assume a tax account registration creates every other required account.

Sources: Nevada Modified Business Tax

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

Nevada business tax review should identify Commerce Tax applicability and any payroll based Modified Business Tax rather than assuming an ordinary state net income return describes the obligation. Commerce Tax depends on Nevada gross revenue and industry, with exclusions and threshold rules requiring actual entity facts. Preserve the federal classification and ownership chart with Nevada revenue sourcing support.

Where Commerce Tax applies, prepare a separate annual bridge from revenue per books to the reported Nevada revenue base. Keep deduction decisions and any Commerce Tax credit used against Modified Business Tax with both return files. At year end, the preparer needs gross receipts detail and credit utilization records as well as net profit; federal income tax and other jurisdictions still require their own analysis.

Sources: Nevada Commerce Tax And MBT Credit FAQ

Commerce Tax And Revenue Classification

Nevada Commerce Tax uses Nevada gross revenue and industry classification, with an annual filing threshold. Retain revenue by business activity and the evidence for where receipts are assigned. Have the preparer evaluate the current threshold and exclusions using the actual entity facts.

For management accounting, customer deposits, operating receipts, borrowings and owner contributions should remain distinct. Accounts payable should preserve the vendor invoice, business purpose, payment and any capitalization decision. An expense recognized in the profit and loss statement does not automatically reduce a gross revenue tax base.

Sources: Nevada Commerce Tax

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

Nevada DETR requires covered employers to report and pay unemployment contributions through its employer workflow, distinct from Modified Business Tax at the Department of Taxation. The quarterly contribution and wage report uses individual gross wages and tips. Save the employer contribution rate notice, wage submission and payment acknowledgment from the current employer service.

Reconcile unemployment report wages to the payroll register and separately bridge those wages to MBT calculations, including any difference attributable to the MBT benefit deduction. An early wage response requested for a benefit claim does not replace the normal quarterly report. Include all quarterly submissions, corrections and outstanding unemployment balances in the annual handoff.

Sources: Nevada DETR Employer UI Information

Monthly Reports And Tax Bridges

Reconcile bank accounts, card settlements, supplier balances and equipment debt before issuing monthly statements. Keep county sales totals linked to gross receipts and tax liabilities, with processing fees shown separately. Explain outstanding customer deposits and inventory differences beside the balance sheet.

Maintain a quarterly wage bridge for MBT and unemployment, plus a separate annual revenue bridge for Commerce Tax review. This allows the owner to understand why a profitable month, a large payroll and a gross revenue threshold measure different things. Keep preparer adjustments documented by period.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Health Benefit Adjustment

Hypothetical example: a Nevada shop pays a health insurance invoice covering several employees and later receives a credit. The bookkeeper saves the coverage details, payment and credit, then gives payroll specialists the facts needed to evaluate the MBT health benefit treatment. The example assumes neither deductibility nor a specific tax saving.

Sources: Nevada Modified Business Tax

Year End And Preservation

At year end, supply reconciled statements, sales and use workpapers, wage comparisons, benefit support, industry revenue detail, asset records and all relevant agency acknowledgments. Keep tax account notices with their original periods and unresolved credits on a separate schedule.

Nevada DOR audit guidance states a four year minimum for registered business records and eight years for an unregistered business in the sales and use context. Preserve invoices, return workpapers, exemption documents and system exports accordingly, with longer holds where required. The usual audit lookback is not the same as the preservation minimum, and other tax or employment categories need separate review.

Sources: Nevada Audit FAQ
