New Mexico Bookkeeping

New Mexico gross receipts tax is imposed on sellers, so the ledger needs receipt classifications and deduction evidence beyond an ordinary sales tax account.

Gross Receipts And Deduction Evidence

New Mexico gross receipts tax is imposed on businesses and may be passed to customers; when passed on, the agency requires it to be separately stated on the invoice. Preserve the activity description and transaction location for the preparer because local location codes affect reporting.

Nontaxable Transaction Certificates relate to specific gross receipts deductions. Keep the certificate type, customer and transaction support together; the agency permits alternative evidence for many deductions but identifies exceptions. Ordinary business expenses do not automatically become gross receipts deductions. Keep net income accounting separate from the tax receipt classification.

Sources: New Mexico Gross Receipts Overview, New Mexico NTTC Resources

Purchase And Vendor Review

New Mexico electronic record rules require detail that supports tax computation and can be interpreted during an examination. Keep supplier identities, item descriptions, amounts, tax information and shipping facts available even where the accounting export uses product or vendor codes.

As a practical accounts payable control, flag purchases requiring compensating tax review and document the preparer decision. Separate goods acquired for resale from items used by the company, and retain the NTTC evidence actually supplied. A certificate used for one type of purchase should not silently carry over to a different activity.

Sources: New Mexico NMAC 3.1.5 Records

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

New Mexico corporate income and franchise reporting is separate from gross receipts and compensating tax. Federal classification helps determine whether the business uses a corporate, S corporation or other pass through return. Keep tax elections, state activity, ownership and federal return support so the preparer can evaluate both income and franchise obligations rather than assuming the gross receipts filing covers everything.

Maintain a separate rollforward of income and franchise estimates, credits and notices. For a pass through entity, give the preparer member residence, allocation schedules and any applicable owner or entity election documents. The year end reconciliation should connect the final tax adjustments to the financial statements without treating the gross receipts tax base as the same measure as taxable income.

Sources: New Mexico Corporate Income And Franchise FYI-350, New Mexico Pass Through Entity Filing

Wage Withholding And Workers Compensation

New Mexico requires covered employers to register and withhold state income tax, with stated exceptions requiring individual facts. Wage withholding uses a separate return from nonwage withholding. Preserve worker instructions, residence, service locations and payroll registers for review.

The agency also collects a workers compensation administration fee for covered employers and employees. Paying that fee does not provide workers compensation insurance, and unemployment reporting is through Workforce Solutions. Reconcile each liability separately rather than treating one payroll provider withdrawal as proof that every employer obligation was completed.

Sources: New Mexico Withholding And Workers Compensation

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

New Mexico Workforce Solutions requires an activated employer account before accepting wage records and unemployment contributions. Its quarterly contribution report distinguishes total wages, excess wages, taxable wages and tax due. Preserve account activation, assigned rate, employee wage detail and accepted reporting evidence apart from TRD withholding and workers compensation fee records.

Reconcile the unemployment contribution to payroll by employee and quarter, retaining any adjustments to excess wages or coverage. Match the payment and any notice to the Workforce Solutions account rather than a revenue account. Include the outstanding balance and amendment history in the year end payroll handoff so the preparer can distinguish contributions from insurance premiums and the separate administration fee.

Sources: New Mexico Workforce Solutions Employer UI Tax, New Mexico Quarterly Wage And Contribution Report

Monthly Financial Reporting

Reconcile bank accounts, customer receipts and unpaid vendor bills before presenting financial statements. Maintain a bridge from accounting income to gross receipts reported, showing deductions and adjustments with document references. A low profit month can still contain substantial gross receipts.

Review customer deposits, supplier credits, loan activity and asset purchases with the owner. Keep a separate compensating tax question list and liability schedule. Explain accounting method differences to the preparer rather than changing the reporting method solely to match a cash balance.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Certificate Mismatch

Hypothetical example: a New Mexico design business receives a customer certificate but cannot connect its type to the service invoiced. The bookkeeper saves the contract, invoice and certificate and flags the receipt for professional deduction review. The certificate is preserved as evidence without treating its mere existence as a completed tax analysis.

Sources: New Mexico NTTC Resources

Year End And Retention Scope

Give the preparer reconciled statements, receipts by location and deduction, NTTC support, compensating tax decisions, wage reconciliations, assets and agency notices. Keep electronic code descriptions with transaction exports so the reviewer can reconstruct the reporting bridge.

NMAC 3.1.5.8 states that the Tax Administration Act sets no fixed retention time, while assessment limitations still apply. FYI-105 recommends maintaining the files for ten years because extended assessments can apply, and distinguishes the ordinary assessment period from longer cases. Treat ten years as agency advice, not an invented universal statutory minimum; federal employment, property and special industry records need their own schedules.

Sources: New Mexico NMAC 3.1.5 Records, New Mexico FYI-105
