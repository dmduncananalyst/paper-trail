Arizona Bookkeeping

Arizona transaction privilege tax is imposed on the vendor's business activity. Accurate activity, location and deduction records matter alongside purchase use tax and separate employer reporting.

TPT Licenses And Business Activity

Arizona describes transaction privilege tax as a tax on the vendor for doing business, rather than an ordinary sales tax on the purchaser. Taxable activities may require licensing, and classification, city and county affect treatment. Keep the legal entity, activity, location and license information together.

A business can have more than one line of work. Preserve contracts and revenue detail that distinguish those activities before the totals reach the tax preparer. The TPT label does not make every receipt taxable, and an income statement category does not automatically establish a statutory business classification.

Sources: Arizona DOR Transaction Privilege Tax

Sales, Deductions And Purchase Use Tax

Maintain gross receipts before payment processing fees, returns and any proposed deduction. Attach certificates and location evidence to the transaction. ADOR's audit resource identifies income classification, exemptions, deductions and purchase tax among its review areas. Keep the supporting reason for treatment instead of only an exempt flag in software.

For vendor bills, distinguish inventory intended for resale from equipment or supplies consumed by the business. Arizona's TPT resource identifies use tax concerns when goods are used or stored in the state without appropriate tax paid. Send uncertain transactions for a determination, preserving the invoice, payment, use location and any certificate already given.

Sources: Arizona DOR Transaction Privilege Tax, Arizona DOR TPT Audit

Payroll And DES Reporting

Arizona DES provides quarterly unemployment tax and wage reporting. Preserve employee identity, gross compensation, work periods, reports, account status changes and payment confirmations. DES warns employers not to simply stop filing when payroll pauses; use the proper account change process and retain its acknowledgment.

Keep income tax withholding obligations and unemployment reporting distinguishable, including separate account evidence and liability reconciliations. A payroll expert should review remote employees, worker status, wage exclusions and benefit deductions. A zero net liability in the general ledger does not establish that required wage detail was filed.

Sources: Arizona DES Reporting Wages And Paying UI Taxes, Arizona DES Employer Record Keeping

Different Retention Rules

ADOR's business recordkeeping page lists a four year baseline for TPT records from the due date or actual filing, whichever is later, and identifies situations requiring longer preservation. DES separately requires employer records for the most recent four calendar years. ADOR also describes different periods for other records, so do not use one number as an automatic deletion rule.

Retain documentation of exemptions, refunds, asset basis, open audits and unfiled periods according to the applicable requirement. A tax professional should reconcile agency and federal obligations before destruction. Export electronic transaction detail and code definitions before replacing software so the records remain understandable.

Sources: Arizona DES Employer Record Keeping, Arizona DOR Business Record Keeping

Monthly Reporting And Income Evidence

As general bookkeeping practice, distinguish loans and owner deposits from customer revenue, reconcile bank/card accounts and review customer and supplier balances. Preserve invoices supporting receivables and credits reducing payables. Do not net unrelated refunds or transfers against sales to make an account easier to reconcile.

Prepare a TPT workpaper that bridges business revenue to activity and location totals for the preparer. Explain differences from financial statements, including timing, deductions under review and tax reimbursements. Maintain a separate payroll liability roll forward showing what was recorded, paid and remains unresolved.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Entity Classification And Income Tax Support

Arizona treats an LLC electing corporate federal taxation as a corporate income tax filer, while a disregarded single member LLC is included with its owner. Corporate return selection also depends on whether activity is wholly within Arizona or spans states. Retain the actual federal election and business locations. TPT reports describe business activity tax and do not replace income tax records or decide the entity classification.

Maintain federal to state adjustment support, asset schedules and receipts by location for the preparer. Partnership and S corporation owners may have an entity tax election to evaluate; preserve the approved election and owner allocation evidence rather than making it through an unexplained accounting entry. Each month reconcile corporate or elected entity estimates and credits by tax year, separate from employee withholding. At year end include capital changes, owner distributions, prior losses and the final federal return so state adjustments can be reviewed.

Sources: Arizona Revenue Corporate Income Tax, Arizona Revenue Partnership Highlights

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Evidence

Arizona DES distinguishes rated employers paying quarterly tax from eligible reimbursable employers billed for benefit charges. TWS supports wage reports, zero wage reports, operational changes and rate notices. Preserve the account type and contribution calculation so a zero rate on a reimbursable wage report is not mistaken for permission to ignore later bills. Reconcile employer tax or reimbursement expense to the applicable notices and payments each month. Include benefit charges, accepted quarterly files and corrections in the annual payroll handoff; do not simply stop reporting when hiring pauses.

Sources: Arizona DES Tax And Wage System

Illustration And Year End Handoff

Hypothetical example: a business performs two different kinds of work under one contract and invoices a customer in another city. The bookkeeper saves the scope, location, billing detail and certificate support, then asks the preparer whether separate TPT classifications or local treatment apply. No tax rate or classification result is assumed.

At year end, assemble reconciliations, activity schedules, asset records, payroll annual totals, returns and unresolved notices. Contractors, retailers and specialized services may require different treatment. Paper Trail can organize and reconcile the evidence; a qualified tax professional or attorney must resolve uncertain tax and employment positions before filing.

Sources: Arizona DOR Transaction Privilege Tax, Arizona DOR TPT Audit
