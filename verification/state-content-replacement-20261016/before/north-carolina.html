North Carolina Bookkeeping

North Carolina contractor and sales files need clear activity evidence, while current employer instructions prescribe a much longer withholding retention schedule.

Sales, Contractors And Transaction Evidence

North Carolina requires retailers, facilitators, real property contractors and consumers to keep records establishing their sales and use tax liability. Retailer records include gross income, gross sales, taxable sales, resale purchases and facilitator transaction information. Keep exemption evidence with the sale it supports.

For construction or repair work, preserve contracts, work descriptions and any applicable capital improvement documentation for professional classification. A contractor label does not settle treatment. Accounts payable should preserve purchase invoices, job use and taxes paid elsewhere so any purchase use tax decision has transaction support.

Sources: North Carolina GS 105-164.22, North Carolina 2026 Sales And Use Bulletins

Employee Certificates And Withholding

The current 2026 employer instructions require North Carolina withholding certificates such as NC-4 and identify payroll reporting duties. Keep signed employee instructions, effective dates, wage registers, amounts withheld, returns and payment confirmations. Work location and residence facts should reach payroll review before state allocations are finalized.

Reconcile periodic payments with the annual NC-3 totals and wage statements. Document corrections in both the payroll system and liability accounts. Unemployment and other employment obligations need their own account checks, and a contractor payment should not be treated as wages or exempt compensation solely from its ledger category.

Sources: North Carolina 2026 Employer Withholding Instructions NC-30

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

North Carolina corporate income and franchise taxes are separate obligations, and an S corporation return can involve franchise tax even when income ordinarily passes to shareholders. Eligible S corporations and partnerships can elect taxed pass through treatment. Preserve federal classification, state source income, ownership, capital information and any year specific election so the preparer can determine the applicable reporting.

Reconcile corporate or entity estimates, franchise payments and owner related credits in separate schedules. Provide the preparer the tax basis adjustments, state allocation detail and member statements alongside the trial balance. A loss in the profit and loss statement should not be used as evidence that no franchise filing or payment applies, and the preparer should resolve any minimum or special rule from the actual facts.

Sources: North Carolina Corporate Income And Franchise Bulletin, North Carolina Taxed Entity Election Notice

Vendor Files And Income Classification

A renovation business can receive progress billings, customer advances and owner loans during the same week. Link each receipt to the contract or financing document and preserve changes in scope. The customer ledger should distinguish amounts billed, collected and still disputed.

For vendor bills, keep purchase approval, job delivery and payment together. Separate equipment retained by the company from materials used on a project. Track supplier credits against the original purchase and ask the preparer to resolve uncertain capitalization or tax treatment using the original documentation.

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

North Carolina DES uses NCSUITS for quarterly tax and wage reports, contributions and wage adjustments. Covered employer reports identify each employee wages, and continuing businesses must submit a zero report for a quarter without wages. Preserve employer registration, rate notices, accepted wage files and contribution payment confirmations.

Compare quarterly wages with payroll and retain the calculation explaining taxable wages, including relevant wages paid in other states for specialist review. Reconcile DES liabilities independently from NCDOR withholding. At year end, give the preparer the report and adjustment history, contribution balance and status changes; the portal retaining report copies does not replace the business retention schedule.

Sources: North Carolina DES Quarterly Tax And Wage Reports

Monthly Close And Tax Reconciliations

Review project receipts and direct spending alongside customer aging and unpaid suppliers. Reconcile cash, cards and debt, then present a profit and loss statement and balance sheet under the agreed accounting basis. Explain unfinished work and customer advances rather than treating bank deposits as the sole revenue measure.

Maintain distinct sales tax, purchase use tax and withholding schedules. Each should connect opening liability, current activity, adjustments and payments to the filed return. Preserve the reason a transaction was reclassified so a future reviewer can reproduce the result.

Hypothetical Renovation Scope Change

Hypothetical example: a North Carolina contractor begins with a repair order and later accepts a broader renovation contract. The bookkeeper retains both scopes, approvals, invoices and project documents and asks the preparer to evaluate the tax treatment of each activity. The example does not classify the entire project from its final invoice title.

Sources: North Carolina GS 105-164.22, North Carolina 2026 Sales And Use Bulletins

Year End And Longer Payroll Preservation

The year end package should contain project revenue detail, sales reconciliations, contractor documents, inventory and asset schedules, debts, annual wage support and unresolved notices. Give the preparer the actual work descriptions behind ambiguous receipts and vendor charges.

The 2026 NC-30 instructions direct retention of withholding records for at least ten years after the last filing of all required reports for the calendar year or the last payment of an amount due for that year, whichever is later. Apply that specific employer instruction rather than a generic three year assumption. Sales, income, federal and property records require separate review, especially where returns or trust tax payments remain unresolved.

Sources: North Carolina 2026 Employer Withholding Instructions NC-30
