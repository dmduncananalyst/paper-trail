Connecticut Bookkeeping

Connecticut business records should connect myconneCT tax filings with separate unemployment and Paid Leave responsibilities. Retention and employee coverage must be checked by program.

DRS Accounts And Business Registration

Connecticut DRS uses myconneCT for registering and managing tax accounts. Keep the legal entity, registration number, locations, activities and authorized contacts in an accessible register. Selling taxable goods or services can require a sales/use permit; a temporary event does not automatically remove that requirement.

Add withholding as a separate tax account when applicable. DRS's new employer information also identifies separate Department of Labor unemployment registration. A business can use the same payroll provider without those registrations becoming interchangeable. Confirm the entity's income or other business tax filings with its tax professional.

Sources: Connecticut DRS Sales Tax Information, Connecticut DRS New Employer Information

Sales And Business Use Purchases

Preserve itemized sales, tax charged, taxable versus exempt detail and delivery evidence. Connecticut taxes specified services as well as many goods, including certain digital products. Keep exemption and resale documents linked to the customer and transaction rather than applying an unsupported exemption to the entire customer account.

Review supplier invoices for purchases used by the business without tax charged. Retain the item description, use location, date and payment for business use tax evaluation. A resale purchase later consumed by the business needs review; a certificate does not make every acquisition permanently exempt. Payable credits should retain a connection to the original invoice.

Sources: Connecticut DRS Sales Tax Information

Withholding, Unemployment And Paid Leave

Maintain withholding certificates, wage registers, filings and payment confirmations, and reconcile DRS withholding separately from Department of Labor unemployment. Actual work/residence circumstances can affect payroll treatment, so preserve them for the specialist. Do not infer correct reporting from the employee's net paycheck.

CT Paid Leave is separately administered. Its employer resource describes covered employer registration, employee deductions and quarterly remittance, even where a provider assists. Keep the account registration, deduction detail, submitted data and contributions distinct from income tax withholding. Eligibility, exemptions and private plans need a specialist's review; this resource supplies no contribution rate.

Sources: Connecticut DRS New Employer Information, Connecticut Paid Leave Employer Responsibilities

Retention By Document Type

DRS's information return instructions require Connecticut information return records for at least four years after the due date. Its Getting Started in Business publication describes six years for seller transaction and resale records. Because an older companion presentation gives a different minimum/recommendation distinction, confirm the current governing rule with DRS before adopting a destruction schedule.

Do not treat a four year information return rule as permission to delete all payroll, leave, asset or sales records. Preserve open audit/dispute evidence longer, and retain source files in a format that can be read after a software change. A dated retention policy should identify each agency and document category.

Sources: Connecticut DRS Wage Statement Filing Requirements, Connecticut DRS Getting Started In Business 2018

Monthly Statements And An Event Example

The monthly accounting routine is general practice: reconcile cash/cards, match receipts to invoices, review unpaid bills and separate borrowing or owner funding from revenue. Roll tax and payroll liabilities forward by program. Show actual remittances and timing differences instead of zeroing an account to make the statement appear complete.

Hypothetical example: a Connecticut business sells products at a weekend event and also invoices for a separately described service. The bookkeeper retains the permit information, sales detail, location, refunds and service contract. The preparer evaluates the taxability of each activity; no rate or blanket service exemption is assumed. Net card deposits are reconciled to gross activity and fees.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Corporation Business Tax And Optional Entity Tax

Connecticut corporation business tax and optional entity tax have separate filing frameworks. DRS explains that qualifying partnerships and S corporations with Connecticut business or source income may elect the PE Tax annually; it is optional rather than an automatic charge on every LLC. Preserve the election, federal classification, member information and the composite return records used with that election.

Keep Connecticut income allocation and state adjustments with the federal business return, and retain member residence and ownership changes. Reconcile any corporation business or elected PE estimates, return payments and credits in distinct monthly schedules. At year end give the preparer the election, prior payments, ownership allocations, assets and federal adjustment evidence. A corporation in a related group may need combined reporting analysis; the bookkeeper should preserve ownership and intercompany records without deciding the group conclusion.

Sources: Connecticut DRS Entity Tax Information, Connecticut DRS Corporation Business Tax Information

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Evidence

Connecticut Labor identifies employers liable for unemployment coverage and directs quarterly tax and wage reports and contributions through ReEmployCT. Maintain its Employer Account Number separately from DRS and Paid Leave registrations. Match employee quarterly wage detail to payroll, then reconcile employer contribution accruals to the report, posted payments and account credits. Retain benefit charge correspondence for review because it can affect future experience rates. The annual package should contain four quarterly files, corrections and the closing unemployment payable, separate from Paid Leave deductions.

Sources: Connecticut Labor Unemployment Insurance Tax, Connecticut Labor Benefit Charge Statements

Year End And Scope Of Advice

Prepare the annual package with reconciliations, receivables/payables, business use purchase reviews, payroll and Paid Leave totals, asset transactions and filed confirmations. DRS provides annual withholding reconciliation and wage statement requirements; compare provider annual totals with the periodic records before submission.

Contractors, retailers, digital sellers and professional firms can need different reviews. This resource does not decide nexus, worker classification, entity elections or private plan eligibility. A qualified tax or payroll professional should resolve those issues. Paper Trail's role is accurate bookkeeping and support for the decision, not a legal conclusion or promise of compliance.

Sources: Connecticut DRS Wage Statement Filing Requirements
