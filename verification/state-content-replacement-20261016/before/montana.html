Montana Bookkeeping

Montana has no general sales tax, but resale paperwork, specialized taxes and employer accounts still require organized evidence.

General Sales Tax And Resale Purchases

Montana does not have a general sales tax. Its Business Registry Resale Certificate can be provided to an outside vendor, but the vendor is not required to accept it and another state may require its own form. Preserve the certificate supplied and the delivery facts with the purchase.

A Montana address does not resolve taxes on transactions delivered or performed elsewhere. As a bookkeeping practice, retain customer destinations, travel and inventory locations for multistate review. Assess specialized activities such as lodging and resort operations with the appropriate professional before deciding which accounts are needed.

Sources: Montana Sales Tax Guidance

Annual Withholding Reconciliation

Montana requires a withholding account for businesses issuing Montana wages or specified wage and withholding statements. Every year an account remains open, an annual MW-3 reconciliation is required. The agency specifies wage statements for Montana wages even where no tax was withheld.

Keep worker instructions, payroll registers, payments and statement submission evidence. Reconcile the annual filing with the wage ledger before sending it to the preparer. Maintain unemployment and insurance records separately from income withholding, and preserve closure evidence if the last payroll ends an account.

Sources: Montana Withholding Accounts, Montana Wage Withholding Returns And Payments

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

Montana C corporations doing business in the state generally file a corporate income tax return, while federal S corporations use Montana pass through reporting. Eligible partnerships and S corporations can elect Montana pass through entity tax annually, with the election applying to affected owners. Preserve federal classification, annual election evidence, owner residence and distributive income schedules.

Reconcile corporate or elected entity tax estimates separately from owner distributions and wage withholding. For an elected entity, retain the designated representative record and calculation of Montana income allocated to affected owners. The year end package should connect payments, carryovers and member information with the return, including any change in ownership or an election affecting resident income.

Sources: Montana Corporate Income Tax, Montana Elective Entity Tax

Vendor And Income Evidence

An outfitting business may buy resale merchandise, rental equipment and supplies for its own use from the same supplier. Describe each item and its purpose in the vendor record. Match the invoice with receiving evidence, any resale certificate and payment; preserve credit memoranda for returned stock.

Separate customer booking deposits from earned receipts under the agreed accounting basis. Keep borrowing, owner advances and refund transactions identifiable in the bank reconciliation. An equipment purchase needs acquisition and financing records so the preparer can determine basis and debt treatment.

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

Montana unemployment quarterly reports must be filed electronically under the current employer guidance. UI eServices supports reporting and contribution payments, while the employer assigned rate and excess wage calculation affect the tax. Preserve accepted wage files, rate notices and payment evidence independently from the revenue withholding reconciliation.

Compare UI gross and taxable wages with employee payroll histories, investigate an unexpected excess wage amount and document corrections by quarter. Reconcile the unemployment liability to agency balances before year end. If the employer uses reimbursement status instead of regular contributions, give the preparer the actual status election and benefit charge statements so the financial statements reflect the correct obligation.

Sources: Montana UI Employer Resources, Montana Reimbursable Employers

Monthly Statements And Seasonal Cash

Review bank balances, cards, booking deposits, supplier bills and equipment debt before preparing monthly statements. A seasonal business should show the amount of cash committed to future bookings and payroll alongside the profit and loss statement. Explain cancellations and refunds with their original reservation references.

Keep a calendar of the actual employer and specialized tax accounts assigned to the business. Match payments with the appropriate liability account and investigate notices before closing the month. A zero sales tax account is not evidence that every other state or local obligation has been evaluated.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Resale Order

Hypothetical example: a Montana outdoor shop orders merchandise from a Washington supplier. The supplier requests Washington documentation instead of accepting the Montana resale certificate. The bookkeeper saves the request, shipment destination and completed paperwork, then refers unresolved treatment to the preparer. The scenario does not promise that the Montana form creates an exemption elsewhere.

Sources: Montana Sales Tax Guidance

Year End And Retention

Provide the preparer with annual sales and booking reconciliations, vendor totals, equipment additions and disposals, debts, MW-3 support and unresolved specialized tax questions. Preserve the descriptions behind resale stock and rental equipment classifications.

Montana DOR recommends keeping ordinary tax records through the limitation period, usually three years after the return was due or filed, whichever is later. Its property guidance calls for records during ownership and at least five years after reporting disposition; loss records should remain through the claim years and at least five years after the final claim. These income and property recommendations do not establish an employer record destruction schedule.

Sources: Montana Tax Record Preservation
