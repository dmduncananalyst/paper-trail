Wisconsin Bookkeeping

Wisconsin requires separate records when taxable retail operations accompany another nontaxable activity. Design revenue categories that preserve that distinction.

Mixed Activity And Exemption Evidence

Wisconsin DOR says a business combining retail sales and other nontaxable activities must maintain separate records for determining sales and use tax. Without the separation, tax may be imposed on all business receipts.

For exempt sales, its guidance identifies purchaser name and address, sale date, item, exemption amount and reason. Preserve a completed certificate where applicable and connect it to the relevant sales. A generic exempt sales total cannot explain an individual transaction.

Sources: Wisconsin Sales And Use Common Questions

Vendor Purchases And Use Tax

DOR directs people with regular use tax obligations from untaxed purchases to obtain the relevant certificate. Keep a review of supplier invoices with item description, use location, tax charged and the preparer's determination.

As general accounts payable practice, match bills to receipt and approval evidence. Separate beginning and ending inventory, operating purchases and assets. Preserve vendor credits and proof of tax paid so refunds or corrections can be evaluated without reconstructing the purchase later.

Sources: Wisconsin Sales And Use Common Questions

Employer Account Records

Wisconsin maintains a separate withholding program and publishes wage statement and information return resources. Keep the employer account notices, employee authorizations, wage detail and filing acknowledgments with payroll records.

Reconcile each payroll liability to the register and remittance history. Compare annual wage statement totals to year to date payroll before submission and retain an explanation for corrections. Confirm current filing instructions with the payroll professional.

Sources: Wisconsin Withholding Tax

Monthly Income And Reporting

General close work should reconcile bank and card balances, customer receivables, unpaid bills and payroll clearing. Produce a profit and loss statement and balance sheet with revenue classifications that agree to the taxable and nontaxable activity schedules.

Identify owner transfers, loan proceeds and customer collections separately. Review margins by activity when shared expenses are allocated, retaining the allocation method so a reporting change is explainable to the owner and preparer.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Workshop And Retail Counter

Hypothetical example: a Wisconsin business has a retail counter and a separate service activity believed to be nontaxable. The bookkeeper preserves contracts and distinct sales records and asks the preparer to confirm treatment. Posting all deposits to service revenue would erase the transaction distinction.

Sources: Wisconsin Sales And Use Common Questions

DWD Contributions And Entity Elections

Wisconsin unemployment is administered through DWD rather than the DOR withholding account. Its employer handbook requires quarterly reporting even in quarters without payroll. Wage detail accompanies the contribution tax report. Save both reports, the rate notice and payment and reconcile wages, employer unemployment expense and the closing liability. Keep correction support with the quarter affected.

DOR's corporation and partnership resources distinguish tax option S corporation and partnership entity level elections, apportionment and owner withholding resources. Preserve the current election, owner residency and allocations, state income modifications and other state tax payments for the preparer. Match approved entity payments to the correct year and keep distributions and guaranteed payments distinguishable so the financial statements support the chosen reporting framework.

Sources: Wisconsin DWD Employer Reporting Handbook, Wisconsin DWD Wage Reporting, Wisconsin Corporation And Partnership Tax

Year End And Record Retention

Supply the activity bridge, exemption file, purchase review, payroll annual support, equipment invoices and debt records with the final reconciled accounts.

Wisconsin DOR says sales and use records must remain for the four year period open to audit, longer where an agreement extends it, and until a contested determination is finally resolved. Its individual income guidance separately identifies longer basis support for property and loss carryforwards. Apply the relevant category rather than discarding everything after four years.

Sources: Wisconsin Sales And Use Common Questions, Wisconsin Individual Income Tax Keeping Records
