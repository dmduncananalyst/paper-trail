Mississippi Bookkeeping

Mississippi records must preserve each business location and the evidence supporting exempt sales; current agency retention statements need careful separation.

Location Records And Exempt Sales

Mississippi DOR calls for a separate set of sales records for each business location. Exempt sales need documentation, and sales involving permits or material purchase certificates require the relevant transaction and purchaser information. Keep the actual product description when an exemption depends on the item.

As an accounts payable control, save supplier invoices with purchase approvals, payment and the job or location receiving the items. Preserve any permit evidence relied upon and send equipment or supply purchases without tax for review. A permit number alone does not explain how the purchased item was used.

Sources: Mississippi Record Keeping And Document Retention

Payroll And Employer Registration

The agency business FAQ describes withholding registration for covered businesses paying Mississippi residents or nonresidents performing services in Mississippi. Preserve employee identity, home address, employment periods, wage payments and withholding records, then give multistate facts to a payroll professional.

Reconcile employee deductions and employer costs separately. Match the payroll register with payment confirmations and wage statements, and retain amended reports with an explanation. A vendor agreement or information return does not decide whether a worker is an employee.

Sources: Mississippi Business Tax FAQ

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

Mississippi administers corporate income and franchise taxes and separate pass through entity returns. Applicability depends on the entity and tax year, so do not assume every legal business form uses only an owner return. Preserve federal classification, state source activity, capital balances and any entity tax election evidence for the preparer.

Use separate schedules for income tax estimates, franchise amounts and member related payments. The franchise phaseout makes the year attached to a balance especially important; a future repeal does not erase an older liability. Include federal and Mississippi returns, capital and ownership changes, reconciled estimates and unresolved notices in the year end handoff.

Sources: Mississippi Corporate Income And Franchise Taxes, Mississippi 2025 Corporate Income And Franchise Tax Instructions

Income, Inventory And Expense Files

DOR identifies records supporting income, deductions and credits, including state and federal returns and their schedules. Keep sales, returns and damaged inventory adjustments linked to source documents. A physical stock count should explain quantity differences from the ledger.

For management purposes, classify owner withdrawals and loan repayments separately from operating expenses. Maintain asset invoices with repair and improvement history. Supplier credits and customer bad debts should have a documented reason instead of being cleared through a general adjustment account.

Sources: Mississippi Record Keeping And Document Retention

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

Mississippi Department of Employment Security requires quarterly wage reports and unemployment taxes for liable employers. Save the MDES account details, assigned rate, employee wage report and payment confirmation separately from Department of Revenue withholding records. Keep a correction linked to the quarter in which the wages belonged.

Compare gross wages in the submitted report with payroll, explain differences in taxable wages and reconcile contributions to payments and agency credits. For seasonal location changes, retain which workers and payroll periods are included in each filing. The year end payroll package should identify unpaid MDES balances and amended quarters alongside the four regular reports.

Sources: Mississippi MDES Quarterly Reporting

Monthly Reporting Across Locations

Reconcile each location sales report to deposits and interlocation transfers before combining the financial statements. A transfer of inventory should preserve the sending and receiving location so it is not mistaken for an external sale or an unexplained shrinkage adjustment.

Review cash, cards, customer aging and unpaid bills, then present consolidated profit and loss and balance sheet results with location exceptions. Maintain separate schedules for sales tax, purchase tax review and payroll liabilities. Investigate agency notices against the exact tax period rather than against the current month balance.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Two Store Transfer

Hypothetical example: a Mississippi hardware business moves stock between two stores and later sells some items to a customer claiming an exemption. The bookkeeper preserves the transfer record separately from the customer invoice and links the customer exemption documentation to the actual sale. The example does not determine eligibility for any exemption.

Sources: Mississippi Record Keeping And Document Retention

Year End And Conflicting Retention Statements

Give the preparer location sales totals, inventory count adjustments, exemption evidence, asset schedules, payroll support and filed returns. Identify material purchase certificates and unresolved use tax questions separately so they receive substantive review.

The general DOR recordkeeping page states at least three years for business records, while its business FAQ specifies a minimum four year period in the sales record answer and at least three years in its withholding answer. Preserve sales records for at least four years while the discrepancy is resolved with DOR or a qualified adviser; do not present the shorter general statement as a safe sales destruction date. Other holds and record categories can require longer.

Sources: Mississippi Record Keeping And Document Retention, Mississippi Business Tax FAQ
