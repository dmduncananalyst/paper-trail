Maine Bookkeeping

Maine businesses need a clear boundary between legacy service provider tax records and sales tax activity beginning in 2026.

The 2026 Service Tax Transition

Maine repealed the Service Provider Tax effective January 1, 2026 and moved the affected services into sales and use tax. The official bulletin identifies fabrication, telecommunications and related services among the affected activities. Retain the service description and transaction date so the preparer can distinguish a current sale from an amendment to a legacy period.

As a practical accounting control, archive the old tax mapping before changing invoice settings. A business migrating fabrication receipts should be able to reproduce its former returns and explain why current invoices use a different tax account. Do not assume an old agency navigation label establishes current tax treatment.

Sources: Maine 2025 Legislative Bulletin 115

Sales And Purchase Documentation

Maine sales records include purchase price information for property and taxable services used in Maine. Attach vendor invoices, shipping evidence and exemption support to the relevant period. A resale purchase and a tool consumed in the business belong on different review paths.

For accounts payable, record supplier credits against the original bill and preserve the reason for the adjustment. Keep delivery charges and separately stated taxes visible. A card charge proves payment but usually does not describe the item well enough to support a tax classification.

Sources: Maine Sales And Use Tax Business Publication December 2025

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

Maine administers corporate income tax separately from sales and payroll taxes. A partnership or S corporation with Maine source income and nonresident members can also have member withholding and return obligations. The 2026 FAQ explicitly separates nonresident member withholding from employee withholding. Preserve entity classification, ownership percentages, member residence and Maine income allocation evidence for preparer review.

Keep member tax payments, distributions and employee payroll on separate schedules. Reconcile amounts actually withheld by the entity to member statements and return copies; a payment by a member is not automatically an entity payment. The year end handoff should include member affidavits, changes in ownership and any composite filing decision as well as the corporate or owner income tax reconciliation that applies.

Sources: Maine Business Tax Types, Maine Member Withholding FAQ

Seasonal Payroll And Employer Accounts

An active Maine withholding account requires quarterly returns even when there were no employees or no withholding. The current agency FAQ specifically says there is no seasonal withholding filing frequency. Maintain a calendar linked to the active account rather than assuming a winter shutdown ends reporting.

Reconcile approved hours, wages, deductions, payments and filed payroll totals. As a general control, retain the payroll provider export and payment acknowledgment together; keep unemployment and other employer obligations on distinct schedules for specialist review.

Sources: Maine Income Tax Withholding FAQ

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

Maine employer unemployment contributions are reported through the Department of Labor workflow, with quarterly ME UC-1 and employee wage detail. This is separate from Maine Revenue Services income withholding even when both use the same payroll register. Preserve unemployment registration, contribution notices, quarterly wage submissions and payment evidence.

For a seasonal employer, compare each quarter wage report with actual payroll activity and retain the reason for any correction. Reconcile contributions independently from withholding, keeping any agency credit assigned to the correct period. Give the preparer all four quarter reports, outstanding adjustments and account status at year end rather than relying on the annual wage statement alone.

Sources: Maine Employer Unemployment Services

Monthly Reports And Income Evidence

A seasonal business should compare current profit and loss results with both the prior month and the corresponding operating season. Reconcile cash, cards, deposits and loans before explaining the results. Customer advance payments and owner funding should each retain their original description.

Review unpaid vendor bills and the purchase tax question list during the close. An offseason cash balance may include customer deposits needed for next season, so explain those balances beside the balance sheet instead of treating every bank dollar as available profit.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Fabrication Shop

Hypothetical example: a Maine metal shop completes a fabricated railing in 2026 and later discovers an invoice correction relating to a 2025 project. The bookkeeper separates the two transaction histories, saves the signed orders and completion evidence, and gives the preparer both tax account mappings. The example establishes no rate, exemption or reporting period conclusion.

Sources: Maine 2025 Legislative Bulletin 115

Year End And Retention

Deliver annual sales totals by activity, legacy corrections, inventory counts, asset invoices, payroll reconciliations and unresolved purchase questions to the tax preparer. Include a written explanation of any conversion between accounting systems so beginning balances can be traced.

The current Maine sales publication requires six year retention and specifically preserves legacy Service Provider Tax records for six years after generation despite repeal. Title 36 section 135 separately ties income tax records under Part 8 to applicable federal rules; it does not give every record one six year destruction date. Preserve records subject to an audit or other hold and obtain a separate employer retention schedule.

Sources: Maine Sales And Use Tax Business Publication December 2025, Maine Title 36 Section 135
