Illinois Bookkeeping

Illinois bookkeeping requires transaction location evidence, detailed sales and purchase support, daily withholding records and separate unemployment reports even when MyTax Illinois provides shared access.

Retail Records And Transaction Locations

Illinois Publication 113 requires retailers to preserve sales, purchases, inventory changes, receivables, payables and return workpapers. It also emphasizes location support for destination transactions. Retain customer delivery addresses and shipment origins where applicable, with gross sales detail and tax. An order assigned only to a postal code can leave the actual destination unresolved.

Link exemption support to individual sales, leases or rentals, including the purchaser, transaction character, date and receipts. For supplier bills, record whether purchases are stock, equipment or supplies and preserve the tax charged. Separate questionable untaxed purchases for use tax review. Inventory records should explain transfers, shrinkage and goods removed from stock.

Sources: Illinois Revenue Publication 113 Recordkeeping

Withholding Dates And Unemployment

Form IL-941 instructions require Illinois withholding to be reported on the date payroll or compensation was paid, rather than the later tax deposit date. Preserve pay dates and tax by payroll run. Schedule P and any applicable credit schedule are part of the return support. Reconcile withheld amounts independently from gross wages and employer payroll costs.

IDES administers unemployment reports separately from Revenue withholding. Its resources cover quarterly contributions and additional monthly wage reporting for qualifying employers. Maintain the IDES account, rate notices, wage files and acknowledgments. MyTax Illinois access can cover both programs, but a contribution payment and a monthly wage report serve different purposes.

Sources: Illinois Revenue IL-941 Instructions, Illinois IDES Employer Tax Information

Accounts Payable And Income Evidence

For management accounting, reconcile vendor statements to open bills before payment. Attach purchase approvals, delivery receipts, credit memos and settlement evidence. Equipment leases and installment contracts should retain their original agreements; a bank debit alone may combine principal, interest, insurance and fees.

Separate customer receipts, advance payments, borrowing and owner contributions. Preserve sales channel reports before processing fees, and connect refunds to the original transactions. For businesses that both sell goods and provide services, descriptions of what was transferred and where it was delivered give the preparer better evidence than one undifferentiated revenue account.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Monthly Statements And Closing Controls

Recommended monthly controls include bank and card reconciliations, processor settlement checks, receivable and payable aging, and review of inventory and asset activity. Present a profit and loss statement and balance sheet with unresolved items identified. Large receivables or seasonal stock purchases may also justify a short cash forecast.

Maintain separate schedules for sales/use tax, income tax withholding and unemployment contributions. Match the return periods, payment dates and agency credits to the ledger. Retain filed returns and supporting exports so an amended filing can be traced to the original calculation. Do not clear an agency balance merely because a payroll service has withdrawn cash.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Annual Handoff And Retention

Prepare the tax handoff with the annual trial balance, reconciliations, location based sales summaries, exemption evidence, purchase tax review, payroll returns, wage statements and IDES reports. Add physical inventory, asset changes, loan balances and owner transactions. Compare annual statement totals with the periodic withholding records and explain every correction.

Publication 113 generally requires receipts documentation for three and one half years after the original or amended return is filed. Records related to an issued tax liability or final notice must remain until the liability is finalized or discharged. The publication also sets access, readability, English language and Illinois storage requirements unless written permission allows another location. Confirm how those conditions apply to electronic storage.

Sources: Illinois Revenue Publication 113 Recordkeeping, Illinois Revenue IL-941 Instructions

Income, Replacement And Elected Entity Taxes

Illinois business taxation includes replacement tax as well as corporate income tax. Publication 129 explains that partnerships and S corporations can have replacement tax on Illinois income even when ordinary income passes to their owners. It also distinguishes an optional PTE tax election from withholding for nonresident members. Retain the entity classification, election and member exemption documents rather than treating all state income payments as one owner distribution.

Maintain state adjustments, receipts sourcing, member allocations and federal return support. Reconcile replacement tax, applicable corporate income tax and elected entity or owner withholding payments separately during the monthly close. At year end give the preparer the payment history, credit carryforwards, ownership changes and Illinois member schedules. Compare credits reported to owners with the entity workpapers; a state payment can support an owner credit without representing employee withholding or satisfying replacement tax.

Sources: Illinois Revenue Publication 129 Entity Information

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Evidence

IDES distinguishes quarterly contribution reports, which calculate unemployment contributions, from additional monthly wage reports that do not calculate or remit contributions. Preserve the employer reporting determination and the applicable wage files so a monthly upload is not mistaken for the quarterly tax return. Reconcile employer contribution expense, agency credits and payments to the quarterly liability. At year end provide all required reports and amendments with annual payroll totals, while keeping the monthly information submissions separately identifiable.

Sources: Illinois IDES Monthly Wage Reporting

A Rockford Delivery Example

Hypothetical example: a Rockford retailer delivers one order to a customer facility and allows another customer to collect an order. The bookkeeper retains exact delivery evidence for the first and pickup evidence for the second, with the order and gross payment detail. The preparer determines sourcing. Payroll issued at month end is recorded by pay date even if the tax deposit settles the following month.
