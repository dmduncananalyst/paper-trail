Maryland Bookkeeping

Maryland sales records and employer withholding records have different preservation periods, and payroll needs accurate residence information.

Sales And Use Tax Evidence

Maryland requires records pertaining to sales and purchases, including information needed to determine sales and use tax. Organize original sales, exempt sales and purchase invoices so a reviewer can trace return totals to transactions. Keep a certificate with the customer relationship and preserve the evidence supporting its use.

As an accounts payable practice, flag equipment and supply invoices without tax, preserve their use location and send the facts for use tax review. Avoid deciding tax solely from a vendor name or a payment description.

Sources: Maryland Business Tax Tip 2 Sales And Use Records

Employer Withholding And Local Information

The Maryland employer publication covers state and local withholding and requires wage dates and amounts, taxes withheld, employee identity, employment periods and exemption certificates. Preserve residence changes with their effective dates so the payroll professional can apply the appropriate instructions.

The Comptroller requires withheld sums to appear in a ledger account identifying the state tax. Reconcile that account with payroll registers, payments and return acknowledgments. The annual MW508 reconciliation needs wage and withholding detail, not simply the annual cash withdrawn by the payroll provider.

Sources: Maryland 2026 Employer Withholding Publication, Maryland Employer Withholding

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

Maryland corporation filing obligations depend on tax classification and state activity; the Comptroller identifies Form 500 for applicable corporations and separate returns for partnerships and S corporations. Pass through entities can have nonresident member tax and an elective entity tax route. Preserve federal classification, member residence, ownership changes, state source income and any signed election documentation for professional evaluation.

Keep entity estimates and nonresident member payments distinct from payroll withholding and owner distributions. The preparer needs federal return schedules, Maryland modifications, multistate activity support and member credit records to explain the tax computation. Reconcile each payment to its entity, tax year and return before year end so an owner credit is not omitted or counted twice.

Sources: Maryland Business Income Tax Information, Maryland Elective Entity Filing

Vendor Bills And Income Classification

A Maryland professional practice can receive insurer settlements, direct customer payments, loan proceeds and owner contributions in one account. Identify the origin of each deposit before posting it. Preserve adjustment explanations when a settlement differs from the customer invoice.

Match vendor bills to approval, receipt and payment. Track equipment financing separately from the equipment cost, and separate principal from financing charges in the debt schedule. These are general bookkeeping practices; tax deductibility and capitalization decisions remain with the preparer.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

Maryland requires quarterly unemployment contribution reports showing gross wages and applicable unemployment tax payments. BEACON provides employer balances, rate information and reporting access through the Department of Labor. Preserve the unemployment account number, assigned rate, employee wage submission and payment acknowledgment independently from the Comptroller withholding records.

Compare reported gross wages and unemployment taxable wages with the payroll export and document exclusions or annual wage base differences for payroll review. Match payments and adjustments to the liability rollforward, and investigate notices against the quarter concerned. The year end package should include BEACON balance evidence, corrected reports and unresolved contribution items.

Sources: Maryland Employer BEACON Instructions

Monthly Reconciliation And Reporting

Close the bank, card, customer and vendor ledgers before delivering a profit and loss statement and balance sheet. Investigate old credits and outstanding checks with the owner. A clean bank reconciliation alone does not establish that customer balances or tax liabilities are correct.

For payroll, compare quarterly wages with year to date records after each close and annotate residence changes affecting local withholding. Maintain a notice log so a payment posted under the wrong account does not become a recurring unexplained adjustment.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Employee Move

Hypothetical example: a Maryland design studio employee changes residence during the year. The bookkeeper obtains the updated withholding certificate and effective date, sends the change through payroll review and preserves the adjustment report. The studio then reconciles the annual wage record to the corrected payroll totals. The example assumes no local rate or retroactive adjustment entitlement.

Sources: Maryland 2026 Employer Withholding Publication

Year End And Record Retention

Prepare the tax handoff with reconciled statements, receivable adjustments, vendor totals, asset and debt schedules, sales returns and the annual payroll reconciliation. Give the preparer a separate list of uncertain exemptions and expenses with missing support.

Maryland Business Tax Tip 2 states four years for sales and purchase records. The 2026 employer publication states at least three years after the related tax became due or was paid, whichever is later, for withholding records. Those are distinct rules; broader employment, federal and property basis obligations may require longer preservation.

Sources: Maryland Business Tax Tip 2 Sales And Use Records, Maryland 2026 Employer Withholding Publication
