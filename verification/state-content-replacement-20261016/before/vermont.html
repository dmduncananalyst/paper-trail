Vermont Bookkeeping

Vermont hospitality records should distinguish menu sales, service charges, tips and employee distributions. These details feed both tax review and payroll reconciliation.

Meals, Rooms And Local Activity

Vermont requires businesses selling taxable meals or rooms to collect and remit Meals and Rooms Tax and obtain the relevant license. Keep receipts separated by meals, alcohol, rooms and other activity rather than using one hospitality revenue total.

The agency identifies local option taxes and separately administered city taxes in Burlington and Rutland. Preserve the business and delivery locations needed for a local review. Do not copy a neighboring property's tax setup without checking the actual location.

Sources: Vermont Meals And Rooms Getting Started

Service Charges And Payroll Evidence

Vermont's compliance guidance distinguishes voluntary customer tips from charges imposed by the operator and emphasizes the treatment of amounts retained by the operator. Retain the customer bill, stated charge and evidence of distribution to employees for preparer review.

Employer registration instructions also address Vermont income tax withholding on wages for employees working in Vermont. Reconcile tip and service charge distributions to payroll, employee withholding and cash payments so the same receipt is not counted as both owner revenue and employee money without explanation.

Sources: Vermont Compliance Corner Archive, Vermont Business Registration Instructions

Vendor Purchases And Income Records

As general accounting practice, connect ingredient, linen and equipment invoices to receiving records and payment. Keep purchases used in the business distinct from merchandise for resale and flag invoices with uncertain tax treatment.

Reconcile reservation deposits, cancellations, refunds and final charges to guest accounts. Identify loans and owner contributions separately from hospitality income. Keep the original booking evidence when a platform settlement deducts fees.

Monthly Hospitality Financial Reports

Complete bank and card reconciliations and compare sales reports, platform payouts and cash counted. Review unpaid vendor bills and the payroll clearing account before issuing the profit and loss statement and balance sheet.

Add a service charge reconciliation showing collections, employee distributions, retained amounts and unresolved differences. Present tax liabilities by applicable program with return and payment references. This is general bookkeeping practice for a usable monthly close.

Hypothetical Vermont Event Meal

Hypothetical example: a Vermont inn adds a service charge to a banquet bill and distributes part of it to staff. The bookkeeper retains the itemized bill and distribution support and asks the preparer to determine the tax treatment of each part. Labeling the whole amount a tip would discard relevant facts.

Sources: Vermont Compliance Corner Archive

Employer Quarterly Reports And Entity Returns

Vermont Labor's current employer reporting page directs employers and payroll service providers to the new UI e-Services system for quarterly reports. Keep the submitted employee wage detail, tax calculation, assigned rate and payment evidence, and compare the employer unemployment expense and payable with those records. Preserve adjustments with the original quarter instead of silently reducing a later period's wages.

Vermont's business entity income tax page covers S corporations, partnerships and relevant LLCs, while its updated form FAQ distinguishes C corporations filing CO-411. It also separates owner and entity reporting and identifies resident and nonresident form questions. Provide the preparer ownership and residency, Vermont activity, income and expense adjustments and capital distributions. Reconcile entity payments and any nonresident owner amounts separately from meals tax and employee withholding.

Sources: Vermont Employer Quarterly Reporting, Vermont Business Entity Income Tax, Vermont Business Income Form FAQs

Year End And Retention

Prepare a handoff containing hospitality receipts, service charge schedules, vendor purchases, wage reports, local tax review, equipment records and filed return copies. Explain outstanding customer deposits and payroll liabilities.

Vermont's Meals and Rooms regulation requires covered records for three years from the required filing date or actual filing date, whichever is later, and warns that assessment exceptions can reach further. Sales and use statute Section 9709 separately requires three years and allows the Commissioner to require longer. Confirm payroll and asset preservation separately.

Sources: Vermont Meals And Rooms Tax Regulations, Vermont Statutes Section 9709
