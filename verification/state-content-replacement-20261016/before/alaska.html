Alaska Bookkeeping

Alaska has no state sales tax, but municipal sales and use tax can still affect a business. Local transaction evidence and state employer records need separate accounting trails.

Local Jurisdiction Records

Alaska's Office of the State Assessor says the state does not levy sales tax, while some cities and boroughs do. Municipalities set their particular rules and forms, and both city and borough taxes can apply. Keep each business location and customer delivery location visible for review rather than labeling every Alaska sale tax free.

Build a jurisdiction register with the relevant municipality, account correspondence, assigned filing information and effective dates of changes. State summaries can describe earlier years; confirm the actual municipal rules before filing. This resource does not determine a remote seller's obligations or substitute one local ordinance for another.

Sources: Alaska DCRA Sales Tax Information

Sales, Exemptions And Purchases

Preserve receipts, item descriptions, service dates, delivery evidence, tax charged, customer refunds and any municipal exemption documents. A settlement deposit cannot demonstrate whether local tax was collected correctly. Local exemptions and caps should be evaluated from the applicable ordinance, not imported from another Alaska community.

For accounts payable, retain invoices and where the purchased item is stored or used. Municipal use tax may exist where sales tax is imposed. If another jurisdiction's tax was paid, preserve that receipt for the adviser to evaluate any credit. Do not create a statewide use tax payable simply because the accounting software offers one.

Sources: Alaska DCRA Sales Tax Information

Employer Accounts And Seasonal Payroll

The Department of Labor and Workforce Development provides a separate unemployment tax account and employer reporting system. Its employer handbook identifies registration, accurate payroll records, quarterly reports, taxes and business change reporting as employer responsibilities. Keep rate notices and confirmations with the wage detail supporting each report.

Seasonal work makes clean payroll history especially useful. Retain hire and separation dates, actual work locations, payment periods, expense reimbursements and the nature of work. Ask a specialist about maritime, fishing, staffing and other special employment arrangements rather than assuming every person paid receives identical treatment.

Sources: Alaska Labor Employer Tax Handbook, Alaska Labor Unemployment Insurance

Revenue, Vendor Bills And Monthly Statements

General accounting practice still applies when no state sales tax exists. Separate operating revenue from financing, owner transfers and deposits for future work. Match supplier bills and credits to payments, reconcile cards and banks, and investigate stale customer balances. Preserve freight and delivery charges with the purchase they support.

Present a consistent income statement and balance sheet, with local tax liabilities separated by municipality and payroll liabilities separated by program. A quiet winter month may still involve unpaid bills or annual expenses. Cash planning should identify those commitments without artificially smoothing real seasonal activity.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Illustration: A Seasonal Equipment Rental

Hypothetical example: an Alaska business rents equipment to customers in more than one municipality. The bookkeeper saves the rental agreement, dates, delivery and return location, deposit, refund and each charge. The local tax preparer can then evaluate the actual jurisdictions and treatment; the example does not assume that all rentals are taxable.

A damaged item is repaired by a vendor and paid with the company card. Keep the repair invoice and connect any customer damage reimbursement to the original rental without hiding either amount inside the net processor deposit. These linked records are useful even where a local tax does not apply.

Sources: Alaska DCRA Sales Tax Information

Corporate Income And Entity Reporting

Alaska has a corporate net income tax program even though it has no individual income tax. Official legislative material identifies the corporate income tax under AS 43.20.011, and Revenue maintains separate corporate and partnership reporting programs. Do not infer from an owner tax return, an LLC name or the absence of a personal state tax that the business has no state filing duty. Ask the preparer to document the entity classification and Alaska activity that determine the actual return.

Keep the federal business return, Alaska operations and receipt detail, ownership interests, asset and payroll locations, and prior state calculations. Reconcile any corporate estimates, return payments and carryforwards by tax year during the monthly close, independently of local sales and employment tax. Include these schedules and federal adjustments in the annual handoff. Corporate group, partnership ownership and specialized industry questions need current return instructions; the corporate forms viewer was not retrievable during this review, so no detailed exemption or filing threshold is asserted.

Sources: Alaska Legislature Corporate Net Income Tax Summary, Alaska Revenue Tax Types

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Evidence

The Alaska employer handbook requires quarterly contribution reporting with employee wage detail and explains employee contributions as well as employer tax. Preserve gross wages, hours and employment changes with the quarterly calculation. Reconcile amounts deducted from workers separately from employer expense, then match the combined remittance to the accepted report and bank payment. At year end, compare quarterly wage totals and employee contribution statements with payroll records. Seasonal activity needs accurate quarter allocation rather than shifting all wages into the busiest quarter.

Sources: Alaska Labor Employment Security Tax Employer Handbook

Year End And Retention Decisions

Provide the tax professional with reconciled accounts, equipment purchases and disposals, seasonal payroll reports, local returns, ownership documents and unresolved municipality notices. Review applicable state entity taxes and industry obligations from the actual entity and activity rather than extrapolating from the absence of a statewide sales tax.

Alaska Labor requires accounting and payroll records to be preserved for at least five years. The employer handbook specifies pay periods, wages, hours, wage computation, special payments and employment changes among employee details. Apply that employer requirement separately from municipality sales records and property basis documents. Preserve pending audit and claim evidence and keep readable source detail when changing systems. Local sales retention requires the applicable municipality rule.

Sources: Alaska Labor Employer Tax Handbook, Alaska Labor Unemployment Insurance, Alaska Labor Employment Security Tax Employer Handbook
