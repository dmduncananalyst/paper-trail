Minnesota Bookkeeping

Minnesota employer records include service locations and daily hours, while sales reporting needs state and local tax detail.

Sales And Local Tax Detail

The Minnesota sales and use tax publication distinguishes registration, local taxes, taxable purchases and reporting. Keep customer delivery evidence, gross sales, refunds and exemption support in a form that can be reconciled to the return. Preserve original and amended filing workpapers together.

For accounts payable, separate purchases consumed by the business from goods intended for resale. Save the vendor invoice and any tax charged elsewhere, and record where the purchase is used. Route uncertain use tax treatment to the preparer before approving the reporting schedule.

Sources: Minnesota Sales And Use Tax Business Publication

Employee Forms And Service Records

Minnesota employers need federal and Minnesota withholding certificates; the new employer publication identifies Form W-4MN. Retain replacements with their effective dates rather than overwriting the earlier employee instructions. Reconcile wages and deductions to the liability accounts and payment confirmations.

The unemployment record rule requires individual service dates, daily hours, location, wages paid and unpaid, pay rate, residence and certain reimbursements. For services across states, it also identifies base of operations, direction and control, and service dates by state. Capture these facts as work occurs so payroll review does not depend on year end recollection.

Sources: Minnesota New Employer Publication, Minnesota Rule 3315.1010

Business Income And Entity Tax Records

Minnesota corporate franchise tax and entity filing treatment require review of the federal classification and Minnesota activity. Certain corporations, partnerships and S corporations can also owe a minimum fee based on Minnesota property, payroll and sales or receipts. Preserve these factors even when the business shows little net income; the fee question uses a different measure from accounting profit.

Keep the trial balance, federal income reconciliation, Minnesota activity schedules and all estimated payments available for the preparer. Where owner level or elective entity treatment applies, include member ownership and residence records and the actual election evidence. At year end, reconcile tax and minimum fee payments by period and document any credit carried to the next year.

Sources: Minnesota 2025 Corporation Franchise Tax Instructions Revised July 2026, Minnesota Minimum Fee

Income And Vendor Review

A field service company can collect deposits for scheduled work while receiving reimbursements and loan funds in the same bank account. Label each source and maintain the job reference. An unexplained deposit should remain on the owner review list until supported.

Payable records should show which job received materials and whether an item became company equipment. Retain receiving evidence, supplier returns and financing agreements. These practical controls help the preparer distinguish an operating cost from an asset or balance sheet item.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Reports

Minnesota employers with employees covered by unemployment insurance or Paid Leave must register for an employer account. The employer reporting resources distinguish quarterly UI wage detail, payment and hours information. Preserve covered employee wages and hours, registration and rate information, submitted reports and confirmations for the applicable programs.

Keep unemployment contributions separately identifiable from Paid Leave premiums and wage withholding in the payroll ledger. Compare accepted wage detail with payroll hours and wages and explain amendments. Provide the preparer a year end reconciliation of each liability and payments, together with agency balances and any coverage questions; sharing employer account access does not make the liabilities interchangeable.

Sources: Minnesota Employer UI And Paid Leave Accounts

Monthly Reports And Wage Reconciliation

Review revenue and direct spending by completed job, with outstanding deposits and unbilled work explained under the chosen accounting basis. Reconcile the bank, cards, customer ledger and supplier balances before issuing the profit and loss statement and balance sheet.

Compare payroll work location records with the payroll system allocation and investigate differences promptly. Maintain separate schedules for income withholding, unemployment and other employer programs that apply. Return acknowledgments should identify both the reporting period and tax type.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Hypothetical Mobile Repair Crew

Hypothetical example: a Minnesota repair crew spends part of a week servicing equipment across the state border. The bookkeeper preserves the daily job tickets, hours, location, travel reimbursements and information about where the work is directed. Payroll specialists receive the facts before deciding state allocation; the scenario assumes no reciprocity or automatic exemption.

Sources: Minnesota Rule 3315.1010

Year End And Record Preservation

Provide the preparer with job revenue detail, inventory adjustments, equipment additions, debt balances, wage reconciliations and sales return history. Keep an exception schedule for missing certificates and uncertain purchase taxes, including the person responsible for resolution.

Minnesota Rule 3315.1010 requires the covered employer service records for at least four years in addition to the current calendar year. Apply that specific unemployment record rule without shortening it to four years from an individual paycheck. Sales, income tax, paid leave and property records require their own retention analysis; an amendment deadline is not itself a destruction rule.

Sources: Minnesota Rule 3315.1010
