Iowa Bookkeeping

Iowa bookkeeping should preserve taxable goods, digital products and service detail, distinguish purchase use tax, and reconcile Revenue filings in GovConnectIowa separately from myIowaUI wage reports.

Sales, Digital Products And Purchase Use Tax

Iowa Revenue explains that sales and use tax can apply to tangible goods, specified digital products and taxable services. The reporting file should describe what was sold and where delivery or first use occurred. Preserve gross sales, returns, tax collected and supported exclusions; do not presume every service or online subscription has the same treatment.

Iowa directs businesses to review purchases for tax the vendor did not collect. Keep the invoice, item or service description, use location, tax charged and review conclusion. Retain a workpaper connecting taxable purchases to the use tax portion of the return. Goods for resale, office equipment and purchased services should remain distinguishable in the vendor file.

Sources: Iowa Revenue Sales And Use Tax Resource

Withholding And Unemployment Accounts

Revenue withholding uses periodic returns and payments based on the employer filing frequency. Its resources distinguish employee withholding certificates, tax payment records and wage statement reporting. Maintain payroll detail, account notices and filing confirmations so the amount withheld can be compared with actual remittances and statements.

Iowa Workforce Development operates myIowaUI separately from GovConnectIowa. Liable employers submit quarterly wage detail and employer payments electronically. Preserve employee wages by quarter, contribution calculations and account correspondence. Do not use the Revenue withholding account as a substitute for UI registration or assume a payroll debit proves that both agencies received reports.

Sources: Iowa Revenue Withholding Tax Information, Iowa Workforce Development Employing Units, Iowa Revenue Wage Statement Filing

Vendor Bills, Assets And Income Evidence

Recommended bookkeeping practice pairs vendor invoices with approval, delivery confirmation and payment. Retain credit memos and match them to purchases before settling supplier statements. If a repair business buys parts and equipment, separate resale stock from its own tools and document stock used internally. Keep asset purchase and disposal records for later basis review.

Record customer receipts against invoices and preserve the underlying gross amount when a processor deducts fees. Separate borrowing, owner funds and transfers from income. Keep customer contracts for deposits or extended projects and document refunds and bad debts. Expense descriptions should explain the business purpose rather than merely repeat a bank merchant name.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Monthly Reporting And Tax Reconciliations

A practical monthly close reconciles banks and cards, merchant settlements, unpaid customer invoices, supplier balances and inventory. Prepare a profit and loss statement and balance sheet, with a cash forecast when large purchasing or payroll commitments are approaching. Review old suspense balances before relying on the reports.

Maintain separate tax movement schedules for sales/use, employee withholding and unemployment. Compare return totals to the ledger, payments to agency confirmations and closing balances to amounts still due. Preserve local sales detail where applicable. Explain any difference between the income statement, sales reporting and cash deposits through documented timing, fees or classification.

General bookkeeping reference. The example and monthly routine are practical illustrations, not additional state requirements.

Sources: IRS, Small Business Recordkeeping

Year End And Retention Categories

Give the preparer annual reconciliations, sales and purchase tax workpapers, exemption support, payroll statements, myIowaUI reports, inventory counts, asset schedules and loan documents. Iowa requires wage statements containing Iowa withholding to be filed electronically through GovConnectIowa. Compare statement totals with periodic payroll and remittance records before filing.

Revenue says sales/use records such as bills, receipts, invoices and register documentation must be maintained for at least three years, and electronic records are permitted. It warns longer records can be requested when returns were never filed. Keep open audit periods and continuing asset records as appropriate; the sales/use minimum does not establish all employment or income tax retention requirements.

Sources: Iowa Revenue Sales And Use Tax Resource, Iowa Revenue Wage Statement Filing

Business Income, Entity Elections And Owner Credits

Iowa has a corporation income tax program and an optional entity tax election for eligible partnerships and S corporations. The election applies for a single year and is binding once made for that year; a disregarded single member LLC cannot make its own election. Preserve federal and Iowa classifications, the approved election and owner schedules so entity payments and owners refundable credits can be traced.

Maintain Iowa income adjustments, source receipts and owner allocation evidence with the federal business return. Where estimates apply, Iowa directs the filing period to the final day of the tax year rather than the installment due date. Use that distinction in monthly payment reconciliations and keep entity estimates separate from payroll withholding. The annual handoff should include payment confirmations, credits, elections, ownership changes and any composite filing questions resolved by the preparer.

Sources: Iowa Revenue Entity Tax Election, Iowa Revenue Estimated Business Payments, Iowa Revenue Corporation Income Tax

Unemployment Contributions And Wage Evidence

Iowa Workforce Development requires liable employers to submit quarterly wage detail and payment through myIowaUI. Its explanation distinguishes total wages and taxable wages used in the quarterly calculation and employer experience account. Retain the employee detail, contribution calculation and account notices. Reconcile employer unemployment expense and payable to posted payments each month, and preserve corrections at employee and quarter level. At year end compare the four quarterly files with payroll records and include unresolved notices separately from GovConnectIowa withholding confirmations.

Sources: Iowa Workforce Development Employing Units

A Cedar Rapids Service Example

Hypothetical example: a Cedar Rapids cleaning business serves a commercial building and a private home while buying supplies online. The bookkeeper keeps separate contracts and service locations, records gross customer receipts before card fees, and retains untaxed supply invoices for review. The preparer evaluates each service and purchase from those facts. Payroll withholding and myIowaUI contributions are reconciled separately.
