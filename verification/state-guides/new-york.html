New York Sales Tax, Payroll & Bookkeeping Records

New York records need to distinguish the locality of a sale, the parts of a combined payroll filing and separate benefit obligations. Preserve transaction detail and employee records instead of relying only on a year end total.

Last Reviewed: October 16, 2026. Source review only. Professional review pending.

Registration And New York Account Records

Keep a register of the business's sales tax Certificate of Authority where applicable, employer accounts, entity details and agency notices. New York's Tax Department says businesses required to register for sales tax should apply at least 20 days before starting the activity. This is a registration lead time, not a rule saying every new business must collect sales tax.

Record what the business sells, where goods or services are delivered, employee work locations and changes in ownership. These facts help a tax professional evaluate registrations and local obligations. A New York mailing address, incorporation document or bank account is not by itself a full nexus analysis. Preserve copies of the registration and assigned filing information instead of relying on a password saved by one employee.

Sources: New York Tax — Do I Need To Register For Sales Tax?, New York Tax — Recordkeeping For Businesses

Sales Tax Audit Trails And Locality Detail

New York sales tax vendor records must establish each sale's taxable status and tax due, connect exempt transactions to supporting certificates, and retain delivery location evidence where relevant. Maintain gross sales, taxable sales, credits, purchases subject to use tax and tax by locality. Invoices should identify taxable and nontaxable items and separately state tax.

For the bookkeeper, this means preserving detail before it is compressed into a settlement deposit. Match processor activity, refunds and fees to the sales system and ledger. A net deposit does not show which customer received a refund or which locality belonged on a return. Use the agency's current industry guidance for the actual product or service; a mixed invoice should not inherit one unreviewed default category.

Sources: New York Tax — Recordkeeping Requirements For Sales Tax Vendors

Purchases, Payables And Vendor Documentation

Save the supplier invoice, description, use/delivery location, exemption basis where relevant and proof of payment. New York's vendor guidance includes purchase records supporting expenses and cost of goods sold, as well as taxable status. Send purchases without tax, uncertain exemptions and unusual bundled charges to the responsible preparer for review; do not silently treat all out of state vendor charges as exempt.

Keep payable adjustments traceable. If a supplier issues a credit or replaces an incorrect invoice, retain the link to the original rather than deleting the history. A vendor account should explain both the remaining amount owed and the tax review status. Obtain identity and contractual documentation needed for information reporting review, while leaving employee/contractor classification to a qualified specialist.

Sources: New York Tax — Recordkeeping Requirements For Sales Tax Vendors

Withholding, Wage Reporting And Benefit Liabilities

Employers required to withhold New York wages file NYS-45 each calendar quarter, with electronic filing/payment requirements described by the Tax Department. Keep payroll registers, withholding details, employee work/residence information, filed reports, amendments and confirmations. Where New York City or Yonkers withholding applies, preserve the data supporting it instead of assuming every New York employee has identical local treatment.

The combined filing does not make every payroll liability interchangeable. Reconcile withholding and unemployment components separately and compare them with provider reports and remittances. Disability and Paid Family Leave coverage are also separate matters addressed by the Workers' Compensation Board. Coverage depends on facts and exceptions; this resource supplies no contribution rate or determination for a specific employer. Save policy invoices, deduction schedules and benefit related payments for the payroll specialist.

Sources: New York Tax — Withholding Tax Filing Requirements, New York WCB — Disability And Paid Family Leave Coverage Requirements

Paid Sick Leave And Retention Boundaries

New York's paid sick leave resource specifies six years of payroll records including weekly leave accrual and use. Preserve the approved policy, hours information, leave balances and payroll links. A bank debit to the payroll company cannot establish how leave was earned or used. Keep access limited to appropriate staff and distinguish accounting evidence from confidential employee medical information.

That payroll period differs from sales tax records: the vendor bulletin states at least three years from the relevant return's due date or filing date, whichever is later, and longer preservation may be required for an audit or proceeding. The general business guidance also identifies a three year baseline after filing. Do not turn these into a single deletion rule; assets, federal obligations, disputes and other employment laws can require longer preservation.

Sources: New York State — Paid Sick Leave Eligibility And Information, New York Tax — Recordkeeping Requirements For Sales Tax Vendors, New York Tax — Recordkeeping For Businesses

Income, Expenses And Entity Tax Preparation

Track gross receipts, refunds, loans, owner contributions and transfers distinctly. Keep receipts and agreements that explain unusual income or expense categories. For a business operating in several states, maintain enough customer and activity detail for an adviser to review allocation and sourcing rather than labeling everything from a bank location.

New York's corporate tax information identifies different tax bases and possible additional metropolitan or New York City obligations. This resource does not calculate those taxes or assume they apply to every business. Provide entity/election documents, ownership information, revenue detail, fixed assets and earlier return adjustments to the CPA or enrolled agent. A bookkeeper's financial statement and a tax return can use different classifications and adjustments; preserve the reconciliation between them.

Sources: New York Tax — Corporate Taxes

Monthly Close And An Illustrative Mixed Sale

The following closing routine is general bookkeeping practice. Reconcile cash and cards; review customer aging, unpaid bills and clearing accounts; then prepare reports on a consistent agreed basis. Roll sales tax and payroll liabilities forward independently so tax collected is not mistaken for sales revenue and an agency payment is not recorded twice as an expense.

Hypothetical example: a retailer delivers an order containing taxable merchandise and an item claimed exempt. Keep the itemized invoice, delivery evidence, certificate where required, tax detail and subsequent refund. If the processor settles a lower net amount after fees, reconcile that settlement separately. The example deliberately supplies no rate or taxability conclusion; its point is to prevent a single gross deposit entry from losing the locality and exemption evidence the preparer needs.

An unpaid invoice remains a customer balance until matched with a receipt or an authorized adjustment. If a refund occurs in a later reporting period, preserve both transaction dates and the link between them. This makes the adjustment explainable without rewriting the earlier sales history.

Sources: New York Tax — Recordkeeping Requirements For Sales Tax Vendors

Year End Package And Professional Review

Prepare reconciliations, open receivables/payables, sales tax return copies, payroll quarterly totals, year end employee forms, asset documents and a list of unresolved items. Compare the year end payroll summary with the filed quarterly reports before handing it off. Use current agency calendars and the assigned filing schedule; entity, payroll and sales tax obligations do not necessarily share one deadline.

Restaurants, contractors, e commerce sellers and professional firms need different taxability or employment reviews. This pilot is not a comprehensive New York City tax resource, a ruling on remote worker withholding or a determination of employee status. A tax professional should review filing positions, a payroll expert should confirm wage reporting and benefits, and counsel should address employment law questions. Paper Trail's bookkeeping role organizes the evidence; it does not guarantee compliance or replace those services.
