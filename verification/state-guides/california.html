California Bookkeeping & Financial Recordkeeping Resource

California records often need to support three different agency relationships: CDTFA sales and use tax, EDD employer reporting, and FTB income or franchise tax. Build an accounting file that explains each balance and preserves the documents behind it.

Last Reviewed: October 16, 2026. Source review only. Professional review pending.

California Registrations And Agency Accounts

Start with an agency account register rather than assuming one California registration covers everything. Keep the legal entity name, ownership changes, federal identification number, CDTFA permit details where applicable, EDD employer account, FTB correspondence and the person authorized to receive notices together. A sales tax permit and an employer payroll account serve different purposes. Entity formation also does not settle whether a particular sale is taxable or which returns a business must file.

CDTFA administers sales and use tax; EDD provides the employer registration and reporting workflow; FTB publishes filing resources for business entity types. Give your tax professional the actual activity, locations, workforce and entity election information. For multistate operations, record customer delivery destinations, employee work locations and inventory locations so a specialist can evaluate nexus. This resource deliberately supplies no universal nexus threshold or registration conclusion.

Sources: CDTFA — Sales & Use Tax In California, EDD — Employer Payroll Taxes, FTB — Keeping Your Tax Records

Sales, Resale Documents And Purchase Use Tax

California sales tax generally concerns retail goods unless an exemption applies; use tax can concern taxable goods used in California when sales tax was not charged. The state, local and district components make the transaction location important. Preserve the product description, sale date, delivery evidence, gross sale, separately identified tax and the documentation supporting any claimed exclusion. Do not substitute the payment processor's net bank deposit for the underlying sale.

For a resale transaction, associate the supporting certificate with the purchaser and transaction records. For purchases, retain vendor invoices and identify charges on which no tax appears. Put those purchases on a review list with their use location and business purpose. A missing tax line does not itself prove an exemption, and an invoice from another state does not itself prove tax is due. Have the authorized preparer resolve the treatment before reporting.

Sources: CDTFA — Sales & Use Tax In California, California Tax Service Center — Keeping Good Business Records

Payroll And Worker Documentation

EDD's employer workflow separates registration, worker reporting, withholding/payment and returns. Keep approved time records, payroll registers, worker addresses and work locations, deductions, employer charges, payment confirmations and filed wage reports. Reconcile gross payroll to the ledger; then reconcile each liability to payments and the payroll provider's reports. An amount withheld from an employee is not ordinary company revenue.

EDD also identifies reporting requirements for new hires and certain independent contractors. Maintain agreements and a vendor payment history, but do not treat a Form 1099 or a vendor's business name as proof of worker status. Classification and multistate wage allocation need a qualified payroll or employment specialist. The exact wage bases, rates, deposit schedule and employment law rules are outside this draft's verified scope.

Sources: EDD — Employer Payroll Taxes, EDD — Reporting Requirements

Payables, Income And Expense Evidence

Use a vendor file that connects the bill, purchase approval, delivery record and payment. Separate inventory bought for resale from equipment, supplies and personal spending. For a disputed invoice, retain the credit memo and correspondence rather than deleting the original bill. Keep a review flag for purchase use tax, missing receipts and uncertain worker classifications; these are different issues and should not share one unexplained suspense balance.

FTB's recordkeeping guidance identifies income, expense and property evidence. In practice, an owner contribution, loan deposit, customer payment and refund can all appear as money entering the same bank account. Label their origin and attach the relevant agreement or invoice. Asset purchases also need acquisition and improvement records so the tax professional can determine basis and depreciation; the bank description alone cannot establish that treatment.

Sources: FTB — Keeping Your Tax Records, California Tax Service Center — Keeping Good Business Records

Monthly Financial Statements And California Liabilities

The following is general bookkeeping practice, not a special California accounting standard. Close bank and card reconciliations, compare customer balances with invoices and receipts, review unpaid vendor bills and investigate old clearing entries. Present a profit and loss statement and balance sheet on the business's agreed accounting basis. If cash flow is tight, show upcoming payroll, approved bills and tax payments separately from the income statement.

Keep distinct liability schedules for sales/use tax and payroll components that actually apply. Each schedule should show opening balance, current period activity, payments, adjustments and closing balance. Compare the ledger with filed return copies and agency notices. Identify timing differences explicitly, such as a liability recorded at month end but paid next month; do not force the account to zero to make a report appear complete.

Illustration: A California Retail Purchase

Hypothetical example: a small retailer buys display equipment from an out of state supplier, and the invoice contains no California tax. The bookkeeper saves the invoice, delivery address, purchase date and payment, distinguishes equipment from resale stock, and sends the purchase for use tax review. No rate or automatic tax conclusion is assumed here. If the preparer determines a liability exists, its support should identify the applicable period and how it was calculated.

Separately, the shop receives a customer card settlement reduced by processing fees. Record the underlying sales, refunds and tax from the sales system, with processor fees and settlement timing reconciled to cash. This prevents a net deposit from understating gross sales or obscuring tax collected. It also preserves a usable trail if the shop later changes its point of sale provider.

Sources: CDTFA — Sales & Use Tax In California

Year End Handoff And Retention

For year end, assemble reconciled accounts, open invoices/bills, inventory support where relevant, fixed asset documents, payroll annual reports, sales/use return copies and unresolved items for the tax professional. Keep filing acknowledgments with the relevant reporting period. Use the agency assigned calendar and current instructions; a calendar year business, fiscal year entity and employer can have different obligations. Disaster relief or an extension should be documented, not inferred.

CDTFA Publication 116 states a minimum four year period for required sales/use tax records and longer preservation during audits or disputes. Export point of sale detail before an overwrite or migration. FTB says its ordinary assessment period is usually four years, with exceptions, and property basis records may need longer preservation. Those statements are not one universal destruction date. Obtain a separate payroll/personnel retention schedule; the older EDD resource located during research is not a substitute for verification of the current edition.

Sources: FTB — Keeping Your Tax Records, California Tax Service Center — Keeping Good Business Records, CDTFA Publication 116 — Retaining Records

Industry Boundaries And Professional Review

A retailer's resale documentation, a contractor's job records and a professional firm's service invoices can require different review. Mixed goods and services contracts, marketplaces, district tax sourcing and worker classification should be evaluated from the actual facts. Do not copy a tax treatment from another company merely because both businesses use the same bookkeeping software.

Paper Trail's role described here is organizing and reconciling records for a useful handoff. A CPA or enrolled agent should review filing positions, entity taxes and tax adjustments; a payroll specialist or attorney should resolve employment and classification questions. Before publication, an independent reviewer should confirm the California specific statements and current agency links.

Sources: CDTFA — Sales & Use Tax In California, EDD — Employer Payroll Taxes, FTB — Keeping Your Tax Records
