Business Expenses Worth Tracking Consistently

Track the costs of operating your business, including direct job costs, rent, software, insurance, professional services, bank fees and other business spending. A useful record shows what was purchased, why it related to the business and how it was paid. Recording a cost does not by itself establish a tax deduction.

Build categories that reflect how the business works

A service business may need separate categories for subcontractors, advertising and project supplies. A seller of products may also need inventory and cost of goods sold records. Choose categories that answer management questions, then apply them consistently. Too many nearly identical categories can make reports harder to compare without adding useful detail.

Keep the purchase evidence and business purpose

A card statement usually identifies the merchant and amount, but it may not explain what was bought. Keep receipts, invoices and supporting notes when the business purpose is not obvious. A mixed purchase may need a reasonable documented split between business and personal amounts. Avoid assigning an entire purchase to a business category simply because a business card was used.

Separate expenses from other uses of cash

Equipment purchases, loan principal, owner withdrawals and transfers can all reduce bank cash without being ordinary operating expenses. Identify them before relying on a profit report. Your tax professional determines deduction eligibility, depreciation and any special limitations. The bookkeeper’s job is to organize complete facts and maintain clear accounts so those decisions are made with adequate support.
