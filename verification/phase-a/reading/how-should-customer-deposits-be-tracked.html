Track Customer Deposits Before the Work Is Complete

Track customer deposits by customer, project, amount and terms, with a clear record of the work or refund still owed. Apply each deposit to the appropriate invoice or refund when its purpose is fulfilled. Accounting and tax treatment depend on the agreement and reporting method, so cash received is not automatically profit.

Keep the agreement with the payment

Record the customer, project, amount, payment date and the terms governing its use or refund. A refundable security deposit is different from an advance payment toward services. A nonrefundable label alone does not resolve every accounting or tax question. Give your tax professional the contract facts when treatment is unclear.

Connect the deposit to the final invoice

For illustration, a customer pays $600 toward a $2,000 project. When the remaining amount becomes due, the invoice and payment record should show the $600 already received and the $1,400 balance. Recording a second $2,000 collection without applying the deposit would overstate the customer’s obligation or duplicate income.

Review outstanding deposits regularly

List deposits for work that is unfinished, canceled or awaiting a refund. Compare the deposit records with customer balances and the relevant balance sheet account when applicable. Accrual financial reporting often tracks obligations for services not yet delivered, while tax treatment of advances can follow different rules.
