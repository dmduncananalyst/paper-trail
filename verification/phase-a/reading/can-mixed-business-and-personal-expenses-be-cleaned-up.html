Separate Personal Spending From Business Records

Yes, mixed business and personal activity can usually be reviewed and separated when enough supporting information exists. The goal is to identify the purpose of each transaction and record owner activity correctly, rather than treating every charge on a business account as a business expense.

Start with the purpose, not the payment card

A personal grocery purchase paid from a business account does not become a business cost. Conversely, a documented business purchase paid personally may still need to appear in the business records. The correct entry for reimbursement, an owner contribution or an owner withdrawal depends on the business structure and the facts.

Split purchases only when the evidence supports it

A single store receipt might contain office supplies and personal items. Use the itemized receipt to separate the amounts. Do not apply an arbitrary business percentage to an entire statement. Costs with both business and personal use, such as certain vehicle or phone expenses, require a supportable allocation and may involve tax professional review.

Prevent the same cleanup next month

Use separate accounts for future business activity and preserve documentation for any exceptions. Mark uncertain items for review while the purchase is still familiar. A practical cleanup deliverable includes corrected categories, a clear owner activity record and a short list of unresolved allocations. Paper Trail can organize these facts so that personal spending does not obscure operating costs or create misleading business reports.
