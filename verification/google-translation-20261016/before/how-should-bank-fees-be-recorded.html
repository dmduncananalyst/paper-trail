Record Bank Fees Without Losing Track of Costs

Record genuine business bank fees in a consistent expense category and match them to the bank statement. Fees may include account maintenance, wire charges or transaction charges. Keep them separate from loan principal, owner withdrawals and unrelated transfers so the report explains what the bank actually charged.

Check whether the fee is already recorded

A bank feed may have imported the fee before reconciliation begins. Searching the register first prevents a second entry. Confirm the date, amount and description against the statement, and investigate a charge that is unfamiliar rather than assigning a category without review.

Distinguish fees from withheld processing costs

A bank fee charged directly to checking is different from a payment processor fee deducted before a customer payout reaches the bank. If a processor collects $500 and sends $485 after a $15 fee, the underlying sales and fee need to be explained separately. The $485 deposit alone does not show the entire activity.

Use the category to notice patterns

Review fee totals over time for repeated overdrafts, unused accounts or higher transaction costs. A waived or refunded fee should be linked to the original charge so the net cost remains understandable. Tax deductibility depends on the business purpose and applicable rules, but the bookkeeping should first record the facts accurately. A consistent fee category supports both reconciliation and an informed discussion about banking costs.
