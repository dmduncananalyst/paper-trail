Reconcile Every Business Credit Card Account

Yes, every business credit card account with a balance or activity should be reconciled against its statement. Reconciling only the bank account confirms payments leaving the bank, but it does not prove that individual card purchases, refunds, fees and the remaining card liability are correct.

Review the right account structure

Some business card programs provide one master statement with several employee cards. Determine whether the software tracks the master account, individual cards or both. Reconcile in a way that matches the issuer’s statement structure so the same activity is not counted in two accounts.

Include cards that were replaced or closed

A replaced card may still have refunds or delayed transactions. A closed account may retain a balance, fee or final payment. Keep the statements and confirm that activity has been recorded through the final period. An inactive label in the software does not establish that the closing balance is supported.

Check payments without repeating expenses

A $500 bank payment reduces the amount owed on the card; the purchases that created that balance should already have their own entries. Compare both sides of payments and investigate unapplied credits. A complete card reconciliation should explain the statement ending balance, outstanding timing items and any remaining differences. This keeps credit spending visible even when it will not be paid from the bank until a later month.
