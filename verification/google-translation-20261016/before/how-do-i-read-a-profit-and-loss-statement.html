Read Your Profit and Loss Statement With Confidence

Read a profit and loss statement from revenue through costs to the final profit or loss for a defined period. First confirm the date range and whether it uses cash or accrual accounting. The report describes financial performance, while the bank balance answers a different question about available cash.

Follow the layers of the report

Revenue shows recognized sales or income. Direct costs, when separately reported, relate to delivering the product or service. Subtracting those costs gives gross profit. Operating expenses such as rent, administrative support and software then reduce that amount. Other income or expense may appear separately depending on the business and report layout.

Use a simple numerical example

A report with $30,000 of revenue, $12,000 of direct costs and $11,000 of other expenses shows $7,000 of profit before any additional items included elsewhere. Gross profit is $18,000, or 60 percent of revenue. These figures illustrate the calculation and are not benchmarks for your industry.

Ask what changed and whether the report is complete

Compare consistent periods, investigate unusual costs and verify that missing transactions or owner payments have not distorted the totals. A loan receipt is not ordinary sales income, and repaying loan principal is not ordinarily a profit and loss expense. Pair the statement with the balance sheet and cash information before making a spending decision. Save explanations for meaningful changes so next month’s comparison has context.
