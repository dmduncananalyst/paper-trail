Find Transactions Missing From the Month

Check completeness by comparing the books with independent records for the entire month, including bank statements, card statements, sales systems and supplier records. A report showing transactions does not prove that all transactions were recorded. Each source needs a defined beginning and ending date.

Check every active account

List the accounts and payment channels used during the month, including newly opened or recently closed accounts. Review statement continuity, imported date ranges and unusual gaps. A disconnected secondary card can leave expenses missing even when the main bank account is fully reconciled.

Include activity outside the bank feed

Cash sales, unpaid customer invoices, unpaid supplier bills and platform funds awaiting payout may not appear in the bank statement. Which items affect the financial reports depends on the reporting basis, but the business still needs operational records to track them. Compare sales totals and invoice activity with deposits without assuming the amounts should always match directly.

Resolve gaps before calling the month complete

Imagine a business changed processors midmonth. The new platform’s deposits may appear in the bank while its fees and refunds are absent from the books. Obtain both platforms’ reports and trace the transition. Keep a checklist of expected statements and source reports, and explain unresolved gaps. This makes the closing process repeatable and gives the owner a clearer view of what the monthly reports include.
