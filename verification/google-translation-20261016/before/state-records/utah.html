Utah Bookkeeping

Utah retail records must connect receipts, exemptions, purchase invoices, and the sales and use tax returns they support. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Utah.

Keeping records that support the books

Every retailer, lessor, or person doing business in Utah must keep complete records used to determine sales and use tax liability. Utah State Tax Commission Publication 25, citing Rule R865.19S.22, says records must show all gross receipts from Utah sales or leases, deductions and exemptions claimed, and bills, invoices, and similar proof for property and products bought for sale, consumption, or lease in Utah. Source: https://tax.utah.gov/forms/2021/pub-25.pdf

How state taxes affect the ledger

Utah imposes an individual income tax and a corporate franchise and income tax, and a sales tax and a use tax. Publication 25 covers sales and use tax, including that use tax applies where taxable goods or services are used in Utah without sales tax having been paid. Source: https://tax.utah.gov/forms/2021/pub-25.pdf

Employers withhold Utah income tax from wages, as in the payroll section, which is a difference from the no income tax states in this batch. Retailers need gross receipts records that separate taxable sales, exempt sales, and deductions, as listed in the recordkeeping section.

Payroll records and employer reporting

Employers must report all newly hired or rehired employees to the Utah New Hire Registry, administered through the Department of Workforce Services, within 20 days of their first day of work. The Department New Hire Reporting Handbook states that rule and cites Utah Code the payroll section5A.7.101 for the registry act. Source, official handbook hosted on jobs.utah.gov: https://jobs.utah.gov/ui/employer/public/NewHire/New%20Hire%20Handbook.pdf

The handbook and the related employer form state that failure to report timely can lead to a civil penalty in a range stated in those materials, and that required information must be provided within 20 calendar days of the first day of work. Source for the form summary: https://jobs.utah.gov/UI/Employer/Public/Handbook/NEW_Hire_form6.pdf

Income, purchases, and documentation

For sales and use tax, the documentation rule is the one in the recordkeeping section, namely gross receipts records, deduction and exemption support, and bills and invoices for purchases. Source: https://tax.utah.gov/forms/2021/pub-25.pdf

How long to retain records

Sales and use tax records must be kept for three years from the date the tax return is filed, and must be available to the Tax Commission in their original format, paper or electronic, according to Publication 25 citing Rule R865.19S.22. Source: https://tax.utah.gov/forms/2021/pub-25.pdf

When an industry needs additional records

Interstate trucking and carriers. Mileage logs, fuel purchase records, and jurisdiction by jurisdiction distance records are required to support apportioned registration and fuel tax reporting. Scope is limited to qualified motor carriers.

Construction. Contractors in Utah generally owe sales tax on materials they incorporate as real property improvement in many fact patterns, so job cost records need to separate materials, labor, and equipment. The general sales tax record rule in the recordkeeping section applies to those purchases, and the specific tax treatment depends on the contract facts, which should be checked in current Tax Commission guidance before a position is taken.

Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Utah retailer keeps gross receipts reports, exempt sale support, and purchase invoices for three years from the filing date of the return they support, in the original paper or electronic format, so the records can be produced to the Tax Commission as Publication 25 describes.

A Salt Lake City employer reports a new hire to the Utah New Hire Registry within 20 days of the first day of work, withholds Utah income tax through its Tax Commission withholding account, and files its quarterly unemployment wage report with Workforce Services, treating those as three separate filings.

Gross receipts and use tax on untaxed purchases

A Utah retailer should be able to show both the tax collected on sales and the purchases reviewed for use tax. These involve opposite sides of the books, accounts receivable and accounts payable, and should not be confused simply because both appear on tax workpapers.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Utah is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

A practical monthly review for Utah businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.
