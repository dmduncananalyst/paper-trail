Make Your Monthly Bookkeeping Review Count

During a bookkeeping review, ask what changed, what remains unresolved and what the reports mean for the business. The most useful questions connect reported totals with the transactions behind them. Begin by confirming the reporting dates, accounting basis and whether all agreed accounts were reconciled.

Ask why the important numbers moved

If revenue or expenses changed substantially, ask which customers, projects or categories explain the difference. Compare the same period lengths and a consistent accounting basis. Ask whether a large cost is recurring, unusual or a timing effect. This makes the discussion more useful than simply noting that one month was better or worse.

Ask which balances need action

Review overdue customer invoices, unpaid vendor bills, unexplained credits and old outstanding transactions where these are included in the scope. Ask whether a loan or card balance agrees with the provider’s statement. A report can be mathematically balanced while still containing transactions that need investigation or a decision from the owner.

Leave with clear responsibilities

Identify missing receipts, disputed items and any question for the tax preparer. Agree who will supply each document or approve each correction and when it is needed. If a previous month changed, request an explanation and updated reports. A review works best as a recurring business conversation, with consistent information and a short list of decisions, rather than a presentation of figures that nobody uses afterward.
