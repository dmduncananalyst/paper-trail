Business Bookkeeping

Business Records

The word retainer can describe different arrangements. A customer may pay a monthly service fee, fund a balance that will be used against future work or make an advance payment for a specific milestone. Those arrangements should not automatically receive identical bookkeeping treatment.

Keep the agreement, invoice and payment connected. Record the service period and any unused balance or credit that must be tracked. If the contract changes, preserve the change with the original record. The objective is a visible trail from the customer’s payment to the work it relates to, with accounting treatment confirmed for the reporting basis in use.

Income and Expenses

A creative business may pay freelancers, purchase stock assets and subscribe to software. Some costs belong to one client engagement. Others support the entire studio. Consistent categories help distinguish those groups without pretending that every shared subscription can be traced neatly to a single campaign.

Client funded purchases also deserve attention. Keep the approval, supplier receipt and customer invoice together so reimbursable spending does not disappear inside general expenses. Whether a reimbursement is reported through revenue and expense accounts or another structure depends on the arrangement. A tidy supporting schedule gives your accounting professional the information needed to confirm that treatment.

Local Business Example

Consider a studio with three monthly service clients and one separate website project. Two service clients have paid. The third invoice is still open. The website customer has paid an advance, while the freelance developer’s first invoice is due next week. The month can look healthy in the bank even though part of that cash supports unfinished work.

A simple operating schedule separates recurring service invoices, project advances, outstanding customer balances and committed supplier costs. The owner can use it to discuss workload and timing without confusing booked sales with available cash. This is an illustrative workflow, not a client result or a prediction of how a particular business should recognize revenue.

If you want to compare recurring services with individual projects, the categories should support that comparison from the start. A report cannot reliably explain margins by service when every sale uses one miscellaneous category and costs have no consistent references.
