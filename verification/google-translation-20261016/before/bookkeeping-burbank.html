Business Bookkeeping

Business Records

A project can feel finished while its bookkeeping remains open. Equipment returns may generate extra charges. A supplier may send a revised bill. A customer may hold the final payment while reviewing deliverables. If these items are scattered across email and card statements, the project total can change long after you thought the work was profitable.

Give each engagement a consistent project name or code. Use that reference on invoices, supplier bills and supporting records. A project report can then distinguish direct costs from shared business expenses, such as general software subscriptions and office rent. Shared expenses still matter, but they should not be assigned to one project simply because that project happened to pay the next bill.

Income and Expenses

A customer payment should be matched to the correct invoice when one exists. Recording both the invoice and its payment as separate sales can overstate revenue. Similarly, paying a supplier bill settles an existing amount owed when that bill has already been recorded. The bank withdrawal is not a second cost.

A useful monthly review checks unpaid invoices, unpaid bills, deposits and reimbursements alongside reconciled bank and card balances. The accounting treatment of advance payments depends on the arrangement and reporting basis. We organize the underlying records and flag treatment that needs confirmation, rather than assuming every incoming transfer is earned revenue.

Local Business Example

Imagine a small vendor collecting $6,000 toward a $10,000 engagement. During the project, it pays $2,400 for equipment rental and receives a $1,600 subcontractor bill that is still unpaid. The remaining $4,000 from the customer is also outstanding. Looking only at the bank account misses both the unpaid cost and the uncollected balance.

A project schedule brings those four items together. It helps the owner see what has been collected, what remains due and which costs have been incurred. It is a planning view, not a promise of final profit. Staff costs, overhead, approved changes and the reporting method still affect the finished picture. These figures are illustrative and do not describe a Paper Trail client.

Monthly Records

Keep the project list current and identify who can approve a disputed charge. Save revised invoices beside the originals, with a note explaining the change. A short list of unresolved items is more useful than silently forcing every transaction into a category.

For an initial conversation, the number of active projects, how you invoice and whether older months are unfinished will tell us far more than a detailed account balance. We can use that overview to discuss the scope of reconciliation, project tracking and reporting. Burbank’s official business portal also provides local business resources separate from bookkeeping.
