Catch Up Your Books When Receipts Are Missing

You can begin catching up before every receipt is located, but missing documentation still needs attention. Statements establish payment activity, while receipts and invoices help explain what was purchased and its business purpose. Some expenses require more detailed substantiation than a bank transaction provides.

Build a useful record inventory

Collect monthly bank and card statements, sales records, supplier invoices, platform reports and the receipts you have. Identify missing periods separately from missing purchase details. If most records are available, an organized gap list helps concentrate the search on specific vendors, dates and amounts instead of postponing the entire project.

Reconstruct supporting details honestly

Request duplicate receipts from vendors or retrieve order histories and invoices from business accounts. For a payment without an itemized receipt, retain the available evidence and document what is known. Do not create an invented receipt or assume a bank withdrawal proves the purchase was deductible. Travel, gifts and similar expenses can have additional documentation requirements.

Keep unresolved items visible

A bookkeeper can organize the transaction and flag the missing support for your tax professional. For example, an online retailer charge may be recorded as needing review until the order details distinguish supplies from equipment or personal purchases. Completion should make the remaining gaps explicit. That provides a clearer tax preparation handoff and a practical basis for improving receipt capture going forward.
