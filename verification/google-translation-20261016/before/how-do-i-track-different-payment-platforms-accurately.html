Payment Processor Reconciliation for Sales, Fees and Payouts

Track each payment platform from gross customer activity through refunds, fees and adjustments to the net payout received by the bank. Recording only the bank deposit can hide the cost of processing and make sales difficult to reconcile. Each platform should have a clear, consistent record trail.

Reconcile the payout components

For illustration, $1,000 of customer payments less a $100 refund and $30 in fees produces an $870 payout when there are no other adjustments. The records should explain all four amounts. A platform may also retain funds, apply chargebacks or combine activity from several days into one deposit.

Avoid counting revenue twice

If sales were already recorded from invoices or an integrated sales system, the bank payout usually needs to match or transfer the existing recorded activity. Adding the payout as another sale can overstate revenue. Use payout identifiers and dates to connect the platform report to the bank deposit, and investigate any remaining platform balance.

Keep year end information aligned

Save sales reports, fee statements, refund details and any annual payment information forms alongside your monthly records. The IRS explains that the annual payment information form is used with other records to determine reporting, so it should not replace the transaction history. Timing differences around month end may be legitimate, but they should be explained. Paper Trail can help organize the separate platforms into one coherent set of books.

Follow the payout without treating it as another sale

A payment processor payout is a settlement of recorded activity, not necessarily a new revenue event. When invoices or sales have already entered the bookkeeping system, connect the payout with that activity. Otherwise the same sale can appear once from the sales system and again from the bank feed.

Save the report that identifies the payout and its components. The useful comparison connects customer activity, refunds, fees, adjustments and the amount transferred to the bank. A consistent payout reference helps explain why one bank deposit covers several sales dates or why a day’s sales have not yet reached the bank.

Explain money still held by the platform

A platform can hold funds or pay them out after the reporting date. The balance remaining with the processor therefore needs attention alongside the bank account. Repeatedly forcing all platform activity to equal same day deposits can hide legitimate timing differences or leave fees and refunds recorded in the wrong place.

Use the reports for the same platform account and reporting period, then review activity crossing the cutoff. Keep disputed transactions and chargebacks identifiable rather than combining every adjustment into an unexplained sales difference. The exact account treatment depends on how the business records its sales and payments.

Use annual payment information alongside the books

An annual payment form is part of the supporting information, not a substitute for the transaction records. Compare it with the relevant platform activity and give your tax preparer the reports explaining refunds, adjustments and timing. Avoid assuming the form amount must equal either bank deposits or taxable income.

An organized monthly payout trail makes that annual comparison easier. It also helps identify a missing settlement, an unexpected fee or an integration that imported the same activity twice.
