Business Bookkeeping

Business Records

A supplier payment shows that money left the bank. By itself, it does not tell you what arrived, what remains on the shelf or which items have been sold. Product records need purchase details, quantities and receiving information as well as the payment trail.

Inventory treatment depends on the accounting method and business circumstances. The working records should still make purchases, returns, stock movements and sales understandable. A consistent product list and clear separation between resale goods, workshop supplies and equipment make it easier to apply the agreed treatment without relying on a generic purchases category.

Income and Expenses

A supplier invoice may include items that arrive in separate shipments. Some goods may be returned or damaged, and the credit may not reach the account until later. Paying the statement total without checking individual documents can leave an unresolved credit or duplicate bill in the books.

Keep purchase references, receiving notes, invoices and credit documents together. A review of the supplier balance should explain what is still due and which credits have been applied. If stock records are held in a separate system, its totals need a defined connection to the accounting records. Complexity should be identified before promising a routine monthly service.

Local Business Example

Imagine a small distributor buying 100 units and selling 60 during the month. The other 40 units remain on hand. If the owner looks only at the purchase payment and sales deposits, the month may be difficult to interpret because some purchased goods remain available for future sales.

A stock movement schedule connects beginning quantities, receipts, sales, returns and ending quantities. The accounting records can then be reviewed against that schedule using the agreed inventory method. A physical count may reveal a difference that needs investigation. These numbers are illustrative, and the example does not prescribe a tax method or inventory valuation policy.

Monthly Records

Bank statements cannot recreate missing quantities, identify damaged goods or confirm whether an order was delivered. Those facts come from operational records and the people handling the stock. If those records are incomplete, the first task is to establish what can be supported and what needs a separate inventory review.

An initial discussion can cover whether you sell goods, make products or mainly provide services, where stock is tracked and whether supplier balances are current. From there, we can discuss purchase categorization, bank reconciliation and payable tracking. Specialized manufacturing costing or complex inventory systems may require an accountant or another specialist to define the method before routine entry begins.
