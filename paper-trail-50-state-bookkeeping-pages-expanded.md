# Alabama Bookkeeping

Sales, use, and rental activity can create different sets of records, especially when a business operates across local tax jurisdictions. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Alabama.

## Keeping records that support the books

Alabama tax law in Title 40 of the Code of Alabama imposes recordkeeping duties tied to the tax being reported. For sales, use, and rental tax, a multistate retailer, seller, vendor, lessor, or person doing business in Alabama must keep complete and adequate records needed for the Alabama Department of Revenue to determine the tax owed. The rule text lists records including gross receipts from sales or rental payments in Alabama, whether or not the seller regards the receipts as taxable. Source: Alabama Department of Revenue, Sales and Use Tax Rulebook, Rule 810-27-1-.7.01 summary in search results, at https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/ultraviewer/viewer/basic_viewer/index.html?form=2022/08/Sales_Use_Rulebook.pdf

Taxpayers must keep copies of filed returns as part of their records, and keep property records for as long as needed to figure basis in property. That wording appears in Alabama individual income tax return instructions published by the Department of Revenue. Source: https://revenue.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/25f40bk.pdf

## How state taxes affect the ledger

Alabama has a state sales tax, a use tax, a rental tax on tangible personal property in the rule cited above, an individual income tax, a corporate income tax, and employer withholding of Alabama income tax. Each tax has its own return, so bookkeeping must separate gross receipts, taxable and nontaxable sales, purchases subject to use tax, and wages subject to withholding. Sources: Alabama Department of Revenue rulebook above, and the Alabama income tax booklet above, which discusses individual income tax, withholding returns, sales tax returns, and Form W-2 filing.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

All Alabama employers must report each newly hired or recalled employee to the Alabama Department of Labor within seven days from the date of hire or reemployment. Employers reporting electronically may transmit twice monthly, with transmissions not less than 12 days and not more than 16 days apart. Employers with five or more employees must report through the Internet system. Required data includes the employee name, address, and Social Security number, the first day of work, whether the person was newly hired or recalled, and the employer Federal Employer Identification Number, name, and address. Failure to report may be fined up to 25 dollars for each violation, as stated on the agency page. Source: Alabama Department of Labor, Alabama New Hire, at https://adol.alabama.gov/employers/alabama-new-hire/

## When an industry needs additional records

Businesses in regulated Alabama industries have extra record duties under their licensing regimes, including motor vehicle dealers and licensing offices for vehicle documents, financial institutions, and businesses subject to Alabama alcohol, tobacco, and motor fuel taxes. The vehicle document retention memo found in search results was addressed to licensing officials, not to general businesses, so it is not generalized here. Source: Alabama Department of Revenue memo at http://revenue.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/Memo_2016-23_Record_Retention_Requirements-1.pdf

Employers in construction and other industries that employ workers covered by Alabama unemployment and workers compensation laws must keep the payroll and injury related records those programs require, in addition to tax records.

## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A small Alabama retailer makes a sale, collects sales tax, and later buys equipment from an out of state seller who charged no tax. Its books need a sales journal showing gross receipts for the first transaction and a purchase record showing the equipment cost for the second, so the sales return and any use tax return can both be prepared from the same records.

A cafe hires a cook on a Monday. The owner reports the new hire to the Department of Labor within seven days, sets up a payroll record with the start date and Social Security number, and files the quarterly unemployment wage report and withholding return from that same payroll register, so the three reports agree.

## Rental transactions and the split between state and local sales taxes

A business that rents equipment as well as selling it cannot assume the same receipt description belongs in both revenue categories. The monthly ledger should identify rental receipts separately, maintain the underlying agreements, and keep sales activity linked to the jurisdiction where it occurred.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Alabama is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

## A practical monthly review for Alabama businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

## What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.

# Alaska Bookkeeping

Without a statewide sales tax, Alaska businesses still need to account for local taxes, industry taxes, and unemployment contributions. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Alaska.

## Keeping records that support the books

Keep a general ledger, bank statements, sales records by location, and payroll registers, because Alaska income is apportioned and because local taxes differ by place of sale.

## How state taxes affect the ledger

There is no Alaska employer withholding of a state personal income tax, because there is no such tax. Payroll bookkeeping for state purposes therefore centers on unemployment insurance, described in the payroll section.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Alaska employers must report newly hired and rehired employees to the Alaska Child Support Services Division. The official Alaska new hire reporting form states that Social Security numbers of newly hired or rehired employees are required under Alaska Statute 25.27.075, subsection b, and gives employer and employee information fields including Federal Employer Identification Number, Alaska Department of Labor number, employee name and address, and date of hire or rehire. Source: Alaska Department of Revenue, Child Support Services Division form, at https://dor.alaska.gov/Portals/7/Documents/Forms/04-1050-NewHireReporting-Fill-In.pdf

## When an industry needs additional records

Oil and gas producers, mining operations, and commercial fisheries businesses have Alaska tax and royalty record duties that most businesses do not have, including production, volume, and value records that support production tax, mining license tax, and fisheries tax returns.

Businesses that sell alcohol, tobacco, or motor fuel in Alaska have excise tax record and reporting duties tied to volumes sold.

## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A shop in Juneau collects and remits the local sales tax imposed there, while a shop in a community with no local sales tax does not. Both keep sales records by location, because the state imposes no statewide sales tax and the duty, if any, comes from the locality.

An Alaska employer hires a seasonal worker for the summer. It reports the hire to the Child Support Services Division with the worker Social Security number and hire date, and reports the wages for unemployment insurance in the quarter paid, showing both the employer and employee shares of the unemployment tax in its payroll register.

## Local sales taxes without a statewide sales tax

A business operating in several Alaska communities should not build its sales ledger around a single statewide tax setting. Instead, the sales export should identify each selling location and preserve the supporting local tax treatment. That distinction matters even when two otherwise similar shops use the same accounting software.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Alaska is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

## A practical monthly review for Alaska businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. # Arizona Bookkeeping

Arizona transaction privilege tax makes the classification and location of receipts especially important. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Arizona.

## Keeping records that support the books

Arizona businesses subject to transaction privilege tax must keep records of gross receipts, deductions, and exempt sales sufficient to complete the transaction privilege tax return by business classification and by location. Arizona taxes are reported by classification, and the Department of Revenue TPT update materials list many classifications, including retail, restaurant and bars, hotels, contracting, commercial rental, and use tax purchases. Source: Arizona Department of Revenue, TPT Update, April 2025, at https://azdor.gov/tpt-update-april-2025

## How state taxes affect the ledger

Arizona has a transaction privilege tax, often called TPT. It is a tax on the privilege of doing business, measured by gross receipts, and it is imposed on the business under classifications, rather than being a true sales tax collected as a separate item in all cases, although businesses commonly pass the cost on. Arizona also has a use tax, a state individual income tax, a corporate income tax, and employer withholding. Source: Arizona Department of Revenue TPT materials cited in the recordkeeping section, which also list use tax classifications for purchases and for inventory.

Marijuana businesses have additional Arizona excise tax classifications. The Department TPT update lists medical marijuana retail, adult use marijuana retail, and related restaurant and accessory classifications. Only licensed marijuana businesses are in those classifications.

Sales must be tracked by classification and by city and county, because state, county, and many city TPT rates apply together and are reported on the same return system.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Arizona unemployment insurance is administered by the Arizona Department of Economic Security. Employers must register, file quarterly wage reports, and pay unemployment tax. The employer record list in the recordkeeping section, with a four calendar year scope, comes from the secondary retention summary cited there.

Employers withhold Arizona income tax using the employee Arizona withholding election, and file withholding returns with the Department of Revenue through AZTaxes.gov.

## When an industry needs additional records

Contracting is a distinct Arizona TPT area. Prime contracting, speculative builder, and owner builder classifications are listed in Department materials, and contracting businesses must track project receipts, subcontractor work, and materials in a way that separates contracting activity from retail sales of materials. Source: Department TPT update cited in the recordkeeping section.

Licensed medical and adult use marijuana businesses have Arizona excise tax and Department of Health Services licensing records in addition to TPT records, and must track sales by the marijuana classifications listed by the Department of Revenue.

## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Phoenix retailer sells goods at retail and also rents equipment. It codes each receipt to the retail classification or to the rental classification in its ledger, by location, because the TPT return requires gross receipts, deductions, and taxable amounts by classification.

An Arizona restaurant hires a server who left and returned after more than 60 days away. The restaurant reports the person as a rehire within 20 days, and keeps the payroll journal, check records, and quarterly wage reports for the four year employer record period described in the retention section.

## Transaction privilege tax classifications

For a business with retail sales and equipment rentals, a single sales total can hide different activity classifications. An informative monthly reconciliation separates the classifications before connecting receipts to the tax return. The classification decision itself belongs with a qualified preparer when the facts are unclear.

# Arkansas Bookkeeping

Retail gross receipts, compensating use tax, and county or city taxes place a premium on documenting each sale. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Arkansas.

## Keeping records that support the books

Arkansas taxpayers must keep records sufficient for the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration, called DFA, to determine tax owed under the taxes DFA administers, including sales and use tax, income tax, and withholding. DFA is the official custodian of records and files required by state tax law to be filed with DFA, under Arkansas Code the state tax section6-18-303, as described on the DFA tax collection data page. Source: https://www.dfa.arkansas.gov/office/taxes/excise-tax-administration/sales-use-tax/tax-collection-data/

Keep sales journals, purchase invoices, exemption certificates, bank records, and payroll registers tied to each return period. No separate Arkansas format for a general ledger for all businesses was found in the sources checked.

## How state taxes affect the ledger

Arkansas has a state sales tax, called gross receipts tax in Arkansas statutes, a compensating use tax, a state individual income tax, a corporate income tax, and employer withholding of Arkansas income tax. DFA administers these taxes. Bookkeeping must separate taxable and exempt sales, and must track out of state purchases for use tax.

Arkansas also has excise taxes that affect only affected businesses, including motor fuel, alcohol, tobacco, and severance taxes, and a mixed drink tax for hospitality businesses serving alcohol.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Unemployment insurance is administered by the Arkansas Division of Workforce Services. Employers must register, file quarterly wage reports, and pay unemployment contributions.

## When an industry needs additional records

Arkansas businesses subject to severance tax, including timber and other natural resources, must keep production and volume records. Alcohol retailers and mixed drink permit holders must keep purchase and sales records that support excise and mixed drink tax returns. Motor fuel businesses must keep gallonage records.

## Bookkeeping in practice

An Arkansas store sells goods in a city and a county that each impose a local sales tax collected by DFA. Its point of sale records show the delivery city for each sale, so the state and local tax on the return can be allocated correctly from the sales journal.

A Little Rock employer hires a new office worker, reports the hire within 20 days, has the worker complete the Arkansas withholding form, and keeps the name, address, occupation, rate, and amount paid each period in the payroll file for at least the three year period described in the payroll section.

## Local gross receipts tax and purchase use tax

The useful bookkeeping distinction is between a sale recorded at its full amount, the sales tax recorded as a liability, and a business purchase that may require use tax review. Keeping these in distinct accounts lets an owner understand income without confusing tax collected with earned revenue.

# California Bookkeeping

California businesses often need separate books and supporting documents for sales tax, employer payroll reporting, and income or franchise tax. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in California.

## Keeping records that support the books

California employers must register for an employer payroll tax account with the Employment Development Department, called EDD, report wages and withholdings, pay unemployment insurance and employment training tax, withhold and remit State Disability Insurance and personal income tax where applicable, and report new employees and, in specified cases, independent contractors. Source: EDD, Am I Required to Register as an Employer, at https://edd.ca.gov/en/payroll_taxes/Am_I_Required_to_Register_as_an_Employer/

## How state taxes affect the ledger

California has a sales tax and a use tax, administered by CDTFA, a personal income tax and a corporation franchise tax and corporate income tax, administered by FTB, and employer payroll taxes administered by EDD, namely unemployment insurance, employment training tax, State Disability Insurance, and personal income tax withholding. District sales and use taxes add local rates by district, so sales records need the place of sale or delivery.

California also has taxes and fees for specific industries administered by CDTFA and other agencies, including cannabis taxes, fuel taxes, alcohol and tobacco taxes, and hazardous waste and other fee programs. Only businesses in those programs are subject to them.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

California employers must report newly hired employees to the New Employee Registry within 20 calendar days of the start of work date, which is the first day services were performed for wages. A rehire after separation of at least 60 consecutive days must also be reported within 20 calendar days. Employers reporting electronically through e-Services for Business submit two monthly reports not less than 12 days and not more than 16 days apart. Required employer information includes the EDD eight digit employer payroll tax account number, Federal Employer Identification Number, business name and address, and contact information. Required employee information includes name, Social Security number, home address, and start date. Source: EDD, California New Employee Registry, at https://edd.ca.gov/en/payroll_taxes/new_hire_reporting

California employers must also report independent contractors in specified cases within 20 calendar days, using Report of Independent Contractor, Form DE 542, where the business is required to file a federal Form 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC for the services, pays or contracts for 600 dollars or more, and the contractor is an individual, sole proprietor, or single member limited liability company. Source: EDD, New Employer Resources, at https://edd.ca.gov/en/Payroll_Taxes/Helpful_Resources_New_Employer

## How long to retain records

For combined report taxpayers under the FTB record maintenance regulation, records for a year must be retained for the longer of the time the franchise or income tax is subject to adjustment, not to exceed eight years from the due date or extended due date of the return, or the period a protest, appeal, or refund suit is pending. A taxpayer need not maintain records beyond eight years for issues not in dispute. Source: FTB, Waters Edge Manual, Chapter 19, at https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/procedures/waters-edge-manual/chapter-19.pdf This eight year rule is stated here only for that combined reporting context, not as a general rule for all California businesses.

## When an industry needs additional records

Commercial cannabis is the clearest California example, with the seven year record rule in the retention section, plus track and trace inventory records and CDTFA cannabis tax records. Only licensed cannabis businesses are subject to those duties.

California construction employers have certified payroll duties on public works projects, and must keep and furnish payroll records for those projects under prevailing wage law. That duty applies to public works contractors and subcontractors, not to all construction work.

## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A California retailer sells an item for resale. It obtains a resale certificate from the buyer, files the certificate with the sales invoice, and keeps the sales journal, register tapes, and purchase invoices together for the four year sales tax record period described in the retention section, so an auditor can trace the exempt sale.

A San Diego cafe hires a barista and also engages a solo repair contractor for 800 dollars of work in a year where a Form 1099 is required. It reports the barista to the New Employee Registry within 20 calendar days of the first day of work, and reports the contractor on Form DE 542 within 20 calendar days under the EDD rule in the payroll section.

## District sales taxes and employment reporting

A California retailer may receive one net card deposit covering numerous taxable sales, refunds, and processing fees. The books should retain the gross transactions and the jurisdiction information instead of treating the bank deposit as sales. Payroll reconciliation needs a separate file linking wage reports, payments, and payroll liabilities.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in California is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

# Colorado Bookkeeping

Businesses selling across Colorado may have to distinguish state collected sales taxes from taxes administered by home rule cities. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Colorado.

## Keeping records that support the books

Colorado taxpayers must keep books and records sufficient to determine liability for Colorado taxes administered by the Colorado Department of Revenue, including income tax, sales and use tax, and withholding. Colorado regulations on market based sourcing state the principle in a related context, that a taxpayer shall retain contemporaneous records explaining its method and underlying assumptions and shall provide those records to the Department on request. Source: Code of Colorado Regulations materials at https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=8043&fileName=1%20CCR%20201-2

Keep sales records by taxing jurisdiction, because Colorado has state, county, city, and special district sales and use taxes, many of them state collected and some home rule city taxes self collected by the city.

## How state taxes affect the ledger

Colorado has a state sales tax, a use tax, a state income tax on individuals and corporations, and employer withholding of Colorado income tax. The Department of Revenue sales and use tax page describes retail sales tax filing, including new mandatory electronic filing phases for retail sales tax, county lodging tax, visitor benefit tax, and local marketing tax beginning in 2026 based on prior year gross sales thresholds. Source: Colorado Department of Revenue, Taxation Division, Sales and Use Tax, at https://tax.colorado.gov/sales-use-tax

Colorado also has severance tax on oil, gas, coal, and minerals, marijuana taxes for licensed marijuana businesses, and excise taxes on fuel, alcohol, and tobacco. Only businesses in those activities are subject to them.

Because many local taxes are state collected and some home rule cities collect their own tax, a Colorado seller needs records that show the delivery address and jurisdiction for each sale, and needs to know which cities it must file with directly.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Colorado employers must report new hires to the State Directory of New Hires within 20 calendar days after the date of hire, or by the first regularly scheduled payroll following the date of hire if that payroll is after the 20 day period. Source: Colorado Department of Labor and Employment, New Employer Checklist, at https://cdle.colorado.gov/employers/recruiting-hiring/new-employer-checklist

The same checklist requires employers to obtain and maintain workers compensation insurance for employees with few exceptions, to register for an unemployment insurance account, and to file required quarterly wage detail reports and pay unemployment insurance premiums through MyUI Employer plus, the Department online portal named on that page.

## How long to retain records

Colorado limitation periods give a practical retention floor. Under Regulation 39-21-107, the statute of limitations on assessments for sales, use, and severance tax, among other non income taxes, is three years from the date the return was filed, subject to exceptions including a written waiver and a timely proposed adjustment. For income tax, the period is generally the federal assessment period plus one year. Source: Code of Colorado Regulations at https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=5443

## When an industry needs additional records

Severance tax producers keep production and volume records. Construction contractors in Colorado have a specific sales and use tax treatment for construction materials, and keep contract and materials records by job. Lodging businesses keep records for state collected county lodging and local marketing district taxes, as shown on the Department sales tax page cited in the state tax section.

Colorado employers in healthcare and other licensed settings follow their licensing board record rules in addition to tax and payroll records. No general healthcare bookkeeping rule for all providers was found in the sources checked.

## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Colorado online seller ships orders to customers in several cities. Its order system saves the delivery address for each order, because state collected local taxes and any self collected home rule city tax depend on where the goods were delivered, not only on where the seller sits.

A Denver employer hires a technician, reports the hire within 20 calendar days, and sets up payroll codes for Colorado withholding, unemployment wages, and FAMLI premiums, so the quarterly unemployment wage report, the FAMLI wage report, and the withholding return can all be prepared from one payroll register with separate totals.

## Home rule cities and paid leave payroll accounting

For Colorado sellers, recording an address is not simply a shipping task. It can also determine which local tax system applies. Payroll records likewise benefit from separate codes for unemployment, withholding, and paid leave, because these balances represent different obligations.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Colorado is subject to the same tax or filing rule. DRS administers these taxes and requires sales and use tax returns even where no sales were made and no tax is due for a period, as stated in the DRS sales and use tax return form instructions found in search results. ## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A small Connecticut employer with no sponsored retirement plan registers for MyCTSavings and for CT Paid Leave, adds two separate payroll deduction codes, and reports a new hire to the Department of Labor within 20 days, so the payroll register shows paid leave contributions, retirement deductions, withholding, and unemployment wages as separate totals.

## Detailed daily wage records and payroll deductions

A payroll summary showing only the amount deposited in an employee bank account is not enough to explain gross wages, deductions, and time worked. Connecticut businesses benefit from connecting time and pay records to the payroll journal and maintaining separate liability schedules for applicable leave and benefit programs.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Connecticut is subject to the same tax or filing rule. # Hawaii Bookkeeping

Hawaii general excise tax applies differently from a traditional sales tax, making gross income and activity classifications central. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Hawaii.

## Keeping records that support the books

Hawaii does not have a single general bookkeeping code for all private businesses separate from its tax laws. The operative duties come from the general excise tax and use tax laws, income tax laws, and wage laws. For general excise tax purposes, businesses must keep records of gross proceeds and gross income, documents that support exemptions claimed, and other books, records of account, and invoices, in English and within the state, for at least three years. Source: Hawaii Department of Taxation, An Introduction to the General Excise Tax, https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/legal/brochures/GE_brochure-23.pdf

Businesses keep a general ledger, bank reconciliations, and source documents for all tax types together, because the Department of Taxation can examine records across tax types during an audit.

## How state taxes affect the ledger

Hawaii has no traditional retail sales tax. Instead it imposes a general excise tax, often called GET, on businesses for the privilege of doing business in the state. GET applies broadly to retail sales, services, contracting, rentals, and commissions, and it is a tax on the business, not a sales tax collected from the consumer in the same legal form as in most states, although businesses commonly pass the cost on visibly. The base state rate is 4 percent, with a county surcharge in Honolulu and other counties that adopt it. Source: Hawaii Department of Taxation brochure linked in the recordkeeping section.

Hawaii also has a use tax on tangible personal property imported for use in the state where GET was not paid, a transient accommodations tax on short term lodging, and state individual and corporate income taxes, plus employer withholding on wages. Each tax requires its own return and supporting schedule, so bookkeeping must separate gross income by activity and by county where a county surcharge applies. The Department requires a completed Schedule GE with periodic and annual GET returns when exemptions or deductions are claimed, and allocation by county on the return.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

New hire reporting. Employers report new hires to the State Directory of New Hires administered through the Child Support Enforcement Agency. The federal and state framework described by that agency gives employers up to 20 days from the date of hire to report employee name, address, and Social Security number, and employer name, address, and federal employer identification number. Source: Hawaii Attorney General, Child Support Enforcement Agency, http://ag.hawaii.gov/csea/locate/

Unemployment insurance. Employers liable under Hawaii employment security law file quarterly wage reports and pay contributions to the Department of Labor and Industrial Relations, Unemployment Insurance Division. Bookkeeping must therefore track wages by employee by quarter, and amounts subject to the state taxable wage base for that year.

## Income, purchases, and documentation

Keep receivable records by customer and by island or county, because GET allocation by county affects the return, and keep payable records that show whether a purchase was for resale, for business use subject to use tax, or for a taxable service.

## When an industry needs additional records

Transient accommodations and short term rentals. Operators subject to transient accommodations tax must keep booking, rate, and occupancy records that support the tax, in addition to GET records on the same rental income. Bookkeeping must separate stays that are taxable from longer stays that may be treated differently.

Contracting. Hawaii taxes contracting under GET at the wholesale or retail treatment depending on the structure, and contractors need records that show the contract amount, materials, and any subcontractor amounts claimed as a deduction or credit where allowed. Documentation of payments to licensed subcontractors matters because unsubstantiated amounts can be taxed in full.

A small retailer hires a clerk. It reports the new hire within 20 days, gives the written pay notice at hire, provides a pay statement each payday showing gross pay, each deduction, and net pay, and retains copies of those pay statements for six years.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Unemployment insurance. Employers file quarterly wage and contribution reports through the Department of Employment Security, with filing and payment also available through MyTax Illinois. Payroll records must support wages by employee by quarter.

## When an industry needs additional records

Construction, motor fuel, alcohol, and healthcare providers have additional excise, licensing, or provider tax records. For example, businesses subject to a provider assessment or a fuel tax must keep records that support gallons, receipts, or patient day or service data used on those returns. The scope is limited to businesses in those licensed or taxed activities.

Nonprofits claiming sales tax exemption must keep their exemption documentation and use it only for qualifying purchases. The letter E exemption number and related records become part of purchase documentation.

## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Chicago retailer sells both taxable goods and goods for resale. It keeps point of sale records with transaction level detail, resale certificates filed by customer, vendor invoices in date order, an annual inventory, and a separate deduction log. On audit it can show why each nontaxable sale was not taxed.

An employer hires a contractor who will be paid on Form 1099 in 2025. It reports the contractor as a newly hired independent contractor within 20 days, files quarterly unemployment wage reports for its employees, and keeps pay stub copies for three years.

## Transaction level sales records and inventory

An Illinois retailer may need more than a daily card settlement total. Individual sales, returns, discounts, exemption documentation, and stock changes should be available to explain the totals shown in the ledger. This becomes especially important when a business switches point of sale systems.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Illinois is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

## A practical monthly review for Illinois businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. ## When an industry needs additional records

Lodging, meals, and rentals. This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Maine is subject to the same tax or filing rule. Records must be easily locatable and organized so the Commissioner of Revenue can determine liability, and machine sensible records must contain transaction level detail and be retrievable in a standard format on request. Failure to maintain complete, accurate, and accessible records can lead the Commissioner to determine tax from other information and can lead to penalties, including disallowance of deductions, credits, and exemptions. Source: [830 CMR 62C.25.1, Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/830-CMR-62c251-record-retention)

Businesses keep a general ledger tied to source documents, bank records, and filed returns, because the regulation makes the taxpayer bear the burden of proof for entries, deductions, and credits claimed. That practice statement follows from the burden rule in the same regulation cited above.

## How state taxes affect the ledger

Massachusetts taxes that commonly drive bookkeeping include personal income tax and withholding on wages, corporate excise, sales and use tax, sales tax on meals, room occupancy excise, and marijuana retail taxes. Source: [Submit employment and wage detail report, Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/submit-employment-and-wage-detail-report)

## Income, purchases, and documentation

Source: [830 CMR 62C.25.1, Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/830-CMR-62c251-record-retention)

## How long to retain records

Records required by 830 CMR 62C.25.1 must be kept as long as their contents are material to Massachusetts tax administration. At a minimum, unless the Commissioner consents in writing to earlier destruction, records must be preserved until the statute of limitations for additional assessments for the period expires, which the regulation states is generally three years after the due date of the return or the date the return was actually filed, whichever is later. Exceptions stated in the regulation include fraud or failure to file, where assessment may occur at any time, an agreed extension, records for a future taxable event such as property basis, which must be kept until three years after the return for the year of disposition, and records supporting a refund, credit, or abatement claim, which must be kept until final resolution of the claim. Source: [830 CMR 62C.25.1, Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/830-CMR-62c251-record-retention)

It is imposed on the seller for the privilege of doing business, and it applies broadly to receipts from selling property and performing services in the state, subject to statutory deductions and exemptions. The statewide rate was reduced to 5 percent effective July 1, 2022, and local option rates add to that rate by location. Source: https://www.tax.newmexico.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Tax-laws-take-effect.pdf

New Mexico also has a compensating tax on the use of property and certain services acquired outside the state, a personal income tax, a corporate income tax, and employer withholding of income tax. Bookkeeping must therefore separate gross receipts, deductible receipts, taxable receipts, purchases subject to compensating tax, and payroll withholding.

Because local rates differ, point of sale and invoicing systems record the delivery or performance location for each transaction, not only the business address.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Employers withhold state income tax and report withholding through the Taxation and Revenue Department. Unemployment insurance tax and quarterly wage reporting go to the New Mexico Department of Workforce Solutions, which assigns the employer unemployment account.

## How long to retain records

Department guidance to combined reporting filers states the normal assessment period is 3 years back from the end of the year the tax was due. The period extends to 6 years where a tax was underreported by more than 25 percent, to 7 years for nonfiled reports, and to 10 years where there is evidence of fraud. The same guidance advises filers that it is in their interest to maintain combined reporting records for 10 years. Source: https://realfile.tax.newmexico.gov/CRS-1%20Filers%20Kit%20For%20Reporting%20Gross%20Receipts%20Withholding%20and%20Compensating%20Taxes%20July%20through%20December%202019.pdf

## When an industry needs additional records

Cannabis businesses are licensed and tracked through the Cannabis Control Division, and licensed activity creates inventory and sales tracking duties in addition to gross receipts tax records. This applies only to licensed cannabis establishments.

Construction contractors have gross receipts reporting tied to construction projects and locations, and the location based return structure means project records must show where work was performed.

## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Santa Fe services firm files gross receipts returns by location. It keeps, for each month, a receipts listing by customer location, the invoices that support two large deductions, and the filed return. When the Department asks about a deduction three years later, the firm can trace the return line back to the invoice and contract.

A shop hires a technician on March 3. The office reports the hire to the New Mexico New Hire Directory within 20 days, opens the unemployment wage record for the quarter, and files the hire paperwork with the first payroll record for that employee.

## Gross receipts reporting for services and property

For a New Mexico service company, an invoice should preserve the service and location detail used when evaluating gross receipts tax. Bookkeeping should keep any deduction support with the receipt, rather than subtracting ordinary expenses from the gross receipts ledger.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in New Mexico is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

## A practical monthly review for New Mexico businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.

The Department of Revenue corporate activity tax page describes the corporate activity tax as a tax that is not a transactional tax such as a retail sales tax and not an income tax, which confirms that Oregon businesses do not collect a state sales tax on retail sales. Source: https://www.oregon.gov/dor/programs/businesses/Pages/Corporate-Activity-Tax.aspx

Oregon has a corporate activity tax, called the CAT. It is an annual tax, applicable to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2020, imposed for the privilege of doing business in Oregon and measured on a business commercial activity, meaning the total amount a business realises from transactions and activity in the normal course of business in Oregon. The CAT is applied to Oregon taxable commercial activity above 1 million dollars. The tax is computed as 250 dollars plus 0.57 percent of Oregon commercial activity above the 1 million dollar threshold, under Oregon Revised Statutes 317A.125. Only taxpayers with more than 1 million dollars of taxable Oregon commercial activity have a payment obligation, and the return is due on the 15th day of the fourth month after the end of the tax year. Source: https://www.oregon.gov/dor/programs/businesses/Pages/Corporate-Activity-Tax.aspx

Oregon also has a personal income tax, a corporate income or excise tax, and employer withholding, plus a statewide transit tax withholding and local payroll based taxes in some areas, including the Portland area transit and arts taxes where they apply. Bookkeeping must keep Oregon receipts, income, and payroll amounts separate from amounts sourced to other states.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Employing units must keep the payroll records described in the recordkeeping section of this state section for a period of 3 calendar years and furnish the information to the Employment Department on demand. The employing unit must also maintain, for each calendar quarter, the date each pay period ends, total wages by pay periods ending in the quarter, and the total number of employees in each pay period. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-471-031-0005

Oregon employers report payroll taxes on a combined quarterly report that covers withholding, unemployment insurance, and other payroll programs, and they report through the Employment Department system called Frances Online for unemployment and paid leave matters, while tax payments and many tax returns are handled through Department of Revenue Revenue Online.

## How long to retain records

Payroll records under Oregon Administrative Rule 471 031 0005 must be kept for a period of 3 calendar years. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-471-031-0005

Motor carrier records for the Oregon weight mile tax must be stored at the carrier principal office or place of business, made available for inspection on request, and retained for a period of 3 years unless the Department authorises otherwise. Distance records must show origin and destination, Oregon entry and exit points, Oregon miles, routes, dates, and daily beginning and ending odometer readings for each vehicle. Source: https://www.oregon.gov/odot/mct/pages/recordkeeping.aspx

## When an industry needs additional records

Motor carriers subject to the Oregon weight mile tax have the detailed distance and vehicle record duties described in the retention section of this state section. This applies to carriers operating covered vehicles on Oregon public highways, not to businesses generally. Source: https://www.oregon.gov/odot/mct/pages/recordkeeping.aspx

Cannabis businesses licensed by the Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission must track inventory and sales in the state cannabis tracking system and keep licensed activity records, in addition to income tax and CAT records. This applies only to licensed cannabis businesses. Oregon also has a cannabis tax administered by the Department of Revenue for licensed retailers.

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Portland wholesaler has no sales tax to collect on Oregon sales, but its Oregon receipts pass 1 million dollars during the year. The bookkeeper keeps a monthly Oregon receipts total from the sales ledger, files the CAT return by the due date in the fourth month after year end, and keeps the receipts worksheet with the return.

A Salem employer prepares its combined quarterly payroll report. The payroll file for the quarter shows each employee name, Social Security number, wages by pay period, hours worked, withholding, unemployment wages, and paid leave contributions, and the file is kept for at least 3 calendar years.

## Commercial Activity Tax and combined payroll reporting

An Oregon business can have no statewide retail sales tax yet still need detailed state sourced revenue information for Corporate Activity Tax. Its payroll file may also need to explain withholding, unemployment, and paid leave figures from the same underlying pay periods.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Oregon is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

For unemployment insurance, an employer record must show the payroll period, place of employment in the state, and scheduled hours, and for each employee the Social Security number and name, dates hired, rehired, and terminated, weekly wage information, hours paid, gross wages by pay period and by quarter, value of noncash remuneration, and special payments such as bonuses, gifts, and prizes. The Tennessee employer handbook states that these records must be maintained for the most recent seven year period. Source, Department of Labor and Workforce Development handbook hosted on tn.gov: https://preprod.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/workforce/documents/employers/HandbookforEmployers6-13-19.pdf

Covered employers file quarterly wage and premium reports for unemployment insurance and are subject to audit under Tennessee Code Annotated the retention section0.7.701 and Rule 0800.10.03.10, as cited in that handbook.

## Income, purchases, and documentation

Purchase invoices are retained and reviewed for use tax, because Tennessee use tax can apply to taxable purchases where sales tax was not collected, and sales records are retained with exemption certificates for exempt sales.

## How long to retain records

For liquor by the drink licensees, Department guidance requires records to be retained for four years, with a limited exception allowing guest tickets to be disposed of after 60 days if both cash register tapes and guest tickets showing the same sales are maintained, and the tapes are kept for the full four years. Source, Department of Revenue guidance hosted on tn.gov: https://preprod.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/revenue/documents/notices/lbd/recreports.pdf

## When an industry needs additional records

Alcohol. Liquor by the drink licensees must keep purchase invoices, delivery tickets, bills of lading, purchase orders, a perpetual inventory record for alcohol and wine used in cooking, and a current price schedule, per the Department guidance in the retention section.

Manufacturing. Manufacturers can claim an industrial machinery exemption and related sales tax exemptions, so purchase records need to show how machinery and equipment are used in manufacturing to support the exemption claimed. The exemption topics are listed in Department support materials referenced in the recordkeeping section.

## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Nashville retailer with two locations keeps sales, exemption certificates, and business tax workpapers separated by location, because a single combined total would not support its location based filings in TNTAP.

A restaurant with a liquor by the drink license keeps alcohol purchase invoices and its price schedule for four years, and keeps register tapes for the full period even if guest tickets are discarded earlier under the exception in the retention section.

## Business tax and franchise tax despite no wage income tax

A Tennessee business may have no state wage income tax withholding yet still need detailed payroll records for unemployment. Its business tax reporting also creates a reason to distinguish revenue by location and activity from the profit shown in financial statements.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Tennessee is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

## A practical monthly review for Tennessee businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

## What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.

# Washington Bookkeeping

Washington gross receipts based business and occupation tax changes how businesses document taxable revenue and classifications. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Washington.

## Keeping records that support the books

All Washington businesses must keep complete and accurate records from which the Department of Revenue may determine any tax for which the business may be liable. Records must demonstrate the amount of gross receipts and sales from all sources, including barter or exchange transactions, and the amount of all deductions, exemptions, or credits claimed, with supporting documentation. Records include general ledgers, sales journals, bills, invoices, cash register tapes, and other original entries, plus federal and state tax returns and workpapers used to prepare them. Source: Washington Department of Revenue, Record keeping requirements, https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/bail-bond-agencies/record-keeping-requirements and the same guidance at https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/convenience-stores/record-keeping-requirements

## How state taxes affect the ledger

Washington does not have a corporate or personal income tax. The Department of Revenue states this directly and lists the taxes Washington businesses generally face instead, namely business and occupation tax, retail sales and use tax, and personal property tax. Source: https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/mortgage-brokers/business-tax-structure-washington-state

The state business and occupation tax, called the B and O tax, is a gross receipts tax measured on the value of products, gross proceeds of sale, or gross income of the business. The Department combined excise tax instructions state that Washington does not have an income tax, that B and O tax is calculated on gross income, and that there are no deductions from B and O tax for labor, materials, taxes, or other costs of doing business. Source: https://dor.wa.gov/file-pay-taxes/file-or-amend-my-return/instructions-completing-combined-excise-tax-return/combined-excise-tax-return-state-business-and-occupation-tax

B and O tax rates differ by classification. The Department B and O page lists retailing at 0.471 percent of gross receipts and wholesaling at 0.484 percent, with service and other activities and other classifications taxed at their own rates. Retail sales tax must also be collected on sales subject to retailing B and O tax unless a deduction or exemption applies. Source for the classification examples: https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/private-mailing-business-tax-guide/business-and-occupation-bo-tax

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Washington has no state wage income tax withholding, because it has no personal income tax, as in the state tax section. Washington employers do have state payroll based programs, including unemployment insurance through the Employment Security Department, workers compensation through the Department of Labor and Industries, and Paid Family and Medical Leave premiums administered by the Employment Security Department, each with wage reports that use payroll records.

Employers must report all new and rehired employees within 20 days of hiring. Washington law cited by the Employment Security Department is Revised Code of Washington 26.23.040. A rehired employee includes a person who has not worked for that employer in the past 60 days, as described by the Department. Report information includes employee name, address, Social Security number, date of birth, and date of hire, and employer name, address, and federal employer identification number. Reports go to the Department of Social and Health Services, Division of Child Support. Sources: https://esd.wa.gov/employer-requirements/reporting-new-and-rehired-employees and https://www.dshs.wa.gov/family-food-and-housing-services/child-support/employer-resources/new-hire-reporting

## Income, purchases, and documentation

Washington documentation is governed by the rule in the recordkeeping section. Records must show gross receipts and sales from all sources, including barter, and must support every deduction, exemption, and credit claimed. The Department list includes purchase and cash disbursement journals, purchase invoices for assets and expense items, sales invoices, financial statements, and reseller permits for wholesale sales. Source: https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/convenience-stores/record-keeping-requirements

## How long to retain records

Business records under Washington Administrative Code 458.20.254 must be preserved for five years. The Department phrases the duty as keeping complete and adequate records for at least five years, and repeats the five year rule for reseller permits and for records after a business closes. Sources: https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/bail-bond-agencies/record-keeping-requirements and https://dor.wa.gov/file-pay-taxes/filing-frequencies-due-dates/annual-business-filers

## When an industry needs additional records

Construction. Contractors are subject to B and O tax classifications for construction activities, retail sales tax or use tax on materials in specified fact patterns, and prevailing wage and workers compensation reporting where those programs apply. Job records need to separate labor, materials, subcontractor costs, and location, because city B and O tax and retail sales tax sourcing can depend on job location.

Cannabis. Licensed cannabis businesses are subject to B and O tax, retail sales tax, and Washington State Liquor and Cannabis Board record and traceability duties, in addition to Department of Revenue tax records. Scope is limited to licensed cannabis businesses.

## Bookkeeping in practice

The following situations illustrate how a business might organize its records. They are examples, not a determination of tax treatment for any particular business.

A Seattle service business with no employees keeps a gross income ledger by B and O classification, files its combined excise tax return in My DOR, and keeps ledgers, invoices, and returns for five years, even though it has no Washington income tax return to file.

A retailer sells goods at wholesale to a reseller and at retail to consumers. It keeps the buyer reseller permit for five years for the wholesale sales, collects retail sales tax on the retail sales, and reports the two categories under different B and O classifications, as in Parts 2 and 4.

## Business and occupation tax on gross income

Washington businesses illustrate the difference between profit and taxable gross income. Supplier costs may be expenses on the profit and loss statement without reducing the gross base for B and O tax. The books should make that difference understandable rather than forcing both calculations into a single number.

This is an accounting workflow distinction, not a claim that every business in Washington is subject to the same tax or filing rule. The applicable treatment depends on the actual transaction and the business activity. A bookkeeper's responsibility is to preserve the transaction evidence and classify the amounts consistently. Determining a disputed tax position or filing treatment is a separate professional judgment.

## A practical monthly review for Washington businesses

Begin with the original source rather than the bank feed. A customer invoice, sales report, payroll register, supplier bill, or contract explains what occurred. Match it to the corresponding deposit or payment, investigate differences, and record adjustments with a clear explanation. When a state tax or payroll report is required, maintain a separate reconciliation from the ledger to the amounts included in that report. That allows an owner to compare operating results without confusing business income with taxes collected, employee withholdings, or other liabilities.

For an owner preparing to hand records to a tax professional, the useful package includes a period summary, filed return copies where applicable, the transactions supporting material adjustments, and a short list of unresolved questions. A reconciled bank account is only part of that package. The quality of the records depends on whether a reviewer can trace important figures back to their sources.

## What to keep available

Maintain organized copies of the invoices, receipts, bank and card statements, payroll reports, account reconciliations, and any exemption or special program documents relevant to the business. A retention schedule should identify which law or program controls each category instead of assuming one deadline covers every record. Keep documents connected to a pending audit, claim, or dispute until the matter is resolved, and verify any destruction decision against the current applicable rule.

# West Virginia Bookkeeping

West Virginia sales tax rules require records of taxable charges and tax collected, with separate treatment for certain industries. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in West Virginia.

## Keeping records that support the books

Each taxpayer under the consumers sales and service tax must keep complete and accurate records of taxable sales and charges, together with a record of tax collected, and must keep all invoices, bills of lading, and other pertinent documents in the form the Tax Commissioner may require by regulation. Source, West Virginia Code the recordkeeping section1.15.23, official code site: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/11-15-23/

For taxes under the Tax Procedure and Administration Act, taxpayers must keep records, receipts, invoices, and other pertinent papers in the form the Tax Commissioner requires, as in West Virginia Code the recordkeeping section1.12B.14 for the mineral severance related article summarized in search results, which states a general record duty for taxpayers liable under that article. Source: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/11-12B-14/

## How state taxes affect the ledger

West Virginia imposes a personal income tax, a corporation net income tax, and a consumers sales and service tax and a corresponding use tax. The consumers sales tax rate in the code materials is stated as six percent in the direct pay permit and related sections summarized in search results, with local and municipal taxes possible in some jurisdictions. No general gross receipts business and occupation tax for ordinary businesses is stated here, because the historic municipal business and occupation taxes are local, not a current statewide tax for all businesses, and should not be confused with the state taxes above.

West Virginia also imposes severance taxes on coal, oil, gas, timber, and other natural resources, and a business registration tax tied to the business registration certificate. Those taxes apply only to businesses in the relevant activities or to businesses required to register.

Sellers need a record of taxable sales and of tax collected as a separate amount, per the recordkeeping section, and purchasers need use tax records for taxable purchases and services where sales tax was not collected.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Employers must report the hiring, rehiring, or return to work of an employee or independent contractor to the Bureau for Child Support Enforcement within 14 days of the date of hiring, rehiring, or return to work. That is shorter than the 20 day rule in most states in this batch. If reports are transmitted magnetically or electronically in two monthly submissions, they must be not less than twelve days nor more than sixteen days apart. The report includes the person name, address, and Social Security number, start date, employer name and address, any different payroll office address, and the employer federal tax identification number. Source, West Virginia Code section 48.18.125, official code site: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/48-18-125/

The same code section provides a civil penalty of no more than 25 dollars per failure to report, with a higher penalty described in the code where the failure results from a conspiracy between employer and employee or contractor not to report or to report falsely, as summarized from the code text in search results.

## Income, purchases, and documentation

Purchase invoices are kept with a use tax review note for services and tangible property brought into West Virginia, and contractor and severance records in Part 6 are kept by job or by well, mine, or property, as applicable.

## How long to retain records

Consumers sales and service tax records and documents must be preserved for not less than three years, unless the Tax Commissioner consents in writing to destruction within that period or orders that they be kept longer. Source: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/11-15-23/

For the article covered by the recordkeeping section1.12B.14, records must be kept for not less than three years after the annual return is filed, unless the Tax Commissioner authorizes earlier destruction in writing, and an extension of time for assessment automatically extends the record period for years covered by the extension agreement. Source: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/11-12B-14/

## When an industry needs additional records

Energy and mining. Coal, oil, gas, and other severance taxpayers must keep production, extraction, sales, and price records that support severance tax returns, in addition to income and sales tax records. Scope is limited to producers and processors in severance taxable activities. The record duty example in the recordkeeping section1.12B.14 in the retention section is from a severance related article.

Motor carriers. International Fuel Tax Agreement licensees keep mileage and fuel records by jurisdiction and file quarterly reports through the Tax Division program. Source: West Virginia Tax Division, International Fuel Tax Agreement, https://tax.wv.gov/business/motorfuel/internationalfueltaxagreement/pages/internationalfueltaxagreement.aspx

A Charleston retailer keeps a daily record of taxable sales and tax collected, plus invoices and bills of lading, for at least three years, and does not destroy them at three years for a year the Tax Commissioner has ordered kept longer or has under audit.

An employer hires a contractor and an employee in the same week and reports both to the Bureau for Child Support Enforcement within 14 days, because West Virginia new hire law in the payroll section covers independent contractors as well as employees, unlike a rule limited only to employees.

A retail tax record explains money collected from customers, while a severance tax record may rely on production quantities and values. West Virginia businesses in both worlds need distinct support for those figures.

# Wisconsin Bookkeeping

Wisconsin business records may support sales and use tax, state income or franchise tax, and specific manufacturing exemptions. Accurate bookkeeping connects transactions to the invoices, receipts, bank activity, payroll records, and tax filings that rely on them. The details below explain the records that matter in Wisconsin.

## Keeping records that support the books

Wisconsin businesses keep federal and Wisconsin returns together with sales journals, exemption certificates, purchase invoices, and payroll registers, because the Department can examine income, sales, and withholding records for the same business in different audits.

## How state taxes affect the ledger

Wisconsin imposes an individual income tax, a corporation franchise and income tax, a sales tax and a use tax, and employer withholding tax on Wisconsin wages. Local taxes that affect records include county sales tax where adopted and, for specified areas, local exposition and other special district taxes.

Franchise tax in Wisconsin is an income tax for most corporations, measured by income, which is different from the Texas franchise tax on margin in this batch, even though both use the name franchise tax. Records must therefore support income, deductions, apportionment, and credits, not only gross receipts.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

All Wisconsin employers and labor organizations with a federal employer identification number must report each newly hired employee within 20 days after the employee starts work, and must report employees rehired, recalled, or returning to work after a separation or unpaid interval of more than 60 days. Legal references listed for the program are Wisconsin Statutes the recordkeeping section03.05, administrative chapter DWD 142, and 42 United States Code section 653a. Sources: Wisconsin Department of Children and Families new hire site, https://www.wisctfemployer.com/new_hire.html and Department of Workforce Development publication, https://dwd.wi.gov/dwd/publications/ui/ucb-10677-p.pdf

Minimum data includes employee name, address, and Social Security number, and employer name, address, and federal employer identification number, as described in the program glossary on the same official contractor hosted site for the Department of Children and Families: https://www.wisctfemployer.com/glossary.html

## Income, purchases, and documentation

A Wisconsin business keeps a use tax review in accounts payable for taxable equipment and supplies bought from a seller that did not charge Wisconsin tax.

## When an industry needs additional records

Manufacturing. Wisconsin provides a manufacturing and agriculture credit against income and franchise tax and sales tax exemptions for certain manufacturing machinery, equipment, and consumables, so production, asset, and usage records are needed to support the credit or exemption claimed. Scope is limited to eligible manufacturers and farmers claiming those provisions.

Alcohol, tobacco, and motor fuel distributors have excise tax permits, invoices, and inventory records tied to gallons, packs, or product movement, in addition to ordinary sales tax records.

A Wisconsin manufacturer keeps machine purchase invoices, production records, and utility and consumable records with its franchise tax file, because those records may be needed to support both a sales tax exemption on equipment and an income tax credit, not only to support a deduction.

Wisconsin manufacturing businesses often need to show what equipment or supplies were used for, not just what they cost. Linking purchase invoices with equipment and production records can help a preparer evaluate eligibility for specialized tax treatment.

# Wyoming Bookkeeping

Wyoming has no personal or corporate income tax, but sales tax, asset related annual reporting, and mineral taxes can shape recordkeeping. ## How state taxes affect the ledger

Because there is no Wyoming income tax, the main state bookkeeping drivers are sales and use tax by county and location, lodging tax where applicable, severance and mineral taxes for producers, workers compensation and unemployment payroll reports, and the asset based annual license tax.

## Payroll records and employer reporting

Wyoming has no state wage income tax withholding, because it has no personal income tax, as in the state tax section.

A Wyoming retailer keeps a county level sales record, because county option taxes change the combined rate and the distribution of tax, even though the return is filed with the state.

A small energy services business keeps equipment purchase invoices with use tax notes, job location records, and payroll reports for unemployment and workers compensation, and reports a new hire within 20 days, because in Wyoming payroll reporting exists even though wage income tax withholding does not.
