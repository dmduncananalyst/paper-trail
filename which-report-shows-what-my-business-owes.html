Find Business Obligations on Your Balance Sheet

The balance sheet shows the business’s recorded liabilities at a specific date. These can include vendor bills, credit card debt, loans and other obligations. Detailed reports, such as an accounts payable aging report and a loan schedule, explain who is owed and when payment may be due.

Use the balance sheet for the overall position

Liability accounts summarize obligations by type. They help you see whether debt or unpaid costs are increasing, but a total alone may not show the payment schedule or the documents behind it. Cash basis reports may present some balances differently, so ask which reporting basis is being used.

Use supporting detail for payment planning

An accounts payable aging report lists recorded vendor bills and groups them by age. Credit card statements show payment requirements for cards, while loan statements and agreements explain debt payments. Payroll, tax and customer deposit liabilities can require separate schedules. These records serve different purposes and should not be replaced by a single list of outgoing bank transactions.

Check that the obligations are complete and accurate

Unentered bills will not appear in a payable report. Duplicate bills can make the business appear to owe too much, while incorrectly applied payments can leave settled bills open. Review vendor statements and payment records to resolve these issues. For near term cash planning, combine verified obligations with expected collections and available cash. A report becomes useful when its balances can be connected to real bills, statements and agreed payment terms.
