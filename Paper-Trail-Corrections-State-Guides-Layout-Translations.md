## 4. 50 substantial, independently researched state financial guides

The user wants coverage for **every state** because each state has real business, tax, workforce, and recordkeeping requirements. The instructions are **not** to invent or force differences on identical accounting principles. Each state page must be genuinely state-specific, substantive, informative, and valuable—not a city-swapped template or doorway page.

### Scope and depth

Create a dedicated **50-State Bookkeeping & Financial Recordkeeping Guides** section connected to the Nationwide Bookkeeping page (but not replacing national service pages or local Los Angeles pages). Plan for **all 50 state-specific guides**, even if publishing is phased for quality and source verification.

Research and explain **where applicable to the state and relevant businesses**:

1. Tax registration and state tax agency requirements that affect business recordkeeping.
2. Sales and use tax, including taxable sales, exemptions, resale records, nexus concepts, documentation, remittance and records (where applicable).
3. State payroll withholding, state unemployment taxes, payroll reporting, related employment or paid-leave liabilities as applicable, and the source documentation a bookkeeper needs.
4. Accounts payable and vendor invoices, purchase-related use tax considerations, contractor/vendor documentation and classification as relevant.
5. Income, expense and receipt tracking, business taxes and state-specific reporting distinctions.
6. Monthly financial statement preparation: reconciliation of state tax and payroll liabilities, cash flow, accounts payable, receivables, consistent categorization (distinguish generally applicable practices from state-specific requirements).
7. Tax-ready and year-end bookkeeping: closing books, organizing state-related tax records for a qualified tax professional, payroll reconciliations, deadlines that genuinely apply.
8. Document retention and recordkeeping obligations, clearly separated by tax and agency where they vary.
9. Industry or entity-specific variations when material (retail, professional services, contractors, restaurants, e-commerce, etc.), with scope clearly labeled so requirements aren't falsely described as universal.
10. Hypothetical, clearly labeled illustrative transactions/examples that show what information to collect and how a state-specific obligation affects the books.
11. Links to authoritative resources and verifiable dates; explain when to contact a CPA, enrolled agent, payroll expert or attorney.

A state page can have different subsections and more/less emphasis than another based on the actual rules. Do not invent state accounting standards where GAAP and ordinary bookkeeping mechanics are the same. Be precise that bookkeeping is not tax filing or licensed advice unless those services are actually provided.

### Editorial and sourcing standard

- Research **each state independently** from the state's revenue/tax agency, department of labor/workforce/unemployment, secretary of state where applicable, other relevant official state sources, IRS/federal sources where needed.
- Verify claims against **primary official sources**, including effective dates, applicability, relevant exemptions and jurisdictional distinctions. No made-up rates, dates, thresholds or legal conclusions. Identify any unverified items for editorial review.
- Substantial and readable: explain why the requirement matters to a business owner, not merely repeat a government link or restate the same introduction in all 50 pages. Avoid a mandatory word count that creates filler, but make each guide meaty enough to stand alone.
- Unique state-centered title/H1, meta description, intro, main examples, relevant government references, internal links, accurate last-reviewed date, canonical, breadcrumbs and appropriate factual structured data. Content must be helpful in search, including AI search, without keyword stuffing or invented Q&A schema.
- No assertions of fictitious offices, client stories, CPA licensing, guaranteed tax savings or guarantees of compliance. Explain service boundaries and provide a compact general-information notice when necessary.
- Create a clean 50-state index; only add approved and actually published guide URLs to the sitemap. Maintain local Los Angeles and national service pages as separate complementary funnels.
- Produce a state-by-state research matrix for all 50 states, with official source URLs, planned distinct angles, verification status, and research gaps. Begin with five **fully developed** pilots (CA, TX, FL, NY, WA) as a quality gate; continue preparing the 50-state plan, but **do not mass-generate or mass-publish the remainder before user approval of quality and design**. The ambition is all 50, not permanently stopping at five.
- Evaluate duplicate-content/doorway risk, factual accuracy, links and mobile layouts before each approved batch. Avoid creating 50 URLs just to have 50 URLs.
