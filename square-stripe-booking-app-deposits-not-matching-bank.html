Why Square, Stripe, and Booking App Deposits Do Not Match Your Bank

Published 11/23/2026

The amount customers paid is not always the amount deposited

A business owner may see one sales total in a payment platform and a smaller amount in the bank. That difference is not necessarily missing money. Processing fees, refunds, chargebacks, reserves, and payout timing can affect what arrives. The challenge is identifying each component so the business records reflect what actually happened.

Gross sales and net payouts serve different purposes

Consider a simple illustration. Customers pay a total of $1,000 through a platform. The platform deducts $35 in fees and processes a $65 refund before sending a $900 payout. The bank shows $900, but that deposit alone does not explain the original customer payments, the refund, or the fees. The correct accounting treatment depends on the underlying transactions and the platform reports.

Payout dates can cross month boundaries

A payment accepted at the end of a month may be deposited in the following month. A later refund may reduce a different payout. Businesses that compare only monthly bank deposits with monthly dashboard sales can see differences even when the platform is operating normally. Reconciliation needs transaction and payout details, not only the headline totals.

What records make reconciliation possible

Useful records may include gross transaction reports, payout statements, fee reports, refunds, chargebacks, sales tax details, and bank statements. Different platforms organize these records differently. A bookkeeper should understand which system is the source of each figure and avoid recording the same sale twice.

Why this matters beyond tax season

Accurate reconciliation helps a business understand revenue, processing costs, and the money actually received. It also makes financial statements more useful for decisions about pricing and operations. Form 1099-K reporting may add another comparison at year end, but it does not replace complete business records.

When to get bookkeeping support

If your platform reports, bank deposits, and books do not agree, start by gathering the relevant statements and transaction reports. Do not force numbers to match with unsupported adjustments. The right next step is to identify the source of each difference and correct the records with appropriate documentation.
