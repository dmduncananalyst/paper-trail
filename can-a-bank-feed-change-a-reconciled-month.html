Can a Bank Feed Change a Reconciled Month?

Published 11/05/2026

A reconciled month can still become unreliable

A business owner opens the accounting dashboard and sees a warning that the beginning balance no longer matches the last reconciliation. The previous month had been closed, the bank statement had been checked, and the figures appeared settled. Now the numbers are different. The owner has not knowingly reopened anything, so the bank connection becomes the first suspect.

A bank feed is a convenient source of transaction activity, but it is not the same as a completed reconciliation. A reconciliation compares the accounting records with an independent bank statement for a defined period. The feed supplies items for review and matching. The reconciliation confirms that the records and statement agree at that point in time.

The important distinction is that a newly downloaded item does not automatically rewrite every reconciled month. The problem usually arises when a feed item is categorized, matched, undone, duplicated, or otherwise handled in a way that changes an existing bookkeeping entry. Changes made directly in the register can also affect a past reconciliation.

What can change after a month is reconciled

Imagine a payment that cleared the bank in March and was recorded in the books. In April, a feed item for that same payment appears. If someone adds it as an entirely new expense rather than matching it to the existing entry, the books can show the payment twice. Depending on the dates and handling, a report for the earlier period may no longer tell the same story.

Another possibility is that a previously reconciled transaction is edited or deleted. Changing the amount, bank account, date, or reconciliation status can create a beginning balance discrepancy for the next reconciliation. Intuit identifies edited, deleted, voided, moved, and unreconciled transactions among the causes of beginning balance problems in QuickBooks Online.

A feed connection can also be interrupted and reconnected, producing overlapping downloaded items. That overlap is not proof that the bank charged the customer twice. It is a reason to compare the feed against the statement and existing ledger before accepting anything.

How to investigate the difference without making it worse

Start with the bank statement for the affected month and the reconciliation report that was saved when the month closed. Identify the exact difference between the previously reconciled balance and the current beginning balance. Then review changes to transactions dated within the reconciled period and inspect pending bank feed items.

The objective is to find the specific entry that explains the difference. Look for a duplicate added from the feed, an existing transaction moved to another account, an amount changed after reconciliation, or a transaction deleted or marked unreconciled. An audit log or transaction history, if available in the accounting platform, can help establish what changed and when.

Do not enter a balancing journal entry simply to force the reconciliation screen to zero. That may conceal the underlying duplicate or missing record and can distort expense, income, or liability balances. The correction needs to follow the actual transaction and supporting documentation.

Why the month end review matters

A bank statement is a reliable reference for cash moving through that account, but it cannot by itself explain whether an amount represents a loan payment, a business purchase, an owner withdrawal, or a transfer. Bookkeeping still requires identifying the nature of transactions and preserving supporting records.

The Internal Revenue Service explains that adequate business records support income, expenses, and tax return entries. That is why fixing a past reconciliation should include more than making the software balance. The corrected records should still agree with real bank activity and documented business events.

A practical monthly close process therefore includes saving reconciliation reports, documenting open questions, and controlling changes to previously closed periods. A later feed download can then be reviewed against that record, rather than treated as a reason to start the month over.

When to bring in bookkeeping help

If several months are affected or no one knows when the discrepancy began, a structured bookkeeping review can isolate the changed transactions and determine which periods need correction.
