Unpaid Vendor Bills at Year End

Yes, unpaid supplier bills identify obligations that may still need payment after year end and help explain future cash needs. Their effect on that year’s expense report depends on the accounting method, the nature of the cost and the supporting facts.

Check more than the invoice date

Under accrual accounting, an expense can relate to a period before payment, although recognition rules and exceptions apply. Under the cash method, expenses are generally recognized when paid, with exceptions for items such as certain assets and prepayments. Keep delivery or service dates, invoice details and payment dates available for review.

Confirm that the balance is real

Compare open bills with supplier statements, bank payments and credit notes. A bill can look unpaid because the payment was entered as a separate expense rather than linked to the bill. Recording another payment would repeat the error and could result in paying the supplier twice.

Create a clear year boundary record

Suppose a December materials bill remains unpaid on December 31 and is paid in January. Preserve the bill and its eventual payment as connected records. Identify disputed bills, expected credits and payments still processing separately. The year end list should tell your tax professional and the business owner which obligations remain open and why.
