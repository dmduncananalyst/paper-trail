Business Bookkeeping

Business Records

A sale might appear in a checkout system, a processor report and the bank feed. A customer invoice may also exist in the accounting software. Those records describe related events, not necessarily separate income. Importing each one as a new sale can make revenue appear larger than it is.

Map the flow before changing entries. Identify where the original sale is recorded, how the payment is applied and how the processor payout reaches the bank. A consistent matching process can preserve the original transaction while explaining fees, refunds and payout timing. Each payment channel should have a clear role in the books.

Income and Expenses

If some customers pay later, a sales total is not enough. The invoice list should identify what has been issued, what has been paid, which amounts are overdue and whether a credit or dispute affects the balance. A part payment needs to reduce the correct invoice, not sit as an unexplained deposit.

For a store that also supplies business customers, immediate retail payments and invoiced orders may need different workflows. The monthly review can compare the customer balance schedule to the accounting records. Customer reminders and collection responsibilities should be agreed separately so an open balance does not create confusion over who will follow up.

Local Business Example

Imagine a shop collecting card payments at the counter while also invoicing a commercial customer for a larger order. The commercial customer pays by bank transfer. If that transfer is entered as a fresh sale after the invoice already recorded the sale, the bookkeeping can count the same order twice.

Matching the transfer to the invoice clears the customer balance without creating another sale. The card processor is reconciled separately using its settlement records. The result is a trail that explains both payment routes. This is an illustrative example, not a statement about a particular Glendale company or Paper Trail client.

Monthly Records

Repeated small differences can signal a process issue. Unapplied payments, old credit balances, refunds posted in the wrong place or duplicate imports deserve attention before another month repeats the same pattern. A documented exception list makes the review specific and manageable.

We can begin with the types of payments you accept, the software involved and the part of the process that feels unreliable. Detailed transaction records come later through the agreed handoff.
